_Captain Drawdown's weekly Sunday selection — 8 candidate stories considered, 6-9 picked. Each link carries our 1-2 sentence take so you don't have to click everything to know what's there._

The week's signal is concentrated on enhanced rock weathering, with two peer-reviewed papers pulling in opposite directions on whether ERW can actually deliver at scale, alongside a commercial deal extending ERW credit access. Around that core, offshore CCS gains a new contractor entrant and mineralization-in-concrete gets a fresh LCA scrutiny.

## ERW: bench progress vs. field limits
- **Nature Biotechnology**: [Engineered bacterial siderophore production accelerates rock weathering for carbon removal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03288-w). Pilot-scale continuous bioreactors using unprocessed seawater and renewable acetate weathered 4 kg of olivine, with engineered cells delivering directly measured removal of 0.50 g CO2 per day. A wet, engineered pathway rather than the usual basalt-on-fields approach.
- **Springer Nature Research Communities**: [Limitations on carbon dioxide uptake by enhanced weathering](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/limitations-on-carbon-dioxide-uptake-by-enhanced-weathering). The authors interrogate the core ERW premise: that ground basaltic rock (high Ca and Mg) spread over row-crop agriculture actually raises net weathering CO2 consumption. Useful counterweight to the bullish measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) narrative dominating ERW commercial announcements.
- **Carbon Herald**: [Commons And InPlanet Team Up To Expand Access To High-Integrity ERW Credits](https://carbonherald.com/commons-and-inplanet-team-up-to-expand-access-to-high-integrity-erw-credits/). A distribution deal pushing InPlanet's tropical-basalt ERW credits through the Commons platform, notable mainly because it lands the same week two papers openly question ERW's uptake ceiling.

## CCS and fuels infrastructure
- **Carbon Herald**: [Jan De Nul Secures First Carbon Capture Contract On UK North Sea Pipeline](https://carbonherald.com/jan-de-nul-secures-first-carbon-capture-contract-on-uk-north-sea-pipeline/). The Belgian dredging and offshore contractor makes its first CCS move on a UK North Sea pipeline, signaling that marine-infrastructure specialists are now treating CO2 transport as an adjacent line of business to offshore wind and cables.
- **Oxford Net Zero**: [A High-Integrity Route for CORSIA: Integrating Geologically Balanced Fuels](https://netzeroclimate.org/publications/a-high-integrity-route-for-corsia-integrating-geologically-balanced-fuels/). Oxford Net Zero proposes a pathway for geologically balanced fuels to qualify under CORSIA, the aviation sector's offsetting scheme. Worth reading if you care whether durable removals get a defined home in aviation compliance demand.

## Mineralization accounting
- **Zenodo**: [Carbonation-cured concrete: LCA meta-analysis, net-benefit framework, and probabilistic analysis](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22061901). A systematic review by J.K. Sah proposing a net-benefit condition framework and a minimum reporting set for CO2 mineralization in cement-based systems, aimed at answering when carbonation-cured concrete is actually cleaner on a lifecycle basis.

The dominant signal this week is ERW under simultaneous scientific pressure from both directions: one lab route claiming measured removals in engineered reactors, another paper questioning whether the field-application premise holds. Conspicuously absent: any material DAC delivery or purchase news, and no visible movement from the large corporate buyer cohort.
