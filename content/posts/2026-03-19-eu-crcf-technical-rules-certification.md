<p>Carbon removal policy in Europe just got more real. The EU has published <a href=https://european-accreditation.org/carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming-%5Bcrcf%5D(/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/)-regulation-eu-2024-3012/>Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2358</a>, the technical rulebook for how carbon removal and carbon farming certification actually works under the Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming (<a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a>) Regulation.</p><p>Translation: the EU didn&rsquo;t just say &ldquo;we want certified carbon removal.&rdquo; It specified exactly how you certify it, who can certify it, and what auditors must check.</p><h2 id=what-the-crcf-covers>What the <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a> Covers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-the-crcf-covers>#</a></h2><p>The <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a> Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 established the first EU-wide voluntary certification framework for three categories:</p><ul><li><strong>Permanent carbon removals</strong> — DAC, BECCS, and other engineered approaches with durable storage</li><li><strong>Carbon farming</strong> — agricultural and land-use practices that sequester carbon in soils and biomass</li><li><strong>Carbon storage in products</strong> — biochar in construction materials, CO₂ in concrete, and similar approaches</li></ul><p>The regulation itself set the policy goals. What was missing — until now — were the detailed rules for how the system actually operates.</p><h2 id=what-the-implementing-regulation-specifies>What the Implementing Regulation Specifies<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-the-implementing-regulation-specifies>#</a></h2><p>Implementing Regulation 2025/2358 addresses three core operational areas:</p><h3 id=1-certification-bodies>1. Certification Bodies<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#1-certification-bodies>#</a></h3><p>Organizations that certify carbon removal projects must be accredited according to <strong>EN ISO/IEC 17065</strong> by a national accreditation body. When conducting verification activities, they must also meet the requirements of <strong>EN ISO/IEC 17029</strong> and <strong>EN ISO 14065</strong>. These are established international standards for conformity assessment — the same types of standards used to certify everything from organic food to industrial safety.</p><h3 id=2-certification-schemes>2. Certification Schemes<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#2-certification-schemes>#</a></h3><p>Schemes that want to certify carbon removal under the <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a> must comply with the new implementing rules. The European Commission will assess each scheme — including consulting the European cooperation for Accreditation (EA) — before granting official recognition.</p><h3 id=3-certification-methodologies>3. Certification Methodologies<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#3-certification-methodologies>#</a></h3><p>The Commission is developing specific methodologies for different types of removal activities:</p><ul><li><strong>Permanent removals</strong> — the first delegated act was adopted on February 3, 2026, and is currently in a two-month scrutiny period with Parliament and the Council. If no objections arise, it should be published in the Official Journal in early April.</li><li><strong>Carbon farming</strong> — draft methodologies are in development</li><li><strong>Carbon storage in products</strong> — forthcoming</li></ul><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>The EU is building something that doesn&rsquo;t really exist anywhere else: a regulated, standardized framework for certifying carbon removal. The US has no equivalent. The UK has early-stage efforts. Most other jurisdictions rely entirely on voluntary registries with no government oversight.</p><p>The <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a> won&rsquo;t replace voluntary markets — it&rsquo;s a voluntary certification framework, not a compliance mechanism. But it creates an EU-endorsed quality standard. Companies and governments that want to buy high-integrity carbon removal credits will be able to look for <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a>-certified projects and know they&rsquo;ve been audited against a consistent, government-reviewed methodology.</p><p>For the CDR industry, this is infrastructure. It&rsquo;s not as exciting as a new DAC plant or a Google carbon removal purchase. But infrastructure is what turns a collection of startups into a functioning market. Without trusted certification, scaling carbon removal means scaling an honor system — and as the <a href=https://heatmap.news/carbon-removal/biomass-carbon-removal-accounting>carbon offset market learned the hard way</a>, honor systems don&rsquo;t scale.</p><p>The EU is building the rails.
