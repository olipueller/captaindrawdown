<p>Tuesday&rsquo;s lineup hit five stories across three continents — a potential billion-dollar CDR contract, European infrastructure that&rsquo;s actually being designed properly, two different flavors of DAC innovation, and a Swiss rodent that might outperform your favorite carbon removal startup.</p><h2 id=what-we-covered-today>What We Covered Today<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-we-covered-today>#</a></h2><p><strong><a href=/posts/microsoft-vaulted-deep-billion-cdr/>Microsoft&rsquo;s CDR Bet: The Vaulted Deep Deal Could Be Worth ~$1B</a></strong> — Microsoft agreed to store 4.9 million tonnes of CO₂ with Houston-based Vaulted Deep. That&rsquo;s more than the entire global CDR industry (excluding forestry) removed in 2023. The estimated price tag: roughly $1 billion. While the federal government freezes DAC hub funding, Microsoft is single-handedly creating a market. Impressive and deeply concerning in equal measure — one company&rsquo;s climate commitment shouldn&rsquo;t have to substitute for industrial policy.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/south-holland-cdr-hub-platform-zero/>South Holland Builds a Regional CDR Hub</a></strong> — The Netherlands showed how Europe does CDR infrastructure differently. Platform Zero brought SeaO2, Skytree, TNO, the Port of Rotterdam, provincial government, and a dozen other players into one room to co-design a CDR fieldlab. No mega-program announcements, no single-winner bets. Just everyone who matters designing the system together. The Dutch aren&rsquo;t going to out-subsidize the US — they might out-organize it.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/humidity-swing-polymers-dac-low-energy/>Humidity-Swing Polymers Could Make DAC Radically Cheaper</a></strong> — ASU researchers mapped exactly why certain polymers capture CO₂ when dry and release it when humid — no heat, no pressure, just weather cycles. The big insight: water transport and CO₂ transport can be optimized independently, which is exactly what materials engineers need to hear. The catch: these polymers need to survive ~100,000 wet-dry cycles to be economical. Fundamental research, not glamorous, absolutely essential.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/beaver-dams-carbon-removal-switzerland/>Beaver Dams Are Surprisingly Effective Carbon Sinks</a></strong> — A Swiss study in <em>Communications Earth & Environment</em> put real numbers on beaver-built wetlands as carbon sinks. Scaled nationally, they could offset 1.2–1.8% of Switzerland&rsquo;s emissions. The mechanism is elegant: beavers dam streams, organic matter accumulates in waterlogged soils where decomposition stalls, carbon piles up for decades. Self-replicating, self-maintaining, no Series A required.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/brewery-dac-beer-carbon-capture/>California Brewery Carbonating Beer With Air-Captured CO₂</a></strong> — Almanac Beer and Aircapture launched the world&rsquo;s first commercial beer carbonated with DAC-sourced CO₂. Sounds like a gimmick — it&rsquo;s actually a sharp business play. The CO₂ supply chain is broken (ethanol plants are sequestering their output for 45Q credits instead of selling it), and DAC solves the supply problem. Aircapture is targeting the $20B commercial CO₂ market with modular units. No federal funding needed. Sometimes gigatonne scale starts in a parking lot.</p><h2 id=stories-we-didnt-cover-but-you-should-know-about>Stories We Didn&rsquo;t Cover (But You Should Know About)<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#stories-we-didnt-cover-but-you-should-know-about>#</a></h2><p><strong>Liferaft signs 10-year deal with Microsoft for 1M CRUs</strong> — Another Microsoft procurement deal, this one with biochar company Liferaft. Microsoft is clearly building a diversified CDR portfolio across geological storage, biochar, and enhanced weathering. The scale of their procurement program continues to dwarf everyone else&rsquo;s.</p><p><strong>Biochar meta-analysis: 83% agricultural GWP reduction</strong> — High-temperature biochar applied at large rates reduced agricultural global warming potential by 83% according to a new meta-analysis. Strong validation for biochar as both a CDR pathway and an agricultural co-benefit story.</p><p><strong>India&rsquo;s tiger reserve converts invasive lantana to biochar</strong> — The Amrabad Tiger Reserve in India is piloting lantana-to-biochar conversion, tackling habitat restoration and local livelihoods simultaneously. CDR meeting conservation — good model for the Global South.</p><p><strong>Startup trying to preserve CDR know-how</strong> — Multiple sources flagged a startup working to salvage carbon removal knowledge before it&rsquo;s lost. With CDR companies failing and talent dispersing, institutional memory is a real problem the sector hasn&rsquo;t addressed.</p><h2 id=the-pattern>The Pattern<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-pattern>#</a></h2><p>Microsoft was everywhere today. The Vaulted Deep deal (~$1B, geological storage) plus the Liferaft deal (biochar) signals a company building a diversified CDR portfolio at a scale that makes most government programs look tentative. When one corporate buyer dominates your entire sector&rsquo;s demand signal, that&rsquo;s both validation and vulnerability.</p><p>Meanwhile, real innovation is happening at every scale — from polymer scientists at ASU labs to beavers in Swiss streams to a DAC unit in a California parking lot. The CDR sector is still finding its footing between megadeals and modularity, between corporate procurement and ecosystem design. Today suggested both paths are moving forward.</p>
