<p>Carbon removal is expanding into new territory — literally. Bolivian packaging and recycling company <a href=https://www.empacar.com.bo/>Empacar S.A.</a> is partnering with <a href=https://puro.earth>Puro.earth</a>, <a href=https://www.cula.tech/>Cula</a>, and <a href=https://www.bioflux.earth/>Bioflux</a> to build a biochar production facility in the Ascención de Guarayos region of Bolivia.</p><p>Once operational, the facility expects to capture approximately 70,000 tonnes of CO₂ annually, with first carbon removal credits targeted for 2027.</p><h2 id=from-sawmill-waste-to-permanent-carbon-storage>From Sawmill Waste to Permanent Carbon Storage<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#from-sawmill-waste-to-permanent-carbon-storage>#</a></h2><p>The facility will use wood waste from local sawmills — operations regulated by Bolivia&rsquo;s forestry authority, the Autoridad de Fiscalización y Control Social de Bosques y Tierra (ABT). Converting this waste into biochar locks carbon into a stable solid form that persists in soil for centuries, rather than letting the wood decompose and release its carbon back into the atmosphere.</p><p>Each partner brings a different piece of the puzzle:</p><ul><li><strong>Empacar</strong> provides local operational expertise — the company has nearly five decades of recycling and circular economy experience in Bolivia</li><li><strong>Puro.earth</strong> brings its crediting platform and certification standards for engineered carbon removal</li><li><strong>Cula</strong> handles digital monitoring, reporting, and verification (MRV)</li><li><strong>Bioflux</strong> acts as biochar project consultant</li></ul><p>The credits will be certified under the Puro standard and issued through the Puro Registry after passing all necessary audits.</p><h2 id=cdr-meets-latin-americas-circular-economy>CDR Meets Latin America&rsquo;s Circular Economy<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#cdr-meets-latin-americas-circular-economy>#</a></h2><p>What makes this project interesting isn&rsquo;t just the scale — 70,000 tonnes/year would make it one of the larger biochar facilities globally. It&rsquo;s the model.</p><p>Empacar launched a new business unit called <strong>CarbonX</strong> at the Bolivia Carbon Forum, positioning it as a platform for &ldquo;transforming environmental challenges into climate assets.&rdquo; CEO Carlos Limpias framed it in terms of both climate impact and local economic opportunity: the facility will create jobs in rural communities while converting what was previously waste into a globally traded commodity.</p><p>&ldquo;Through CarbonX, we are transforming industrial biomass residues into permanent carbon removal while creating economic opportunities for rural communities,&rdquo; Limpias said in <a href=https://carbonherald.com/empacar-puro-earth-cula-bioflux-unite-on-new-biochar-initiative-in-bolivia/>the announcement</a>.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>Most CDR activity is concentrated in North America and Europe. Projects like this in Bolivia signal that the industry is beginning to reach new geographies — places where feedstock is abundant, labor costs are lower, and existing industrial operations can be converted to serve the carbon removal market.</p><p>Latin America has enormous potential for biochar CDR. Agricultural and forestry residues are plentiful across the continent. If the economics work in Bolivia — a country not exactly known for its carbon market infrastructure — they&rsquo;ll work in a lot of other places too.</p><p>Puro.earth&rsquo;s Jan Willem Bode put it plainly: &ldquo;Scaling this industry requires more than technology — it requires trusted standards, transparent data and credible certification, and strong entrepreneurial companies willing to participate in this market.&rdquo;</p><p>The question now is whether 2027 delivery holds. Biochar projects tend to have shorter development timelines than DAC or BECCS, but first-of-kind facilities in new markets always carry execution risk. If Empacar delivers, expect more Latin American biochar to follow.</p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/verde-bioasphalt-biochar-roads-carbon-sinks/>Roads as Carbon Sinks: Verde Locks In Major Biochar Supply</a></li><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/green-carbon-india-biochar-partnership/>Japan-India Biochar Alliance: Industrial-Scale CDR Comes to Asia</a></li></ul>
