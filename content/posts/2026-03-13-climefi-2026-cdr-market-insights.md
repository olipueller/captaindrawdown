<p>ClimeFi just published its 2026 CDR market insights based on a December 2025 RFP that attracted 114 suppliers from 39 countries across 142 projects. The headline: <strong>durable CDR supply is getting tight.</strong></p><h2 id=the-numbers>The numbers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-numbers>#</a></h2><ul><li><strong>Only 52% of projected 2026 supply is still unreserved</strong> — making it the most constrained year in the 2026–2030 window</li><li>Across 2026–2030, 58% of total supply remains available</li><li><strong>66% of projects</strong> across all pathways (biochar, DACCS, BECCS, biomass, mCDR, mineralization) are now at commercialization stage</li><li>Prices are <strong>converging within pathways</strong> as suppliers reach operations, secure verification, and build track records</li></ul><h2 id=whats-driving-the-squeeze>What&rsquo;s driving the squeeze<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#whats-driving-the-squeeze>#</a></h2><p>Demand from corporate buyers is outpacing supply buildout. Companies like Microsoft, Boeing, LEGO, and Mercedes F1 are locking in multi-year deals — and the supply side simply can&rsquo;t scale fast enough.</p><p>The tightest spot is 2026 itself. Projects that broke ground in 2023–2024 are hitting commercial delivery, but there aren&rsquo;t enough of them to cover growing demand. By 2028–2030, supply projections look better as more projects reach operational scale.</p><h2 id=price-convergence--but-with-caveats>Price convergence — but with caveats<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#price-convergence--but-with-caveats>#</a></h2><p>ClimeFi found that prices within the same CDR pathway are narrowing, especially for mature pathways like biochar where there&rsquo;s enough operational history to benchmark. But they&rsquo;re careful to note the market is still young: MRV methodologies, quality benchmarks, and regulatory frameworks all influence pricing strategies differently across suppliers.</p><p>Cross-pathway price comparison remains difficult. A tonne of DACCS is not the same product as a tonne of biochar is not the same as a tonne of ocean alkalinity enhancement — even if they all claim to remove one tonne of CO₂.</p><h2 id=what-this-means-for-buyers>What this means for buyers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means-for-buyers>#</a></h2><p>If you&rsquo;re a corporate buyer thinking about CDR procurement: 2026 is already half-spoken-for. Waiting means fewer options and less negotiating leverage. The smart corporate buyers are building multi-year, multi-pathway portfolios now.</p><p>For CDR startups: the demand signal is real. The bottleneck isn&rsquo;t buyers — it&rsquo;s verified, deliverable supply.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://carbonherald.com/climefi-publishes-2026-insights-on-the-durable-carbon-removal-market/>Carbon Herald</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/lego-carbon-removal-portfolio/>LEGO Commits $7.9M to Carbon Removal — Testing Everything from Bio-Oil to Ocean CDR</a></li><li><a href=/posts/climefi-two-thirds-cdr-projects-commercial/>Two-Thirds of Durable CDR Projects Have Hit Commercialization</a></li><li><a href=/posts/lego-bets-on-carbon-removal/>Lego Just Tripled Down on Carbon Removal — Here&rsquo;s What They&rsquo;re Buying</a></li></ul>
