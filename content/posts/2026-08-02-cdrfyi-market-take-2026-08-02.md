Q1 2026 landed at 2.3 million tonnes of durable CDR contracted — roughly 560% of Q1 2025 volume, according to [CDR.fyi's report](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/2026-q1-durable-cdr-market-update-from-promise-to-proof). That is the largest opening quarter this market has ever recorded, and it deserves a careful read rather than a victory lap.

Start with the composition. CDR.fyi's Q1 update reports that Microsoft alone accounted for 43% of the quarter's volume via a single 1-million-tonne biochar deal with Liferaft — the second-largest BCR purchase in history. Strip Microsoft out and the remaining 113-plus buyers still contracted 1.3 million tonnes, which CDR.fyi flags as the third-highest non-Microsoft quarterly total ever. That is genuinely encouraging breadth underneath a very narrow peak. But the peak matters: one buyer, one method, one deal is still moving nearly half the market.

The method mix tells the same story from a different angle. CDR.fyi reports biochar carbon removal at 93% of contracted volume, and biochar was the method in all five of the top transactions, which together made up about 91% of the quarter. Biochar has become the workhorse of contracted tonnes because it is delivering — and here the delivery data backs it up: CDR.fyi's Q1 update logs 145,000 tonnes delivered and just over 100,000 tonnes retired, the second-highest quarter on record for both, with deliveries up 67% year over year. Contracts converting into actual carbon in storage is the metric I care most about, and that ratio is finally moving in the right direction.

Now the caveat I want practitioners to hold onto. CDR.fyi explicitly notes that 2.3 million tonnes, while a record Q1, is "a significant step down from the exceptional pace of mid-2025." Q1 has historically been the market's weakest quarter, so a record here does not automatically imply a record year. And CDR.fyi's finding that intermediaries facilitated 74% of contracted tonnes — their highest share on record — cuts two ways: it signals a maturing sourcing layer, but it also confirms that most buyers still cannot or will not run diligence in-house. Concentration in buyers, in method, and now in transaction pathways remains the fragility this market has to grow out of.

The physics of scaling to gigatonnes does not care whether tonnes come from one hyperscaler or a thousand mid-caps, but the politics and durability of demand absolutely do. What I will be watching through Q2: whether non-Microsoft, non-biochar volume can hold its floor, and whether delivered tonnes keep compounding at that 67% pace — because promise is cheap, and proof is what buys us the next decade.
