<p>Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage — BECCS — is the IPCC&rsquo;s workhorse. It appears in the vast majority of net-zero pathways. Climate models lean on it to close the gap between what emissions reductions can achieve and what the atmosphere actually needs. It&rsquo;s supposed to be the technology that makes the math work.</p><p>A new preprint from researchers at Princeton University, the University of Hong Kong, and the World Resources Institute says the math might be wrong.</p><h2 id=the-core-problem-carbon-debt>The Core Problem: Carbon Debt<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-core-problem-carbon-debt>#</a></h2><p>Their finding is stark: BECCS is &ldquo;likely to produce higher emissions for decades than natural gas without carbon capture.&rdquo;</p><p>How is that possible for a technology that&rsquo;s supposed to <em>remove</em> carbon?</p><p>The answer lies in carbon debt — the time lag between when biomass is harvested and when new growth reabsorbs the same amount of CO₂. When you burn a tree for energy (even if you capture the smokestack CO₂), you release carbon that took decades to accumulate. The replacement tree won&rsquo;t recapture that carbon for another 40-100 years, depending on the species and growing conditions.</p><p>During that gap, the atmosphere sees a net increase in CO₂. And if you&rsquo;re comparing BECCS to natural gas <em>without</em> capture, the biomass route can actually be worse — because the upfront carbon release from biomass harvesting exceeds the fossil emissions it&rsquo;s supposed to replace.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters-for-net-zero-models>Why This Matters for Net-Zero Models<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters-for-net-zero-models>#</a></h2><p>The IPCC&rsquo;s Sixth Assessment Report includes BECCS in nearly every pathway that limits warming to 1.5°C or 2°C. Some scenarios assume 5-10 gigatons of CO₂ removal per year from BECCS by 2050 — numbers that dwarf every other CDR method combined.</p><p>If those projections carry a decades-long carbon debt that models don&rsquo;t fully account for, the climate math changes dramatically. We might be planning to remove carbon that we first emitted by deploying the removal technology itself.</p><p>This isn&rsquo;t a new concern. Scientists have debated biomass carbon accounting for years. The EU&rsquo;s Renewable Energy Directive treats biomass as carbon-neutral at the smokestack — a simplification that critics have long challenged. What&rsquo;s new here is the direct comparison to unabated fossil fuels, and the blunt conclusion that BECCS could be <em>worse</em> than doing nothing for decades.</p><h2 id=not-all-beccs-is-equal>Not All BECCS Is Equal<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#not-all-beccs-is-equal>#</a></h2><p>Context matters. The researchers focused on woody biomass — the kind of BECCS that involves harvesting forests or using wood pellets. Agricultural residues, purpose-grown energy crops, and waste biomass have different carbon debt profiles. A BECCS plant running on sawmill waste or sugarcane bagasse doesn&rsquo;t carry the same multi-decade regrowth penalty as one burning whole trees.</p><p>The problem is that most large-scale BECCS proposals involve woody biomass, because that&rsquo;s where the volumes are. The Drax power station in the UK — the world&rsquo;s largest BECCS pilot — runs primarily on wood pellets, many imported from North American forests.</p><h2 id=what-this-means-for-cdr>What This Means for CDR<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means-for-cdr>#</a></h2><p>This preprint doesn&rsquo;t kill BECCS as a concept. But it does three things:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Demands better accounting.</strong> Carbon debt needs to be reflected in lifecycle assessments, not assumed away. When and how the carbon comes back matters as much as whether it comes back at all.</p></li><li><p><strong>Challenges model assumptions.</strong> If IPCC pathways are built on BECCS delivering net-negative emissions from day one, and the reality involves decades of net-positive emissions first, those pathways need revision.</p></li><li><p><strong>Strengthens the case for other CDR methods.</strong> <a href=/posts/cdr-researcher-census/>Direct air capture</a>, <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a>, biochar from waste streams, and ocean CDR don&rsquo;t carry the same carbon debt problem. They remove carbon without first releasing it.</p></li></ol><p>The CDR community has been debating the integrity of <a href=/posts/biomass-carbon-credits-accounting-crisis/>biomass carbon credits</a> all week. This preprint adds fuel — pun intended — to the fire.</p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/biomass-carbon-credits-accounting-crisis/>Biomass Carbon Credits Face an Accounting Crisis Before They Even Scale</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-crcf-certification-methodologies/>EU&rsquo;s Carbon Removal Certification Framework Gets Its Technical Rulebook</a></li><li><a href=/posts/georgia-tech-cdr-transparency-research/>Georgia Tech: CDR Won&rsquo;t Work Without Radical Transparency</a></li></ul>
