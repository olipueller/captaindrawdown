<p>A quieter Monday after yesterday&rsquo;s capital-flow blockbusters — but today&rsquo;s stories cut deeper on the &ldquo;how&rdquo; and &ldquo;whether&rdquo; of carbon removal. A German expert calls CDR a dangerous dream, the EU responds by issuing the first real certification methodologies, biology offers a radically different path to DAC, a visit to Mammoth exposes corporate accountability gaps, and biochar finds maybe its most creative feedstock yet: dirty diapers.</p><hr><h2 id=today-on-captaindrawdown>Today on CaptainDrawdown<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#today-on-captaindrawdown>#</a></h2><h3 id=-from-germany-the-dangerous-dream-of-co-removal--a-skeptics-case>🌍 From Germany: &ldquo;The Dangerous Dream of CO₂ Removal&rdquo; — A Skeptic&rsquo;s Case<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-from-germany-the-dangerous-dream-of-co-removal--a-skeptics-case>#</a></h3><p>Peter Droege, director of the Liechtenstein Institute for Strategic Development, argues in klimareporter.de that Germany&rsquo;s publicly funded CDR research programs — &ldquo;CDR terra&rdquo; and &ldquo;CDR mare&rdquo; — are a dangerous distraction. His four critiques: CDR capacity can&rsquo;t cover even Germany&rsquo;s residual emissions, the CDR belief system enables fossil fuel delay, ocean-based methods risk ecosystem harm, and biosphere regeneration should take priority over tech fixes.</p><p>We disagree with the conclusion — CDR and emission cuts aren&rsquo;t either/or. But the math challenge is real, and this kind of honest skepticism keeps the field accountable. Worth reading in full for anyone who wants to understand the European debate.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/german-cdr-criticism-klimareporter/>Read our full analysis →</a></strong></p><hr><h3 id=-bio-dac-microalgae-raceways-that-capture-co-straight-from-air>🔬 Bio-DAC: Microalgae Raceways That Capture CO₂ Straight From Air<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-bio-dac-microalgae-raceways-that-capture-co-straight-from-air>#</a></h3><p>Forget giant fans and chemical solvents. Researchers demonstrated a fundamentally different approach to direct air capture: 600 m² microalgae raceway reactors growing Scenedesmus under extreme carbon limitation. Starve the algae of concentrated CO₂, and they get remarkably good at pulling trace amounts from the atmosphere through photosynthesis.</p><p>The energy economics are intriguing — sunlight is the input, water is the medium, and biology handles the chemistry. Still early-stage, but if scaling works, bio-DAC could undercut engineered DAC costs dramatically.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/bio-dac-microalgae-raceway-co2/>Read our breakdown →</a></strong></p><hr><h3 id=-eus-carbon-removal-certification-framework-gets-its-first-methodologies>🏛️ EU&rsquo;s Carbon Removal Certification Framework Gets Its First Methodologies<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-eus-carbon-removal-certification-framework-gets-its-first-methodologies>#</a></h3><p>The European Commission adopted a delegated act setting out certification methodologies for permanent carbon removals under the Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming (CRCF) Regulation. This is the first standardised EU framework for certifying CDR activities — voluntary, but designed to become the quality benchmark with independent verification by recognised certification bodies.</p><p>The CRCF was adopted in December 2024 as part of the EU&rsquo;s climate neutrality 2050 strategy. Now the methodologies give it teeth. And if CDR credits become eligible for the EU Emissions Trading System — which the Commission must assess by July 2026 — that&rsquo;s a compliance market for engineered removal.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/eu-crcf-certification-methodologies/>Read our analysis →</a></strong></p><hr><h3 id=-investment-lessons-from-standing-next-to-mammoth>💰 Investment Lessons From Standing Next to Mammoth<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-investment-lessons-from-standing-next-to-mammoth>#</a></h3><p>Harmonic Financial Planning visited Climeworks&rsquo; Mammoth in Iceland — the world&rsquo;s largest DAC facility — and came back with a reality check for climate investors. The tech is viscerally real. But Harvard Business School studied 1,000+ companies with 2020 emissions targets: nearly a third never disclosed whether they achieved them. Among those that did report, many missed their goals but barely admitted it.</p><p>Then there&rsquo;s &ldquo;greenhushing&rdquo; — companies doing climate work but refusing to talk about it, fearing scrutiny. CDR technology exists. Corporate accountability? Still catching up.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/mammoth-dac-investment-lessons/>Read the full story →</a></strong></p><hr><h3 id=-diapers--biochar-turning-baby-waste-into-carbon-removal>♻️ Diapers → Biochar: Turning Baby Waste Into Carbon Removal<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-diapers--biochar-turning-baby-waste-into-carbon-removal>#</a></h3><p>Disposable diapers are the third-largest consumer item in US landfills. Over 3 million diapers diverted from landfills so far.</p><p>The plant-based diaper market hit $1.42 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $3.52 billion by 2033.
