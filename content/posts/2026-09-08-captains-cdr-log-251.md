## Why this matters now

Verra's Verified Carbon Standard version 5 just earned an eligibility stamp from the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market. Press coverage is already collapsing that into "ICVCM-approved credits." It isn't. The tick applies to the program - the governance, registry, and tracking plumbing - not to the tonnage math of any specific methodology sitting on top. If you are buying, developing, or writing about carbon removal credits this week, that distinction is the whole story.

## What is it?

The Core Carbon Principles are a two-track quality framework run by the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market. Track one assesses whether a crediting program (Verra, Gold Standard, ART, etc.) has adequate governance, registry infrastructure, and a defensible measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) framework. Track two assesses individual methodology categories - biochar, afforestation/reforestation, enhanced rock weathering (ERW, spreading crushed silicate rock to draw down CO2), direct air capture - one at a time.

A program can pass track one while its methodologies are still queued for, or failing, track two. That is exactly where Verra sits today.

## Who's involved?

Verra runs the VCS, the largest voluntary carbon registry. The Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market is the independent body that issues the Core Carbon Principles label. Buyers, brokers, and offset-reliant corporates are the audience the label is aimed at.

## What just happened?

Verra's VCS v5 was recognized as meeting the Core Carbon Principles at the program level, per [Verra's own program notice](https://verra.org/program-notice/verra-launches-version-5-of-the-vcs-program/). The v5 upgrade tightens community safeguards, additionality tests, and reversal risk management, according to [Biochar Today's summary](https://biochartoday.com/news/verra-wins-integrity-council-approval-for-vcs-version-5-program-standards/).

Notably, even the outlet reporting the win framed it precisely. Biochar Today ([@biochartoday.bsky.social](https://bsky.app/profile/biochartoday.bsky.social/post/3muwp7pyb5x2s)) wrote that the Integrity Council "recognized Verra's VCS Version 5 as CCP-Eligible" and tied that recognition to specific governance upgrades - not to any credit-quality guarantee.

The steel-man of the critique: program-level labels are worth having. Bad plumbing sinks good methodologies, and v5's safeguards are real improvements. But the objection stands. A governance tick cannot resolve pathway-level measurement disputes, and pretending it can misleads buyers.

Two preprints published in the same window make the point concrete. A [CDRXIV preprint from Ahmed, Suhrhoff, Reinhard and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.70212/cdrxiv.2026533.v2) shows the alkalinity component of biochar-driven removal is systematically mis-accounted when cation chemistry is ignored. A [Stanford Digital Repository paper from Rogers, Maher, and Caers](https://doi.org/10.25740/ty118xz8869) constrains the measurement basis for crediting soil alkalinity enhancement. Both suggest the tonnage math for two popular removal pathways is still moving.

A parallel conflation is worth noting. The UK just published its [list of qualifying overseas carbon pricing schemes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-cbam-current-qualifying-carbon-pricing-schemes/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-list-of-current-qualifying-carbon-pricing-schemes) for its border adjustment mechanism. "Qualifying" means eligible for the adjustment calculation, not that the 15 schemes are equivalent in stringency. Same pattern, different regulator.

## Open questions

- Which VCS v5 methodology categories does the Integrity Council assess next, and in what order?
- Do removal-heavy categories like biochar and ERW clear the category-level bar as cleanly as the program did?
- How will registries update issued credits if a methodology fails category assessment after v5 program approval?
- Will buyer contracts start distinguishing "program-eligible" from "category-eligible" credits in writing?
