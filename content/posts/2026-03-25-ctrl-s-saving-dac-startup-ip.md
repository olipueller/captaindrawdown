<p>The name says it all. Ctrl-S — the keyboard shortcut for <em>save</em> — is a new company doing exactly that for the direct air capture sector: saving the work before it&rsquo;s lost.</p><p>Here&rsquo;s the context. DAC investment has <a href=https://heatmap.news/>dropped more than 60%</a> from its 2022 peak. The Trump administration cancelled tens of millions in federal DAC funding. Companies that raised on the 2021-2022 climate hype cycle are running out of runway, and when a deeptech startup dies, its IP, experimental data, and hard-won engineering knowledge often die with it. Papers in drawers. Lab notebooks in storage units. Sorbent formulations that took years to develop, gone.</p><p>Jason Hochman, who spent four years running the Direct Air Capture Coalition, saw this coming and founded Ctrl-S to do something about it.</p><h2 id=a-knowledge-salvage-operation>A Knowledge Salvage Operation<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#a-knowledge-salvage-operation>#</a></h2><p>The model is straightforward but clever. Ctrl-S acquires intellectual property, experimental data, and engineering knowledge from distressed DAC companies. It assembles these assets into a structured library that better-positioned companies can access through subscriptions, licensing fees, and royalties.</p><p>The founding team reads like a who&rsquo;s-who of DAC institutional knowledge: Nicole Williams, former IP Lead at Climeworks, and Silvan Aeschlimann bring deep expertise in what&rsquo;s actually protectable and valuable in this space. They&rsquo;re not just hoarding patents — they&rsquo;re diligencing every acquisition for technoeconomics, lifecycle analysis, and commercial potential.</p><p>Crucially, they distinguish between companies that failed because the tech didn&rsquo;t work versus companies that failed because the market wasn&rsquo;t ready, the funding dried up, or the team couldn&rsquo;t execute. That distinction determines whether an asset is a cautionary tale or a shortcut.</p><h2 id=the-negative-learnings-are-the-real-gold>The Negative Learnings Are the Real Gold<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-negative-learnings-are-the-real-gold>#</a></h2><p>Here&rsquo;s what caught my eye: Ctrl-S explicitly values &ldquo;negative learnings&rdquo; — the things that <em>didn&rsquo;t</em> work. This matters enormously for the emerging field of AI-driven materials science. Machine learning models for discovering new sorbents, solvents, and contactors need training data, and knowing what fails is just as informative as knowing what succeeds. Every dead-end experiment a shuttered startup ran is a data point that could prevent another company from spending months on the same dead end.</p><p>There&rsquo;s also a cross-industry angle. Components like air contactors and sorbent materials have applications beyond CO₂ capture — think hydrogen separation, industrial gas purification, even air quality. The IP library Ctrl-S is building could have value well beyond the DAC sector.</p><h2 id=what-this-tells-us-about-the-industry>What This Tells Us About the Industry<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-tells-us-about-the-industry>#</a></h2><p>When an industry starts building knowledge preservation infrastructure during a downturn, that&rsquo;s a sign of maturation, not decline. The semiconductor industry did this. Biotech did this. It&rsquo;s what happens when people who&rsquo;ve been in the trenches decide the work is too important to lose, even when the money gets scared.</p><p>The DAC funding winter is real. But the technology didn&rsquo;t stop working just because VCs got cautious and politicians got hostile. Ctrl-S is betting that when the market turns — and it will — the companies that can leapfrog failed experiments will move fastest.</p><p><strong>Ctrl-S. Because the worst thing about a funding winter isn&rsquo;t the companies that die — it&rsquo;s the knowledge that dies with them.</strong></p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href=https://heatmap.news/>Heatmap News</a>; Direct Air Capture Coalition; Ctrl-S founding team announcements.</em></p>
