<p>Here&rsquo;s an uncomfortable truth the Paris Agreement doesn&rsquo;t really grapple with: even if we hit 1.5°C and hold there, the damage keeps piling up.</p><p>Two new studies from IIASA (the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis in Vienna), published in <em>Environmental Research Letters</em>, lay out just how long the carbon removal commitment needs to last. The answer? Centuries.</p><h2 id=the-lag-problem>The Lag Problem<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-lag-problem>#</a></h2><p>Sea-level rise doesn&rsquo;t stop when temperatures stabilize. Neither does permafrost thaw. These are slow-moving processes with enormous inertia — think of a supertanker that keeps drifting long after the engines are cut.</p><p>The IIASA researchers modeled what it takes to actually halt the growth of these delayed impacts. Simply reaching net-zero isn&rsquo;t enough. You need sustained <em>net-negative</em> emissions — pulling more CO₂ out of the atmosphere than we put in — for hundreds of years.</p><h2 id=what-this-means-for-cdr>What This Means for CDR<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means-for-cdr>#</a></h2><p>The implications for the carbon removal industry are profound:</p><p><strong>1. CDR isn&rsquo;t a temporary fix.</strong> Current policy timelines (2050 net-zero targets) are a starting point, not a finish line. The real commitment is multi-generational.</p><p><strong>2. Durability matters more than ever.</strong> If we need CDR operating for centuries, the storage has to last at least that long. That&rsquo;s why approaches like geological mineralization, deep ocean storage, and DACCS with geological sequestration are so critical — and why short-lived offsets are fundamentally inadequate for this job.</p><p><strong>3. The investment case just got longer.</strong> Companies and governments planning CDR infrastructure need to think in terms of permanent institutions, not project timelines. This is closer to building a water treatment system than launching a startup.</p><h2 id=the-uncomfortable-bottom-line>The Uncomfortable Bottom Line<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-uncomfortable-bottom-line>#</a></h2><p>We&rsquo;re not going to remove our way out of the climate crisis in a few decades. The physics demands a durable, multi-century commitment to pulling carbon from the atmosphere. That makes every investment in CDR capacity today not just a climate action — it&rsquo;s infrastructure for a civilizational commitment our grandchildren&rsquo;s grandchildren will still need.</p><p>The sooner policy catches up to this reality, the better our odds.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://iiasa.ac.at/news/mar-2026/centuries-of-net-negative-emissions-required-to-secure-a-safe-climate-future>IIASA — Centuries of net-negative emissions required to secure a safe climate future</a> | Published in <em>Environmental Research Letters</em>, March 2026</p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li>[What Is <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>Enhanced Weathering</a>?
