<p>The voluntary carbon market has a credibility problem. Everyone knows it. The question is whether the rules being drafted right now can fix it — or at least prevent CDR from inheriting the same mess.</p><p>The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) is <a href=https://www.usresistnews.org/2026/03/16/the-first-global-rules-for-carbon-removal-credits-are-being-written-right-now-environment-policy-brief-191/>finalizing updates</a> to the technical standards that govern how greenhouse gas emissions and removals are reported and managed. The revised ISO 14001:2026 is planned for publication in April 2026, with a three-year transition period. For the first time, these standards will explicitly address how carbon removal is measured, verified, and credited.</p><p>This is the plumbing. It&rsquo;s not exciting. It&rsquo;s essential.</p><h2 id=why-standards-matter-now>Why Standards Matter Now<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-standards-matter-now>#</a></h2><p>The IPCC&rsquo;s Sixth Assessment Report was unambiguous: large-scale carbon dioxide removal is required across every pathway that limits warming to 1.5°C. That finding kicked off a scramble. Governments are building <a href=/posts/canada-first-government-cdr-procurement/>procurement programs</a> and <a href=/posts/eu-crcf-certification-methodologies/>certification frameworks</a>. Companies are spending billions on <a href=/posts/big-tech-carbon-credits-explode-ai-race/>carbon removal credits</a>. Startups are deploying DAC, <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a>, biochar, and ocean alkalinity enhancement.</p><p>But without globally recognized rules for what counts as a legitimate removal — how long carbon must be stored, how accurately it must be measured, what baseline to compare against — the market is building on shifting ground.</p><p>Right now, CDR represents <a href=https://icvcm.org/engineered-carbon-dioxide-removal-cdr/>less than 1% of the voluntary carbon market</a> by issued volume. It&rsquo;s going to grow fast. The rules written in the next 12 months will determine whether that growth produces a credible climate tool or a repeat of the offset market&rsquo;s worst failures.</p><h2 id=the-offset-cautionary-tale>The Offset Cautionary Tale<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-offset-cautionary-tale>#</a></h2><p>The track record isn&rsquo;t encouraging. A <a href=https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-environ-112823-064813>2025 review paper</a> found that the problems with carbon offsets — reforestation, renewable energy credits, avoided deforestation — aren&rsquo;t edge cases. They&rsquo;re systemic. Among Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) projects, 85% had a &ldquo;low likelihood of ensuring environmental integrity.&rdquo; Only 2% scored high.</p><p>In the U.S., more than a third of offset credits come from improved forest management projects. Studies consistently find these have minimal real benefit — trees that would have grown anyway, baselines drawn generously, permanence assumptions that wildfire seasons keep destroying.</p><p>CDR is supposed to be different. DAC captures CO₂ from ambient air and stores it as mineralized rock. <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>Enhanced weathering</a> measures dissolved alkalinity in runoff. Biochar locks carbon in a stable solid. The physics of these approaches is harder to game than &ldquo;we promise not to cut down these trees.&rdquo;</p><p>But &ldquo;harder to game&rdquo; isn&rsquo;t &ldquo;impossible to game.&rdquo; Without rigorous standards, the CDR credit market could still end up with over-crediting, questionable baselines, and durability claims that don&rsquo;t hold up.</p><h2 id=what-the-iso-update-covers>What the ISO Update Covers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-the-iso-update-covers>#</a></h2><p>The revised standard includes requirements for:</p><ul><li><strong>Determining GHG emission and removal boundaries</strong> — what&rsquo;s in scope, what&rsquo;s out</li><li><strong>Quantifying an organization&rsquo;s emissions and removals</strong> — actual measurement, not estimates</li><li><strong>Identifying specific actions aimed at improving GHG management</strong> — accountability for what companies say they&rsquo;re doing</li></ul><p>For CDR specifically, the key debates center on storage durability (how long must carbon stay put?), measurement accuracy (what margin of error is acceptable?), and additionality (would this removal have happened without the credit revenue?).</p><p>These aren&rsquo;t academic questions. They determine whether a DAC operator selling 1,000-year geological storage gets the same credit as a soil carbon project claiming 20-year persistence. The answer should obviously be no — but without explicit standards, the market treats them the same.</p><h2 id=what-else-is-moving>What Else Is Moving<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-else-is-moving>#</a></h2><p>The ISO update isn&rsquo;t happening in isolation:</p><ul><li>The <strong>EU&rsquo;s <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a></strong> has adopted <a href=/posts/eu-crcf-certification-methodologies/>certification methodologies</a> for permanent carbon removals, with potential ETS integration by July 2026</li><li>The <strong>ICVCM</strong> (Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market) has been developing Core Carbon Principles with specific CDR guidance</li><li><strong>Puro.earth</strong> and <strong>Isometric</strong> are building CDR-specific registries with methodologies for individual pathways</li></ul><p>The risk is fragmentation — a patchwork of regional and private standards that don&rsquo;t interoperate. A <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a>-certified credit in Europe, an ICVCM-aligned credit in North America, and an ISO-compliant credit globally could all mean slightly different things. The ISO update could serve as the baseline that harmonizes everything else, if it&rsquo;s rigorous enough.</p><h2 id=the-stakes>The Stakes<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-stakes>#</a></h2><p>Carbon removal credits are currently a niche product bought mostly by <a href=/posts/microsoft-93-percent-cdr-credits/>a handful of tech companies</a>. If the standards are done right — strict on durability, precise on measurement, clear on additionality — CDR credits could eventually integrate into compliance markets like the EU ETS, unlocking demand at an entirely different scale.</p><p>If the standards are done badly — loose definitions, generous baselines, weak verification — CDR will inherit the reputational damage that&rsquo;s crippled the offset market, and the technology that climate science says we need will struggle to attract the capital it needs to scale.</p><p>April 2026. That&rsquo;s when ISO 14001:2026 is expected. Three years to transition. The CDR market&rsquo;s credibility window is right now.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://www.usresistnews.org/2026/03/16/the-first-global-rules-for-carbon-removal-credits-are-being-written-right-now-environment-policy-brief-191/>USResist News</a>.</em></p><hr><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/eu-crcf-certification-methodologies/>EU&rsquo;s Carbon Removal Certification Framework Gets Its First Methodologies</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biomass-carbon-credits-accounting-crisis/>Biomass Carbon Credits Face an Accounting Crisis Before They Even Scale</a></li><li><a href=/posts/georgia-tech-cdr-transparency-research/>Georgia Tech: CDR Won&rsquo;t Work Without Radical Transparency</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-crcf-carbon-removal-certification/>The EU Just Built the World&rsquo;s First Government CDR Credit Label</a></li></ul>
