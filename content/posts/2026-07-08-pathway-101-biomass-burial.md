The core idea is old-fashioned to the point of being counterintuitive: plants already pull carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere for free, so instead of building machines to capture CO₂, take biomass that would otherwise rot or burn and put it somewhere it can't decompose. Biomass burial — sometimes called terrestrial storage of biomass, wood vaulting, or biomass carbon removal and storage without the energy step — matters for durable CDR because it sidesteps the two most expensive parts of engineered removal: capture and (in most variants) compression. The bet is that preventing decomposition is cheaper per tonne than direct air capture, and more durable than forests. Whether that bet holds depends almost entirely on how well you can keep oxygen, water, and microbes away from dead plant matter for centuries.
