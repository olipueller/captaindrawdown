Three preprints landed on CDRxiv this cycle from Andes Ag and collaborators, and taken together they do something the enhanced weathering field has been quietly asking for: they build a bench-to-mesocosm evidence stack for the idea that a specific microbe — *Bacillus subtilis* strain MP1 — can measurably speed silicate dissolution and push the resulting cations toward carbonate formation. That caught my eye because "biology accelerates weathering" has long been asserted in general terms but rarely pinned to a named strain, a mass balance, and a CDR number in the same body of work.

**What we knew going in.** Enhanced rock weathering has been climbing the CDR hype curve for a few years — [Nature Reviews Earth & Environment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-025-00713-7) called out rapidly growing commercial investment, and modeling in [Communications Sustainability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44458-026-00034-w) projects up to 0.76–1.1 Gt CO₂/yr by 2050–2100. But the ground truth has been sobering. A recent [C&EN summary](https://cen.acs.org/environment/climate-change/enhanced-rock-weathering-co2-sequestration-climate-change/103/web/2025/12) of a three-year field trial reported removal rates under 10% of expectations, and a [Frontiers in Climate](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2025.1657058/full) Midwestern trial found evidence for CDR with steel slag but not basalt. Meanwhile, [Project Drawdown](https://drawdown.org/explorer/deploy-enhanced-rock-weathering) flags unpredictable effectiveness and hard MRV as the persistent bottlenecks. The gap between lab kinetics and field yield is where microbes have long been suspected to matter — and where these papers are aimed.

**What's new.** The first paper, [Yip et al.](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/480), shows that *B. subtilis* MP1 and MP2 incubated with basalt release significantly more soluble Ca²⁺ than abiotic controls, and — this is the fun part — the cations released (Ca²⁺, Fe²⁺/³⁺, Mn²⁺) themselves trigger bacterial sporulation. That turns sporulation into a candidate biosensor for silicate dissolution, a genuinely novel MRV angle. SEM images show feldspar twinning on treated basalt surfaces alongside ovoid spores parked at the mineral interface.

The second, [Lawrence et al.](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/464), goes after the MRV problem head-on: in a 63-day soybean mesocosm inoculated with MP1, they use sequential extractions to distinguish newly weathered base cations from cations merely reshuffled among exchangeable, oxidizable, and reducible pools. That distinction is exactly what field ERW trials struggle with. They report a 4.5% increase in base cation charge over controls, with 37–67% of that ending up as carbonates — a CDR of 0.20–0.36 g CO₂/kg soil. Notably, no feedstock was added: the microbe accelerated weathering of silicates already in the soil, which they frame as "microbially accelerated weathering" (MAW) distinct from feedstock-based EW.

The third, [Yang et al.](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/369), tests the combination: MP1 plus crushed basalt in a 91-day mesocosm at pH 6.6. Co-application raised carbonate alkalinity beyond basalt alone, and total carbon was highest in the combined treatment — hinting that MP1 may offset the organic-carbon priming losses that have dogged basalt trials.

**How the field is reacting.** Secondary discussion so far is mostly the underlying microbiology — sporulation mechanics in *B. subtilis* ([Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-026-10705-y), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42555712/)) — rather than CDR commentary. It's early. But the sporulation-as-biosensor idea is the kind of thing that could travel.

**Who this bears on.** Every ERW operator working with basalt — [Lithos](https://www.lithoscarbon.com/), [InPlanet](https://inplanet.earth/), [Terradot](https://terradot.earth/), [Alt Carbon](https://altcarbon.com/) — has a stake in whether a microbial co-amendment can lift field yields toward lab expectations and blunt priming losses. The MAW-without-feedstock framing in Lawrence et al. is a different animal: it points toward a lower-logistics pathway that skips mining and grinding entirely, though at what looks like modest per-kg-soil rates. [CREW Carbon](https://crewcarbon.com/) and [CarbonRun](https://www.carbonrun.io/), operating in the alkalinity-enhancement adjacency, are less directly touched but share the MRV puzzle of proving cations came from silicates.

**What I'm watching next.** Whether MP1 survives and functions in a real field with native microbial competition; whether the 4.5% base-cation bump scales or saturates; whether sporulation-based biosensing gets picked up by other groups; and whether any of the basalt-deploying companies run a co-inoculation trial. If MAW holds up outside the mesocosm, the ERW yield gap has a new lever.
