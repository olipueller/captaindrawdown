<p>In October 2025, the Trump administration cancelled over <strong>$1.2 billion in federal funding</strong> for the two flagship US Direct Air Capture hubs — Project Cypress in Louisiana and the South Texas DAC Hub. It was the single largest withdrawal of public capital from the CDR sector in history.</p><p>The fallout is still shaping the market today.</p><h2 id=what-changed>What Changed<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-changed>#</a></h2><p>The Biden administration spent 2021-2024 building DAC as a standalone climate industry. The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law and Inflation Reduction Act provided the architecture: direct federal co-funding for first-of-a-kind commercial facilities, designed to prove that DAC can work at scale and attract private capital.</p><p>The Trump administration <a href=https://enkiai.com/carbon-capture/dac-market-2026-political-shifts-redefine-investment-risk>kept the $180/ton 45Q tax credit</a> — but that credit is far more valuable for Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR) than for permanent geological storage. The result: DAC&rsquo;s most government-incentivized pathway now runs through the oil industry.</p><p>Meanwhile, the Department of Energy&rsquo;s Office of Clean Energy Demonstrations — the institutional home for DAC hub oversight — was gutted. Projects that were months from breaking ground found themselves without federal partners, without co-funding, and in some cases without permits.</p><h2 id=the-industrys-response>The Industry&rsquo;s Response<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-industrys-response>#</a></h2><p>The DAC sector is splitting into two tracks:</p><p><strong>Track 1: EOR-aligned projects</strong> that can leverage the 45Q credit while capturing CO₂ for use in oil recovery. These have a clear business case — but a questionable climate case. Using captured atmospheric CO₂ to extract more fossil fuels is, at best, a complicated accounting exercise.</p><p><strong>Track 2: Pure sequestration projects</strong> that need to find alternative funding. Canada&rsquo;s new <a href=https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/politics/2026/03/05/new-coalition-targets-100m-for-canadian-carbon-removal-projects-by-2030/>$100M Advance Carbon Removal Coalition</a> is one example of how other countries are filling the gap. The IEA&rsquo;s <a href=https://carbonherald.com/iea-carbon-management-technologies-gain-funding-and-policy-support/>State of Energy Innovation report</a> shows CDR venture capital still flowing — but the &ldquo;missing middle&rdquo; between VC and project finance is wider than ever.</p><h2 id=what-this-means>What This Means<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means>#</a></h2><p>The US was supposed to be the world&rsquo;s DAC proving ground. That role is now contested. Europe (with <a href=https://www.handelsblatt.com/unternehmen/nachhaltigkeit/klimaziele-co-entnahme-im-kampf-gegen-den-klimawandel-ist-entscheidend/100201919.html>Germany&rsquo;s €98M CDR budget</a> and Denmark&rsquo;s $4.2B CCS fund), Canada, and even Iceland are positioning themselves as alternatives.</p><p>For investors: US DAC is no longer a policy-backed bet — it&rsquo;s a regulatory roulette. Political risk has replaced technology risk as the sector&rsquo;s biggest variable.</p><p>For the CDR market globally: the lesson is clear. Dependence on any single government&rsquo;s funding is fragile. The industry needs diversified demand — from corporate buyers (Boeing, Microsoft, Mercedes), multilateral frameworks, and multiple national programs simultaneously.</p><p>$1.2 billion disappeared overnight. The technology didn&rsquo;t change. The politics did.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://enkiai.com/carbon-capture/dac-market-2026-political-shifts-redefine-investment-risk>EnkiAI — DAC Market 2026: Political Shifts Redefine Investment Risk</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/iea-cdr-top-vc-bet/>IEA Report: Carbon Removal Is Now One of the Hottest VC Categories in Energy</a></li><li><a href=/posts/cdr-roundup-feb-2026/>CDR Roundup: What&rsquo;s Moving in Carbon Removal (Feb 2026)</a></li><li><a href=/posts/500mw-solar-for-stratos-dac/>500MW of Solar for the World&rsquo;s Largest DAC Plant</a></li><li><a href=/posts/climeworks-calgary-hq-cold-climate-dac/>Climeworks Opens Calgary HQ — Cold-Climate DAC Testing by Fall 2026</a></li></ul>
