<p>This one&rsquo;s different. Not a tech startup. Not a corporate buyer. A <strong>local council in rural England</strong> just became the first UK local authority to partially own a biochar carbon removal system.</p><p>Shropshire Council has partnered with Raft Energy and Biodynamic Carbon (a joint venture between the council and Carbon Hill Ltd.) to produce activated biochar and generate verified carbon removal credits.</p><h2 id=how-it-works>How it works<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#how-it-works>#</a></h2><p>The system — already operational near Welshpool — integrates biochar production with existing anaerobic digestion (AD) infrastructure. Raft Energy&rsquo;s product, ActiCH4R, is an activated biochar that gets added to biogas plants. The biochar:</p><ol><li><strong>Boosts energy output</strong> from anaerobic digestion</li><li><strong>Improves fertiliser quality</strong> from the digestate</li><li><strong>Locks away carbon permanently</strong> — generating certified removal credits under the European Biochar Certificate (EBC)</li></ol><p>A second unit, fully owned by the council, is being commissioned this spring in Ludlow.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why this matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>CDR usually makes headlines through billion-dollar DAC plants or tech startup funding rounds. This is the opposite: small-scale, practical, integrated into existing waste infrastructure, owned by a local government.</p><p>The economics work because biochar serves triple duty — energy boost, soil amendment, and carbon removal. It&rsquo;s not a pure CDR play that needs carbon credit revenue to survive. The carbon credits are a bonus on top of operational value.</p><p>It&rsquo;s also a model for other local authorities. The UK has hundreds of AD plants. If biochar integration can work in Shropshire, it can work anywhere with organic waste streams and a pyrolysis unit.</p><p>Shropshire isn&rsquo;t going to single-handedly solve climate change with a biochar kiln near Welshpool. But this is what deployment looks like at the municipal level: unglamorous, practical, and quietly effective.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://newsroom.shropshire.gov.uk/2026/03/shropshire-council-partners-with-raft-energy-to-lead-the-way-in-sustainable-carbon-removal/>Shropshire Council Newsroom</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/green-carbon-india-biochar-partnership/>Japan-India Biochar Alliance: Industrial-Scale CDR Comes to Asia</a></li><li><a href=/posts/west-virginia-biochar-tax-credit/>West Virginia Bets on Biochar: Coal State Stacks Tax Credits for Carbon Removal</a></li></ul>
