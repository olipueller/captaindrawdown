> Take on a podcast episode from **Reversing Climate Change**, originally published Thu, 23 Ap.
> Listen: <https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/396-Why-We-All-Keep-Going-to-Carbon-Unboundw-Oli-Katz--Unbound-Summits-e3ibamm>

[Listen here](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/396-Why-We-All-Keep-Going-to-Carbon-Unboundw-Oli-Katz--Unbound-Summits-e3ibamm). Ross Kenyon hosts Oliver Katz, founder and CEO of Unbound Summits, the company behind Carbon Unbound — the conference series that has, fairly or not, become the default in-person gathering for the durable carbon dioxide removal industry on both US coasts and in Europe. The conversation is meta: it's about the business of running a CDR conference, why people keep showing up, and what that says about where the industry is in its lifecycle. Working only from publisher show notes here, so treat specifics with the usual caution.

The substantive thread, per the notes, is Katz on the economics of conference organizing and the "pay-to-play dynamics of industry speaking slots." That's worth a CDR practitioner's attention because it cuts directly at how information flows in this market. If suppliers buying stage time is the dominant funding model, the agenda you see at any sector event — Carbon Unbound included — is partly a reflection of who could afford to be on it, not who has the most interesting tonnes. Buyers triaging a dozen pathways from a conference seat should price that in. Katz also reportedly discusses why the parallel adaptation event series didn't work, which is a useful negative result: adaptation lacks the buyer-supplier-registry-policy stack that gives durable CDR enough commercial gravity to sustain a recurring trade show. CDR has a market — thin, subsidized, but real — and adaptation, so far, doesn't.

The Schelling-point framing is the other piece worth taking seriously. Durable CDR is still small enough that one or two events a year can plausibly host most of the buyers, suppliers, registries and policy folks in one room. Compare to where [Frontier](https://frontierclimate.com/) publishes deal terms or where [CDR.fyi](https://www.cdr.fyi/) tracks delivered tonnes — the industry's coordination infrastructure remains startlingly compact. For context on the buyer-side legal plumbing the episode's sponsor (Philip Lee LLP) works on, the prior RCC episodes on [project finance](https://carbonremoval.substack.com/) and CORSIA are linked in the notes and are more technically meaty than this one will be. If you want a harder-edged read on whether convening = progress, the recurring critique that conferences substitute for tonnes delivered is worth holding alongside Katz's pitch.

Useful for: practitioners deciding whether Carbon Unbound East Coast (NYC, May 19-20) is worth the flight, and anyone curious about the political economy of who gets a microphone in CDR. Skip if you want technical pathway content — this is an industry-meta episode, not a tonnes-and-measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) one.
