Google, McKinsey, and Meta just signed 10-year deals with Living Carbon for 131,240 tonnes of CDR from engineered trees planted on old Appalachian mine lands.
---
The buyers are Symbiosis Coalition members. Living Carbon uses trees with enhanced photosynthesis, planted on degraded US coal mining sites. The offtake structure locks in a decade of removals from a single afforestation project.
---
Two signals worth watching. First, hyperscalers are diversifying beyond DAC and mineralization into engineered biology. Second, reclaimed mine land is becoming a preferred CDR substrate. Cheap, degraded, and symbolically tidy for coal country.
---
Caveat: tree-based removals carry real durability risk. Fire, disease, and land-use reversal can release stored carbon. The 10-year offtake is not the same as 100-year storage. MRV (measurement, reporting, verification) on growth and permanence will decide if this category holds
