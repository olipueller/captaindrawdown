<p>Climeworks is the most recognized name in direct air capture. The Swiss company operates Orca in Iceland (4,000 tCO₂/year), is building Mammoth (36,000 tCO₂/year), and has become synonymous with permanent, technology-based carbon removal. Its brand is DAC.</p><p>So why is Climeworks now integrating biochar into its product offering?</p><p>Because portfolio construction logic is replacing single-pathway thinking across the CDR market. And Climeworks is smart enough to follow the signal.</p><h2 id=the-cost-curve-argument>The Cost Curve Argument<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-cost-curve-argument>#</a></h2><p>DAC is expensive. Current costs are $600-1,000+ per tonne CO₂. Even at projected scale (millions of tonnes per year), costs are expected to remain above $200-300/tonne for at least a decade. The permanence is excellent: CO₂ is captured from air and injected into geological storage, where it mineralizes over thousands of years. But the price tag limits the buyer pool.</p><p>Biochar occupies a different position on the cost-permanence curve. High-quality biochar, produced by pyrolyzing biomass at temperatures above 500°C, costs $100-300 per tonne CO₂. The carbon is locked into stable aromatic structures with H/C organic ratios below 0.4, giving mean residence times in the hundreds to thousands of years. Not geological permanence, but far longer than the 100-year accounting horizon most carbon markets use.</p><p>By offering both pathways, Climeworks can serve buyers at multiple price points. A corporate buyer with a $500/tonne budget gets DAC. A buyer at $150/tonne gets certified biochar. A buyer building a diversified portfolio gets a blended cost that balances permanence, price, and delivery certainty.</p><h2 id=what-high-quality-means-in-biochar>What &ldquo;High-Quality&rdquo; Means in Biochar<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-high-quality-means-in-biochar>#</a></h2><p>Not all biochar qualifies. Climeworks&rsquo; reputation depends on scientific rigor, and integrating low-quality biochar would undermine it. The biochar entering Climeworks&rsquo; portfolio is certified against established standards:</p><p><strong>Pyrolysis temperature</strong> above 450-500°C. Higher temperatures produce more aromatic carbon with greater long-term stability. Below this range, the resulting char contains more labile carbon that decomposes faster in soil.</p><p><strong>H/C organic ratio</strong> below 0.4. This is the primary proxy for carbon stability, used by both the European Biochar Certificate (EBC) and the International Biochar Initiative (IBI). Lower ratios indicate more stable carbon.</p><p><strong>Third-party certification.</strong> Independent auditing of feedstock sourcing, production parameters, and product analysis. Not self-reported data.</p><p><strong>Contaminant screening.</strong> Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and heavy metal levels below regulatory thresholds for soil application.</p><p>This quality bar matters because, as we covered <a href=/posts/biochar-quality-credibility-carbon-removal/>this week</a>, the biochar market has historically struggled with quality variance. Climeworks entering the biochar market with its verification standards could raise the floor for the entire pathway.</p><h2 id=supplier-strategy-vs-buyer-strategy>Supplier Strategy vs. Buyer Strategy<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#supplier-strategy-vs-buyer-strategy>#</a></h2><p>The Climeworks move mirrors what is happening on the buyer side. Companies like <a href=/posts/tapestry-climeworks-10-year-cdr-partnership/>Tapestry</a> (DAC + biochar), <a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cur8-carbon-removal-portfolio/>Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1</a> (6 technologies across 7 projects), and <a href=/posts/lego-carbon-removal-portfolio/>LEGO</a> are all building diversified CDR portfolios rather than betting on a single pathway.</p><p>Suppliers face the same logic. A CDR company offering only one pathway faces concentration risk: if that pathway hits a cost wall, a regulatory hurdle, or a scaling bottleneck, the company has no fallback. Multi-pathway suppliers can rebalance their offering as costs, technologies, and buyer preferences shift.</p><p>Climeworks is not pivoting away from DAC. Orca and Mammoth are the core business. Biochar is portfolio extension: a way to serve more buyers, offer blended pricing, and hedge against the risk that DAC costs decline slower than projected.</p><h2 id=the-broader-trend>The Broader Trend<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-broader-trend>#</a></h2><p>Three years ago, CDR companies were defined by their pathway. You were a DAC company, a biochar company, an <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a> company. That framing is breaking down. The market is moving toward portfolio construction on both the supply and demand side.</p><p>Climeworks adding biochar is the same trend as CUR8 and Isometric launching a <a href=/posts/cur8-isometric-2030-portfolio-cdr/>multi-pathway 2030 Portfolio</a>. The question is no longer &ldquo;which pathway wins?&rdquo; but &ldquo;how do you construct a portfolio that balances cost, permanence, scalability, and delivery risk?&rdquo;</p><p>That is a sign of market maturation.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://biochartoday.com>BiocharToday</a>, April 2026.</em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quality-credibility-carbon-removal/>Biochar Quality Is the CDR Field&rsquo;s Credibility Problem</a></li><li><a href=/posts/tapestry-climeworks-10-year-cdr-partnership/>Tapestry Expands Climeworks Partnership: DAC Plus Biochar</a></li><li><a href=/posts/enhanced-weathering-biochar-co-deployment/>Biochar + Basalt: Wageningen Shows Co-Deployment Changes the Weathering Game</a></li><li><a href=/posts/cur8-isometric-2030-portfolio-cdr/>CUR8 and Isometric Launch Joint 2030 Portfolio CDR Offering</a></li></ul>
