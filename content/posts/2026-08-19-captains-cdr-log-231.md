The counterfactual fight between CarbonPlan and Isometric is about to stop being an accounting debate and start being a pricing event. Within 30 to 90 days, expect at least one major durable-removal buyer or registry to publicly restate procurement criteria in a way that separates counterfactual-tight tonnes from counterfactual-loose tonnes. By Q1 2027, I expect a $50 to $150 per tonne price gap to open between the two categories. The mechanism is simple. When a leading registry and a leading independent analyst publicly disagree on whether a category counts as removal at all, buyers who have paid a premium for durability cannot afford ambiguity in their disclosures.

The trigger is [CarbonPlan's direct challenge to Isometric's wastewater alkalinity protocol](https://carbonplan.org/blog/counterfactual-accounting-update), which argues the protocol lets avoided emissions get counted as removal. That is not a technical footnote. It is a category dispute, and category disputes move prices.

**The 30-day window.** The first move will be quiet. Expect due-diligence questionnaires from durable-removal buyers to grow new sections on counterfactual treatment. Watch offtake announcements for language shifts. Deals like the [Senken and Carbonsate 50,000-tonne biomass storage offtake in Namibia](https://biochartoday.com/news/senken-and-carbonsate-partner-on-europes-largest-biomass-storage-offtake-agreement/) set a live per-tonne benchmark for what buyers will pay when the counterfactual is boring, or in the case of biomass burial, physically obvious. That benchmark becomes the anchor against which alkalinity-based credits get quoted at a discount. The leading indicator is not price. It is contract language.

**The 90-day window.** Expect at least one procurement-side actor to publish revised criteria that explicitly reference counterfactual treatment. I will not name which buyer, because none of the sources I was handed today name one. But the pattern is what to watch. The CDRXIV preprint from Nordahl and colleagues, [Accounting Methods Dictate Carbon Removal Credit Integrity and Outcomes](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/514), argues directly that CDR commands a premium over offsets and that blurred accounting erodes that premium. That is the intellectual scaffolding buyers will cite when they tighten their criteria. Glen Peters ([@glenpeters.bsky.social](https://bsky.app/profile/glenpeters.bsky.social/post/3mtgavi2cqk2o)) frames the underlying problem crisply: "The achieved sequestration rate is nearly always lower than the announced rate. At what stage should models include this and adjust costs accordingly?" Buyers are about to answer that question with their checkbooks.

**The 180-day window.** The longer arc is regulatory. The [European Commission's move to integrate permanent removals into the EU ETS](https://www.negative-emissions.org/updates/press-release-european-commission-takes-historic-step-to-integrate-permanent-carbon-removals-into-the-eu-etsnbsp) turns the definition of removal into a legal category with a price attached. If the integration draft specifies a counterfactual standard, the CarbonPlan-Isometric dispute stops being an argument between two organizations and becomes an eligibility test with billions of euros of compliance demand behind it. Julian Smart's Zenodo paper on [why cost reduction alone cannot deliver bankable CDR](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21916904) argues bankability depends on credit-category integrity, not just dollars per tonne. That is the variable moving right now.

**What would falsify this forecast.** If, by end of Q1 2027, no major durable-removal buyer publishes revised procurement language referencing counterfactuals, and the EU ETS draft skips a counterfactual standard, and secondary-market pricing for alkalinity-based credits remains within $25 per tonne of biomass-burial or mineralization credits of similar durability rating, then the market absorbed the CarbonPlan critique without repricing. That is possible. Category disputes sometimes get settled by protocol amendments rather than price gaps. If Isometric revises the wastewater protocol to satisfy CarbonPlan's counterfactual objection within 60 days, the pricing event collapses into a technical footnote.

**The bet.** Six months from today, at least one durable-removal offtake announcement of 10,000 tonnes or more will contain explicit contract language distinguishing its counterfactual treatment from alkalinity-based or process-adjacent credits. That is a specific, checkable claim. If it does not happen, the market handled this dispute more quietly than I expect.

The economics here are straightforward. Buyers paying $300 to $1,000 per tonne for durable removal cannot let a $100 per tonne category get labeled the same way in their annual reports. The willingness-to-pay for durability was always a willingness-to-pay for a defensible counterfactual. That defense just got harder for one category and easier for another. Prices will follow.
