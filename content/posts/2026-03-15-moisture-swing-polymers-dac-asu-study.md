<p>Most direct air capture systems have an energy problem. They need heat — often a lot of it — to release captured CO₂ from their sorbents. That heat costs money and energy, and it&rsquo;s a major reason DAC still runs $400-1,000+ per tonne.</p><p>Moisture-swing sorbents work differently. They absorb CO₂ when dry and release it when wet. No heat required. Just water. It&rsquo;s an idea that&rsquo;s been around for over a decade, pioneered by Klaus Lackner at Arizona State University. But making it work efficiently requires understanding exactly what happens inside these materials at a structural level.</p><p>A new study from ASU, published in <em>Materials Today Chemistry</em>, just delivered that understanding.</p><h2 id=what-they-found>What They Found<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-they-found>#</a></h2><p>Petra Fromme&rsquo;s team at ASU&rsquo;s School of Molecular Sciences performed the first comprehensive structural characterization of two commercially available moisture-swing polymers: Fumasep FAA-3 and IRA-900.</p><p>Using a battery of techniques — X-ray diffraction, electron microscopy, atomic force microscopy — combined with functional measurements of CO₂ and water sorption, they mapped how these materials behave at different scales and humidity levels.</p><p>The key finding: both polymers handle water similarly (suggesting molecular structure controls water movement), but their CO₂ capture performance diverges sharply. IRA-900, the material with larger macropores, captured more CO₂ and captured it faster.</p><p>In other words: <strong>macropore structure governs CO₂ sorption capacity and kinetics.</strong> Bigger pores mean faster, more efficient carbon capture.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>This might sound like a niche materials science result, but it has direct implications for the future cost of DAC.</p><p>Right now, the dominant DAC companies (Climeworks, Carbon Engineering/Oxy, Heirloom) use temperature-swing or chemical processes that require significant energy input. Moisture-swing systems could dramatically cut that energy bill — but only if the sorbent materials can be optimized for capacity and speed.</p><p>Fromme&rsquo;s team just identified the structural variable that matters most. That turns sorbent development from trial-and-error into targeted engineering. You want better DAC performance? Design materials with optimal macropore architecture.</p><p>&ldquo;This work is so important because it shows for the first time the structural characterization of two direct air capture materials with a unique combination of techniques,&rdquo; Fromme said.</p><h2 id=the-broader-dac-materials-race>The Broader DAC Materials Race<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-broader-dac-materials-race>#</a></h2><p>Cheaper DAC probably won&rsquo;t come from building bigger versions of today&rsquo;s plants. It&rsquo;ll come from fundamentally different sorbent materials that capture CO₂ more efficiently, with less energy, and at lower cost to manufacture.</p><p>This study is a step in that direction. By revealing the structural characteristics that control performance, it gives materials scientists a roadmap for designing next-generation moisture-swing sorbents optimized from the atomic level up.</p><p>The lead author, doctoral student Gayathri Yogaganeshan, put it simply: &ldquo;Our research addresses the urgent challenge of removing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by studying materials for low-energy, humidity-activated direct air capture.&rdquo;</p><p>Low-energy DAC at scale would change the economics of carbon removal entirely. We&rsquo;re not there yet — but now we know what to optimize.</p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href="https://www.nanowerk.com/news2/green/newsid=68856.php">Nanowerk</a>, Yogaganeshan et al., Materials Today Chemistry (DOI: 10.1016/j.mtchem.2026.103465), <a href=https://www.enerzine.com/des-materiaux-actives-par-lhumidite-pourraient-rendre-la-capture-du-co%E2%82%82-dans-lair-plus-efficace/183737-2026-03>Enerzine</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/sustaera-dac-breakthrough-3x-cheaper/>Sustaera&rsquo;s DAC Breakthrough: 3× Cheaper</a></li><li><a href=/posts/dacma-hamburg-dac-competence-center/>DACMA Hamburg: Germany&rsquo;s New DAC Competence Center</a></li><li><a href=/posts/500mw-solar-for-stratos-dac/>500 MW Solar for Stratos DAC</a></li></ul>
