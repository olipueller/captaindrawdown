**Carbon Herald just published [Opinion: We Can Turn Biomass Carbon Into A Durable Climate Solution At Scale, But We Need To Get The Rules Right](https://carbonherald.com/biomass-carbon-durable-climate-solution/).**

In an opinion published by Carbon Herald, the author makes the case that biomass-based carbon removal could become a durable climate solution at large scale, while warning that outcomes depend heavily on how standards and rules are written. The piece points to the role plants play in pulling carbon from the atmosphere each year and frames biomass pathways as a meaningful lever for removals. It also references recent activity in the standards space, including Gold Standard's release of new methodologies such as a first biochar standard. The author argues that getting definitions, accounting, and durability requirements right will determine whether biomass carbon delivers real climate value.

_Our take (Context):_ The framing is reasonable: biomass removals live or die on accounting rules, durability assumptions, and leakage treatment. The piece is an opinion, not data, so the substantive test is in the methodology details that standards bodies actually adopt. Worth watching how Gold Standard's biochar criteria handle permanence and feedstock sourcing.
