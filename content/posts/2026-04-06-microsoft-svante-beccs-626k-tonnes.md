<p>Microsoft just signed a 15-year offtake agreement for 626,000 tonnes of carbon removal credits from a BECCS project in Saskatchewan. The deal is with Svante&rsquo;s subsidiary Carbon Alpha and the Meadow Lake Tribal Council (MLTC) — making this one of the largest Indigenous-partnered CDR deals on record.</p><h2 id=the-numbers>The Numbers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-numbers>#</a></h2><p>The North Star project will capture up to <strong>90,000 tonnes of biogenic CO₂ per year</strong> at full capacity. The facility sits alongside the MLTC Bioenergy Centre, which generates renewable power from waste biomass sourced from local forestry operations. Captured CO₂ gets transported and permanently stored in geological formations underground.</p><p>That&rsquo;s a fully integrated chain: waste biomass in, renewable energy plus permanent carbon storage out.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>This deal is a textbook example of what makes BECCS credible when done right:</p><p><strong>Feedstock matters.</strong> The project uses genuine waste biomass from forestry operations — not purpose-grown crops competing with food production. That&rsquo;s the difference between BECCS that adds up and BECCS that just moves emissions around.</p><p><strong>Offtake unlocks financing.</strong> A 15-year commitment from Microsoft provides the revenue certainty needed to reach a final investment decision. This is the flywheel the CDR market needs: credible buyer commits early, project gets financed, tonnes get delivered. Without advance purchase agreements at this scale, most large CDR projects stay on paper.</p><p><strong>Indigenous partnership sets a template.</strong> The Meadow Lake Tribal Council isn&rsquo;t just a landowner here — they&rsquo;re a co-developer through the Bioenergy Centre. As CDR scales, the projects that get community partnership right will move faster through permitting and build more durable social license.</p><h2 id=the-bigger-picture>The Bigger Picture<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-bigger-picture>#</a></h2><p>Microsoft is building one of the most diversified CDR portfolios in the world. They&rsquo;re buying DAC from Climeworks, biochar from multiple suppliers, <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a>, and now large-scale BECCS. Their approach is method-agnostic and volume-hungry — exactly the buyer profile the market needs to mature.</p><p>Construction on North Star is expected later this decade, with operations targeting 2029. Svante will fund development until the final investment decision.</p><p>For anyone tracking BECCS specifically: this project, combined with Svante&rsquo;s <a href=https://www.captaindrawdown.com/posts/svante-beccs-paper-mill-500k-tonnes/>US paper mill initiative</a> targeting 500,000+ tonnes annually, puts Svante&rsquo;s BECCS pipeline well above 1 million tonnes per year. That&rsquo;s DAC-scale ambition from the BECCS side.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://carbonherald.com/microsoft-signs-major-beccs-carbon-removal-deal-with-svante/>Carbon Herald</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/svante-acquires-carbon-alpha-beccs/>Svante Acquires Carbon Alpha: BECCS Meets First Nations Partnership</a></li><li><a href=/posts/canada-first-government-cdr-procurement/>Canada Becomes First Government to Buy CDR Credits</a></li><li><a href=/posts/microsoft-liferaft-biggest-us-biochar-deal/>Microsoft Just Signed the Biggest Biochar Deal in US History</a></li><li><a href=/posts/svante-beccs-paper-mill-500k-tonnes/>A US Paper Mill Wants to Capture Half a Million Tonnes of CO₂ Per Year</a></li></ul>
