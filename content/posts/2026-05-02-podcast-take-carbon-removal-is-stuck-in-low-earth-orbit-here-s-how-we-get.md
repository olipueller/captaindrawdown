> Take on a podcast episode from **The Carbon Curve**, originally published Thu, 30 Ap.
> Listen: <https://carboncurve.substack.com/p/carbon-removal-is-stuck-in-low-earth>

On [The Carbon Curve, episode 64](https://carboncurve.substack.com/p/carbon-removal-is-stuck-in-low-earth), Na'im Merchant sits down with Julio Friedmann, Chief Scientist at Carbon Direct, to unpack a new Carbon Direct report titled *CDR 2.0: Five Pillars of Successful Project Deployment and Delivery*. The framing: carbon dioxide removal got to orbit on first-of-a-kind tonnes and Frontier-style offtakes, but the next stage — infrastructure-scale projects with bankable contracts — needs a different operating manual. Friedmann walks through what buyers actually want now, why project assurance is becoming the gating factor, and where the talent and standards bottlenecks really sit.

The substantive pivot in the conversation is from "build a market" to "de-risk a project." Friedmann's argument, per the show notes, is that CDR 1.0 buyers tolerated technology risk in exchange for portfolio learning; CDR 2.0 buyers — and especially the lenders behind them — won't sign multi-year offtakes without project-finance-grade diligence on delivery, monitoring, and counterparty quality. The five pillars cover that gap: project assurance, talent, industry-led standards, bankable offtakes, and (per the report title) deployment discipline. The point on project management talent is worth flagging — it's a constraint that doesn't show up in techno-economic models but is currently rate-limiting in mineralization and biomass pathways alike. The Quebec Surficial Mineralization Hub gets a nod as a concrete example of where this plays out.

For context, Carbon Direct's diligence work sits behind a meaningful share of the [Frontier](https://frontierclimate.com/) and [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/corporate-responsibility/sustainability/carbon-removal-program) portfolios, which makes Friedmann's read on what buyers want non-hypothetical. The "bankable offtake" thread connects to ongoing work by the [Advance Carbon Removal Coalition](https://www.advancecarbonremoval.org/) on standardizing contract terms, and to the [2026 State of the Voluntary Carbon Market](https://www.carbon-direct.com/) report Carbon Direct released alongside this episode. Practitioners who've followed the [debate over project-level measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) rigor](https://carboncurve.substack.com/) and the gap between announced and delivered tonnes on [CDR.fyi](https://www.cdr.fyi/) will recognize the diagnosis: the industry's announced pipeline is large, the delivered pipeline is small, and closing that gap is now a procurement and project-management problem more than a science problem.

Useful for: developers preparing to raise project finance, buyer-side diligence teams writing offtake terms, and policy folks thinking about how procurement standards translate into bankability. Skip if you're looking for pathway-level technical detail — this is a markets-and-deployment conversation.
