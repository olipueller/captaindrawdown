**The Carbon Curve just published [Introducing Carbon Curve Intelligence](https://carboncurve.substack.com/p/introducing-carbon-curve-intelligence).**

The Carbon Curve reports that Na'im Merchant, who tracks the sector at Carbon Removal Canada, has launched Carbon Curve Intelligence at intelligence.carboncurve.co after initially building it as a personal tool. The system checks roughly 200 sources each day, including news sites, company newsrooms, government pages, podcasts and newsletters, plus new research literature. Rather than republishing articles, it extracts structured fields such as buyer, seller, tonnage, pathway and date, and stores each as a dated record with a link to the original source. The database currently holds thousands of records, among them hundreds of deals and policies across dozens of countries. Search and saving are free; a Pro tier adds features, chiefly a plugin that connects the database to AI assistants like Claude or ChatGPT. The tool does not rate or rank companies, and most records are extracted by AI and published automatically, with a feedback form for corrections.

_Our take (Useful):_ A structured, source-linked record of CDR transactions and policy is genuinely useful in a sector where information is scattered and Google misses a lot. The main thing to watch is data quality: records are machine-extracted and auto-published, so errors in tonnage or counterparties will surface before anyone reviews them. The piece also does not explain how contracted, delivered and announced volumes are distinguished, which matters for anyone using it for diligence.
