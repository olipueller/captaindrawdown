## 🌍 ERW Reality Check: Up to 1.1 Billion Tonnes Per Year by 2100

A new modelling study led by Chuan Liao at Cornell University puts a realistic ceiling on enhanced rock weathering's potential. Rather than the often-cited theoretical max of 5 Gt/yr, the team incorporated actual farmer adoption rates, regional climate, and supply-chain maturity to arrive at **350–750 Mt CO₂/yr by 2050** and **700 Mt–1.1 Gt CO₂/yr by 2100**.

Key insight: Asia, Latin America, and sub-Saharan Africa will eventually surpass Europe and North America — higher temperatures and precipitation speed up weathering, and farmers there could earn more per tonne of rock spread. A separate paper from Marcus Schiedung at Germany's Thünen Institute sounds a note of caution: dry soils can slow weathering by a factor of 25.

For the ERW sector, this is validation with asterisks — the pathway is real but deeply dependent on regional conditions and MRV rigor.

**Source:** [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2517484-spreading-crushed-rock-on-farms-could-absorb-1-billion-tonnes-of-co2/)

## 🌊 Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement: First Quantified Open-Water Results

The Loc-Ness project (Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution) presented provisional results at the Ocean Sciences Meeting 2026 in Glasgow. After dispersing 65,000L of sodium hydroxide over 1 km² in the Gulf of Maine, the team measured **2–10 tonnes of additional CO₂ drawdown in just 4 days**, with models suggesting up to 50 tonnes over a year. Crucially, they found no measurable impact on phytoplankton, zooplankton, or fish/lobster larvae.

This is a big MRV win for ocean CDR — demonstrating that small-scale OAE can be "engineered, tracked and monitored with high precision." Microsoft and Google have already signed OAE agreements with Ebb Carbon.

**Source:** [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/ocean-carbon-storage-boosted-by-alkalinity-enhancement-finds-open-water-trial/4023027.article)

## ⚖️ US Policy: The Endangerment Finding Is Gone

The fallout from the Trump EPA's Feb 12 rescission of the 2009 GHG endangerment finding continues. Called "the single largest deregulatory action in U.S. history," it removes the legal basis for regulating CO₂ as a pollutant under the Clean Air Act. Multiple environmental groups have filed federal lawsuits.

For CDR, this matters: weaker US emissions regulation weakens one of the demand drivers for carbon removal credits. Separately, 125+ civil society groups are urging Congress to dismantle the 45Q tax credit, arguing most captured CO₂ ends up in enhanced oil recovery rather than permanent storage.

**Sources:** [Hartford Courant](https://www.courant.com/2026/03/02/ct-environmental-groups-are-sounding-the-alarm-after-epa-guts-critical-regulation-heres-why/) | [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/45q-tax-credit-faces-growing-backlash-over-oil-linked-carbon-capture/)

## 🌏 APACdr: Asia-Pacific Gets Its Own CDR Initiative

The Carbon Business Council and Emerald Climate launched **APACdr**, a Singapore-anchored working group to accelerate carbon removal across Asia-Pacific. The region holds 60% of the global population and produces about half of global emissions. The inaugural session in January brought 26 organizations together. Plans include demand aggregation mechanisms, blended pricing, and an APACdr Summit during Ecosperity Week (May 18–21, 2026).

**Source:** [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/the-carbon-business-council-emerald-climate-back-removals-in-asia-pacific-with-new-apacdr-initiative/)

## 🏗️ DAC Meets Construction: Carbon-Negative Building Aggregates

Mission Zero Technologies' electrochemical DAC (electricity-only, no heat needed) has been integrated with OCO Technology's carbonation process at a Norfolk facility to produce **carbon-negative building aggregates** — turning waste and atmospheric CO₂ into construction materials. The project won the C2I 2025 Energy award and represents one of the most promising CO₂ utilization pathways.

**Source:** [The Engineer](https://www.theengineer.co.uk/content/in-depth/c2i-2025-energy-winner-carbon-capture-for-sustainable-construction)

## 🌡️ Market Tone

**Science bullish, policy bearish.** ERW modelling matures, OAE delivers first quantified results, and voluntary carbon market projections stay aggressive (31% CAGR). But the US regulatory environment is turning hostile — the endangerment finding rescission removes foundational CO₂ regulation, and the 45Q backlash could threaten the main US financial incentive for carbon capture. The APACdr launch signals Asia-Pacific stepping up, which may partially offset US pullback. Takeaway for CDR investors: the long-term thesis strengthens on science, but near-term US policy risk has increased significantly.
