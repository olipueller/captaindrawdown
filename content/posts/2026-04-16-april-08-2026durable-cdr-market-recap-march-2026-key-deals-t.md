March 2026 was a monster month for durable CDR contracting. Roughly 1.51 million tonnes of carbon removal were contracted in a single month, driven by two massive biochar-related deals and a string of smaller but significant agreements involving Google, Boeing, and others.
## Why it matters
To put 1.51 million tonnes in perspective: the entire durable CDR market delivered only a few million tonnes cumulatively over recent years. A single month approaching that scale signals that corporate procurement is shifting from pilot-sized purchases to industrial-volume commitments. The deals also reveal which CDR pathways are attracting the biggest checks right now, and biochar and pyrolysis are clearly leading.
## The big deals
**Liferaft and Microsoft** signed a 10-year offtake agreement for 1 million tonnes of CDR, facilitated by intermediary Supercritical. The credits will come from Liferaft's large-scale pyrolysis facilities in Iowa and Illinois. This is the kind of long-duration, high-volume contract the CDR market has been waiting for. Microsoft continues to be the single most aggressive corporate buyer in the field, and locking in a decade-long commitment gives Liferaft the revenue certainty needed to build and operate major infrastructure.
**Altitude and Empacar** agreed to a 305,000-tonne purchase. Empacar is a Bolivian sustainable packaging company that is building a biochar facility in Santa Cruz targeting roughly 70,000 tonnes of CO₂ removal per year, with credits to be issued on Puro.earth or equivalent methodologies. This deal is notable for channeling significant CDR investment into the Global South.
**Google and AMP** struck a deal for 200,000 tonnes of CDR. AMP is a Colorado-based recycling company, and the purchase will fund the addition of biochar production capacity to their existing U.S. recycling operations. It's an interesting model: bolting carbon removal onto existing waste-processing infrastructure.
**Boeing and Carbonfuture** signed a multi-year agreement for at least 40,000 tonnes. Carbonfuture will supply credits from a diversified portfolio initially sourced from four biochar projects across the Global South.
**Mercedes-AMG F1** expanded its CDR portfolio through CUR8, investing in 18,900 tonnes across nature-based, hybrid, and engineered projects spanning five durable CDR methods in six countries.
## Smaller but significant signals
A few deals stood out not for their volume but for what they represent structurally.
The first publicly announced transaction for credits under the EU's Carbon Removal and Carbon Farming (CRCF) framework was completed. Adyen and Nasdaq purchased credits from Stockholm Exergi in a deal structured by ClimeFi. This matters because it shows the EU regulatory framework is starting to generate real market activity.
DAC (direct air capture) company Sirona Technologies completed a multi-year offtake facilitated by Patch, with credits from its modular DAC projects supporting deployment of its next commercial facility. Kenya-based DAC developer Octavia Carbon also secured an offtake agreement facilitated by Carbon Direct, though volume and pricing weren't disclosed.
## Partnerships and procurement pipelines
ClimeFi launched its Beyond 2030 RFP, targeting procurement of 100,000 to 500,000 tonnes of durable CDR on behalf of multiple corporate buyers. The RFP requires permanence of at least 200 years. That's a serious volume signal for suppliers.
Climeworks and Lithos Carbon partnered to bring verified enhanced rock weathering credits to market, building on a relationship dating back to 2023, with a target of 3.5 million tonnes over more than a decade. This is a notable cross-pathway collaboration between a DAC leader and an enhanced weathering company.
On the project side, Inherit Carbon Solutions commenced operations on what it calls the world's first BECCS (bioenergy with carbon capture and storage) project capturing biogenic CO₂ from biogas, with liquefied CO₂ now being permanently stored 2,600 meters below the North Sea.
## Financing momentum
Canada emerged as a focal point for CDR financing. The Advance Carbon Removal Coalition launched with backing from the Canadian government, Carbon Removal Canada, BMO, RBC, Shopify, Vancity, NorthX, and ClimeFi, aiming to mobilize $100 million for Canadian CDR by 2030. Separately, the Canadian government issued a tender for CDR credit suppliers as part of its commitment to purchase at least CAD 10 million in carbon removal services by 2030.
Sweden's Energy Agency launched two funding calls totaling about €29.4 million for research and projects focused on capturing and permanently storing biogenic or atmospheric greenhouse gases. Frontier launched its 2026 Innovation Program targeting ocean alkalinity enhancement, surficial mineralization, and open-system MRV (measurement, reporting, and verification).
## Caveats
The headline number of 1.51 million tonnes is dominated by a single deal. Remove the Liferaft-Microsoft agreement and you're at roughly 500,000 tonnes, which is still strong but tells a different story about market breadth. Biochar and pyrolysis account for the vast majority of contracted volume, which raises concentration risk if those pathways face durability or verification challenges.
These are contracted tonnes, not delivered tonnes. Multi-year offtake agreements depend on facilities being built, operated, and verified. The gap between contracted and delivered CDR remains one of the field's biggest open questions.
It's also worth repeating: CDR exists to address hard-to-abate residual emissions. Even a month this strong doesn't change the math. Fossil fuel phase-out remains the primary lever for climate action. Carbon removal handles what's left over, not what we choose not to cut.
