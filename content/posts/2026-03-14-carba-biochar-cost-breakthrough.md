<p>The single biggest thing holding back carbon removal? Cost. Direct air capture runs $400–1,000 per ton. <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>Enhanced weathering</a> is cheaper but slower. Biochar sits in a sweet spot — and a Minnesota startup just made it sweeter.</p><p><strong>Carba</strong>, co-founded by University of Minnesota Distinguished McKnight Professor Paul Dauenhauer, has developed a proprietary process that converts plant-based waste into biochar stable enough to lock carbon underground for over 1,000 years. Their target: $100–200 per ton of CO₂ removed.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>Most biochar companies pyrolyze biomass. That&rsquo;s not new. What Carba brings is a university-backed engineering approach to <em>cost reduction at scale</em>. The partnership with the University of Minnesota isn&rsquo;t just a branding exercise — it&rsquo;s serious process engineering aimed at making biochar CDR economically competitive with avoided emissions.</p><p>NPR&rsquo;s Climate Cast <a href=https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/03/13/how-a-minnesota-startup-is-taking-carbon-out-of-the-atmosphere>featured Carba this week</a>, with Dauenhauer explaining the approach: take agricultural and forestry waste that would otherwise decompose and release CO₂, convert it into a carbon-rich solid, and bury it. The carbon stays put for millennia.</p><h2 id=the-100ton-question>The $100/ton Question<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-100ton-question>#</a></h2><p>At $200/ton, Carba is already selling carbon removal credits to buyers. Their roadmap targets $100/ton — a price point that could unlock demand far beyond the current pool of corporate early-movers like Microsoft and Shopify.</p><p>For context: Boeing just locked in 40,000 tons of biochar credits. The biochar market is <a href=https://crypto.newswireservice.net/press-releases/biochar-market-projected-to-grow-from-usd-876-88-million-in-2025-to-usd-3640-39-million-by-2035/>projected to grow</a> from $877 million in 2025 to $3.6 billion by 2035.</p><h2 id=the-cdi-angle>The CDI Angle<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-cdi-angle>#</a></h2><p>CDI has <a href=https://www.carbon-drawdown.de>several biochar companies in its portfolio</a> — from SYNCRAFT&rsquo;s power-generating pyrolysis to Happy Ground&rsquo;s tropical biochar in Thailand. The pathway works. The question has always been whether the economics can scale. Carba&rsquo;s university-backed engineering approach is exactly the kind of innovation that could answer it.</p><p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> $100/ton biochar CDR isn&rsquo;t science fiction. It&rsquo;s a Minnesota lab working on it right now.</p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href=https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/03/13/how-a-minnesota-startup-is-taking-carbon-out-of-the-atmosphere>MPR News / NPR Climate Cast</a>, <a href=https://carba.com/press/join-the-minneapolis-buyers-club>Carba</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/green-carbon-india-biochar-partnership/>Japan-India Biochar Alliance: Industrial-Scale CDR Comes to Asia</a></li><li><a href=/posts/west-virginia-biochar-tax-credit/>West Virginia Bets on Biochar: Coal State Stacks Tax Credits for Carbon Removal</a></li></ul>
