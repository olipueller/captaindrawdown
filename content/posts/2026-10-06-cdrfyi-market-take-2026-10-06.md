[CDR.fyi's report](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/cdr-monthly-recap-july-2026) for July 2026 puts durable CDR contracted in the month at around 22,000 tonnes. In the same recap, CDR.fyi notes that Frontier Infrastructure Holdings and Carbonfuture made 750,000 ethanol-based BECCS credits from Project Sprint available to global buyers under Puro.earth. One deal's worth of inventory is roughly 34 times the entire month's contracted volume. That gap between what's on the shelf and what's being bought is the story I'd take from July.

Supply is no longer the bottleneck it was framed as a few years ago, at least for some pathways. Ethanol BECCS is the cheapest durable capture available because fermentation produces a nearly pure CO₂ stream. Inventory at that scale shifts the pressure onto procurement. CDR.fyi lists the month's activity as led by Gevo, Nasdaq, Vaulted Deep, BlackRock, Lithos and Shopify. That list mixes suppliers and buyers, and it spans ethanol BECCS, subsurface biomass storage and enhanced weathering. The demand side still leans on a familiar set of corporate and financial names. Shopify is a long-standing Frontier member. Nasdaq and BlackRock bring capital-markets infrastructure rather than pure offset demand. The PayPal–3Degrees portfolio, also reported by CDR.fyi, follows the same pattern of blending biochar (Heartyculture in India) with Gevo's North Dakota BECCS.

The project pipeline in the recap shows how uneven scaling is across methods. CDR.fyi reports Ucaneo's new Berlin DAC plant at 150 tonnes per year, billed as Germany's largest. DBG Energy's planned Belize biochar facility targets 50,000 tonnes annually. Nuada's BECCS unit with IDEX is rated up to 10,000 tonnes. ProClime and Cadira's $10 million for seven Indian biochar plants targets about 35,000 credits a year. Biochar and BECCS are moving in five-figure tonnage blocks per site, while electrochemical DAC is still proving its process at the hundreds-of-tonnes level. That ratio reflects energy and capital intensity. It isn't a judgment on the technology's ceiling.

On prior periods, the July recap text doesn't state earlier monthly totals. I won't characterize 22,000 tonnes as up or down without that data, and I'd point readers to CDR.fyi's portal for the series. What I can say is that monthly durable-CDR figures tend to be lumpy. A single large offtake can swing a month by an order of magnitude, so one month's number says little about the trend.

My caveat is that "available" and "contracted" are different states. The 750,000 Project Sprint credits are an offer, not a sale. The annual capacities quoted for Belize, Kogeban and the Indian biochar network are design targets, not delivered tonnes. The 700+ CORCs Inherit Carbon Solutions issued from Norway's VEAS facility, which CDR.fyi describes as Europe's first certified BECCS credits, are the only figures in this batch that represent verified, issued removal. The other figures describe capacity that still has to be built or sold.

The compliance plumbing CDR.fyi flags will decide whether the overhang clears. That includes Climeworks' CRCF- and CORSIA-aligned portfolio and Evero's application under the UK's Greenhouse Gas Removal Business Model. I'll be watching whether those channels turn 750,000-tonne offers into signed tonnes over the coming months.
