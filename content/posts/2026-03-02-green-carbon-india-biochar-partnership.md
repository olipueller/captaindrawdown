<p>While most CDR headlines focus on North America and Europe, Asia is quietly building serious carbon removal capacity. The latest example: Japan&rsquo;s Green Carbon Inc. has partnered with Ahmedabad-based Excellent Enfab to deploy industrial-scale biochar production in India.</p><h2 id=the-deal>The Deal<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-deal>#</a></h2><p>Two new pyrolysis plants will be built in Gujarat and West Bengal. Feedstock comes from locally sourced agricultural residues — peanut shells, flower stems, bamboo — the kind of waste that would otherwise decompose and release CO₂ back into the atmosphere.</p><p>The target: <strong>18,000 tonnes of CDR credits per year</strong>, sustained over at least 10 years, for a total of 180,000 tonnes. The credits will be certified under the <a href=https://isometric.com/>Isometric</a> standard, which requires rigorous lifecycle accounting and permanent carbon storage verification.</p><p>The first plant is scheduled for commercial operations in Q2 2026.</p><h2 id=why-it-matters>Why It Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-it-matters>#</a></h2><p>India faces a dual challenge that makes biochar uniquely compelling. Over the past 70 years, average soil organic carbon content has dropped from 1% to just 0.3% — a critical level. Applying biochar to agricultural land doesn&rsquo;t just store carbon; it rebuilds degraded soil, improves water retention, and reduces fertilizer dependency.</p><p>The partnership also produces wood vinegar as a byproduct, which serves as a natural pesticide alternative. This isn&rsquo;t just carbon removal — it&rsquo;s a circular economy play.</p><h2 id=the-bigger-picture>The Bigger Picture<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-bigger-picture>#</a></h2><p>Green Carbon&rsquo;s strategy extends across Asia — Thailand, Australia, South America. Their portfolio includes rice paddy credits, forest conservation, mangrove planting, and cattle methane reduction alongside biochar.</p><p>CDI has written extensively about biochar at different scales, featuring <a href=https://www.carbon-drawdown.de/blog/2026-2-9-how-to-make-biochar-at-different-scales-large-and-small-ft-cotierra-amp-syncraft>portfolio companies Cotierra and SYNCRAFT</a>. The approach in India — using local waste streams and 50 TPD industrial pyrolysis — represents yet another model for scaling. Biochar&rsquo;s strength is precisely this adaptability: small-scale artisanal production in Thailand, gasification combined heat and power in Austria, and now industrial pyrolysis fed by agricultural waste in India.</p><p>Biochar already delivers over 90% of commercially traded permanent CDR credits globally. With 40% of this year&rsquo;s quality supply already contracted, projects like this matter.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://green-carbon.co.jp/en/excellent-enfab-incorporation/>Green Carbon Inc.</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Goes All-In on Carbon Removal — 7 New Projects Across 6 Pathways</a></li><li><a href=/posts/boeing-carbonfuture-40k-tonnes/>Boeing Bets Big on Biochar — 40,000 Tonnes of Carbon Removal via Carbonfuture</a></li></ul>
