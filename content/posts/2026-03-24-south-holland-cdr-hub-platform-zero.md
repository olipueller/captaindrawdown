<p>While the US spent 2025 freezing $3.5 billion in DAC hub funding, the Netherlands quietly started building the kind of CDR infrastructure that actually scales.</p><p><a href=https://www.seao2.com/blog/accelerating-carbon-dioxide-removal-in-south-holland-with-platform-zero>Platform Zero</a> just organized a CDR strategy session in South Holland with an attendance list that tells you everything about how Europe does industrial policy differently. At the table: Skytree (DAC), SeaO2 (ocean alkalinity enhancement), TNO (the Netherlands&rsquo; heavyweight national research organization), Provincie Zuid-Holland, Gemeente Rotterdam, the Port of Rotterdam, InnovationQuarter, the Netherlands Enterprise Agency, Paebbl, Carbyon, everox, Zero Emission Fuels, EBN B.V., and Division Q.</p><p>Government. Research. Startups. Port infrastructure. In one room. Designing together.</p><h2 id=the-fieldlab-model>The fieldlab model<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-fieldlab-model>#</a></h2><p>They&rsquo;re not building a single facility — they&rsquo;re building an ecosystem. Platform Zero calls it a &ldquo;Fieldlab,&rdquo; organized around four pillars:</p><ol><li><strong>Defining success</strong> for regional CDR (what does &ldquo;working&rdquo; look like?)</li><li><strong>Identifying barriers</strong> — regulatory, technical, permitting</li><li><strong>Mapping infrastructure</strong> — what exists, what&rsquo;s needed, where it connects</li><li><strong>Building partnerships</strong> — who does what, and who pays</li></ol><p>A key insight from the session: combining CCU (carbon utilization — making stuff with CO₂) and CCS (carbon storage — putting it underground) isn&rsquo;t optional. You need both revenue streams to make CDR economics work at the regional level. Utilization creates near-term cash flow; storage creates long-term climate impact.</p><h2 id=why-the-netherlands-is-perfect-for-this>Why the Netherlands is perfect for this<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-the-netherlands-is-perfect-for-this>#</a></h2><p>Rotterdam has the largest port in Europe. TNO is a world-class applied research institution. The country already has a dense pipeline network from decades of natural gas infrastructure. And the startup ecosystem is stacked — Skytree, Carbyon, SeaO2, and Paebbl all operate within driving distance.</p><p>South Holland already has the hard infrastructure. The fieldlab is about building the soft infrastructure: regulatory clarity, shared standards, pipeline access agreements, coordinated permitting.</p><h2 id=the-contrast-with-the-us>The contrast with the US<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-contrast-with-the-us>#</a></h2><p>The American approach to CDR hubs was: announce a mega-program, pick a few winners, spend years in environmental review, then have the next administration freeze the funding. The Dutch approach: get everyone in a room, design the system together, start testing in an existing industrial cluster, iterate.</p><p>It&rsquo;s less dramatic. It also works.</p><p>Europe isn&rsquo;t going to out-subsidize the US on CDR. But it might out-organize it. The fieldlab model — test, iterate, scale within a functioning ecosystem — is how you build industrial capacity without betting everything on one political cycle.</p><p>The CDR sector doesn&rsquo;t just need money. It needs places where all the pieces connect. South Holland is building one.</p>
