<p>Wednesday&rsquo;s five stories span policy, preservation, forests, robotics, and agriculture — and the throughline is that carbon removal keeps getting more real, more diverse, and less dependent on any single approach or government.</p><h2 id=what-we-covered-today>What We Covered Today<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-we-covered-today>#</a></h2><p><strong><a href=/posts/canada-government-cdr-credits-purchase/>Canada Creates the First National CDR Procurement Program</a></strong> — While Washington cancels DAC hub funding, Ottawa launched a dedicated procurement program for carbon dioxide removal credits. The $7M CAD budget is modest. The mechanism isn&rsquo;t. This is the first time any national government has directly purchased CDR credits — not tax credits, not subsidies, actual procurement. The signal to the market: sovereign demand exists. Watch for other governments to follow within 12 months.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/ctrl-s-saving-dac-startup-ip/>Ctrl-S: Saving DAC&rsquo;s Hard-Won Knowledge from the Funding Winter</a></strong> — DAC investment has crashed 60%+ from its peak. Startups are folding. And with them goes years of hard-won engineering knowledge — the &ldquo;negative learnings&rdquo; that tell the next generation what <em>doesn&rsquo;t</em> work. Ctrl-S is acquiring IP, experimental data, and institutional memory from distressed CDR companies before it&rsquo;s lost forever. The most valuable asset in a down market isn&rsquo;t cheap equity. It&rsquo;s data that cost tens of millions to generate.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/old-growth-forests-carbon-storage-sweden/>Old-Growth Forests Store 72% More Carbon Than Managed Ones</a></strong> — A landmark study in <em>Science</em> compared old-growth and managed boreal forests in Sweden. The result: unmanaged forests store 72% more carbon, and the majority of that difference is underground in soil — not in the trees themselves. The implications for BECCS (bioenergy with carbon capture) are uncomfortable: if harvesting forests releases massive soil carbon stores, the net climate math might not work the way proponents claim.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/arca-climate-rovers-mining-waste-cdr/>Autonomous Rovers + Mining Waste = Carbon Removal</a></strong> — Vancouver&rsquo;s Arca Climate is deploying autonomous rovers to churn mining tailings for enhanced weathering, using microwave activation to boost mineral reactivity. Their 18-month pilot showed 10–20× more CO₂ absorption compared to passive approaches. The tech stack is wild — autonomous vehicles, real-time geochemistry monitoring, microwave treatment — but the logic is sound: mining waste is already excavated, already crushed, and already a liability. Turning it into a carbon removal asset is elegant.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/biochar-livestock-ghg-emissions-meta-study/>Biochar Cuts Agricultural Emissions by Up to 83%</a></strong> — A massive meta-analysis of 78 peer-reviewed studies found biochar reduces agricultural greenhouse gas emissions across the board: CO₂ by 24%, methane by 36%, nitrous oxide by 39%. High-temperature biochar at large application rates hit the 83% figure. The dual benefit — permanent carbon sink <em>plus</em> active emissions reduction — makes biochar&rsquo;s economic case stronger than almost any other CDR pathway.</p><h2 id=stories-we-didnt-cover-but-you-should-know-about>Stories We Didn&rsquo;t Cover (But You Should Know About)<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#stories-we-didnt-cover-but-you-should-know-about>#</a></h2><p><strong>Microsoft and Liferaft finalize 1 million CRU biochar deal</strong> — Microsoft&rsquo;s CDR procurement machine keeps churning. After last week&rsquo;s ~$1B Vaulted Deep deal for geological storage, they&rsquo;ve now locked in one million carbon removal units with US biochar company Liferaft. The 10-year agreement makes it one of the largest biochar offtake deals ever signed. Microsoft isn&rsquo;t just buying CDR — it&rsquo;s building a diversified portfolio across geological, ocean, biochar, and enhanced weathering pathways. No other buyer comes close.</p><p><strong>Biochar on abandoned cropland could unlock low-cost removal</strong> — Research flagged by multiple sources suggests abandoned agricultural land could be a sweet spot for biochar deployment: no competition with food production, already-degraded soils that benefit most from amendment, and marginal land that&rsquo;s cheap to access. It&rsquo;s the kind of unglamorous, high-potential application that actually scales.</p><p><strong>Japan&rsquo;s first electric-powered DAC agricultural pilot</strong> — Japan launched its first demonstration using electrically-powered direct air capture to co-capture CO₂ and water for greenhouse agriculture. Simultaneously addressing water scarcity and carbon removal in agricultural settings. A reminder that Asia&rsquo;s CDR innovation is accelerating fast and English-language media consistently undercounts it.</p><p><strong>Asia-Pacific CDR policy accelerating</strong> — From India&rsquo;s Prakriti carbon market to Japan&rsquo;s DAC pilots to Southeast Asian nature-based programs, the APAC region is moving from discussion to deployment. The next CDR gigaton won&rsquo;t come from North America alone.</p><h2 id=the-pattern>The Pattern<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-pattern>#</a></h2><p>Today&rsquo;s mix tells a clear story about where CDR is heading in 2026. Government procurement is emerging (Canada), the sector is maturing enough to worry about institutional memory (Ctrl-S), natural systems are getting serious quantification (old-growth forests), hardware innovation is getting weirder and more practical (Arca), and biochar continues to stack evidence as the most versatile pathway in the portfolio.</p><p>The Microsoft-Liferaft deal — their second major CDR contract in two days — reinforces what we flagged yesterday: one company&rsquo;s climate procurement budget shouldn&rsquo;t have to substitute for industrial policy. But until governments catch up to Canada&rsquo;s lead, that&rsquo;s the world we&rsquo;re in.</p><p><em>Published by <a href=https://www.captaindrawdown.com>CaptainDrawdown</a> — on a mission to speed up negative emissions.</em></p>
