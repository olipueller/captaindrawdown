The number that stopped me: 30-50% swings in 10-year CO2 drawdown efficiency over tens to hundreds of kilometres of coastline. That comes from Yinghuan Xie, Paul Spence, Stuart Corney and Lennart Bach at the University of Tasmania, whose preprint [Global assessment of marine CO2 removal efficiency in the coastal ocean](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11414863/v1) was posted October 20, 2026. Two companion pieces landed the same month: a four-model overshoot study from Anusha Sathyanadh, Jörg Schwinger, Helene Muri and colleagues ([discussion started October 22, 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2026-5784)), and Chiara Ciscato and co-authors' seasonal analysis of European coastal OAE in *Earth System Dynamics* ([published October 7, 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-17-1513-2026)). Read together, they answer the same question at three scales: when you perturb seawater chemistry, how much atmospheric CO2 actually follows?

**Where the field stood**

OAE is among the most actively studied marine CDR approaches, and everyone agrees the ocean does the heavy lifting of equilibration over months to decades across basin scales — too big to measure, so models carry the burden. Zhou et al. (2025) mapped global OAE efficiency at 1° resolution; He and Tyka (2023) and Palmiéri and Yool (2024) established that roughly half of the induced uptake happens far from the injection site. The constraint has always been compute: conventional simulations are expensive enough that deployment regions get modelled as 100-1000 km blobs, and multi-model coupled assessments have been rare.

**What's new**

Xie et al. sidestep the compute wall. Their "virtual absorbers" are Lagrangian particles carrying a DIC deficit that decays via gas exchange whenever the particle sits in the mixed layer; everything else — currents, wind, sea ice, carbonate sensitivity — is read from existing model fields. Benchmarked against CESM2 at 1°, the method lands within ±8% mean absolute error; against ACCESS-OM2 at 0.1°, within ±4%. The cost drops by 2-6 orders of magnitude — a 10-year, 0.1° single-site assessment that would run USD 30,000-60,000 conventionally becomes essentially free. That let them assess 149,521 coastal sites, four seasons each, at 4-12 km spacing.

The map is the payoff. (Sub)tropical coasts generally exceed 80% efficiency within a decade. Antarctica, parts of the North Atlantic, the Mediterranean, Japan, Australia south of 20°S, New Zealand, the Gulf of Oman and the Canary Islands are weaker — and heterogeneous. Hokkaido, the Canaries, Tasmania and the Antarctic Peninsula show 30-50% efficiency differences between neighbouring stretches, mostly because some nearshore water-mass pathways dive below the mixed layer and carry the deficit into the interior. Importantly, many low-efficiency trajectories *plateau* rather than slowly catch up, so the loss likely persists beyond 10 years. The authors built an interactive site browser and archived code on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23071881).

Sathyanadh et al. zoom out to the whole Earth system. Four emission-driven ESMs (NorESM, AWI-ESM, EC-Earth, FOCI-MOPS) deploy 203 Gt Ca(OH)2 in the EEZs of the US, Europe and China from 2040-2099 — a scenario grounded in cement-industry spare capacity and an LCA of ocean liming — plus a matched direct ocean carbon removal case. Global OAE efficiency in the final decade spans 0.53-0.69 mol CO2 per mol alkalinity; DOCR 0.66-0.94. Only 42-57% (OAE) and 49-65% (DOCR) of uptake occurs inside the deployment regions. Atmospheric CO2 falls 7.6 ± 1.6 ppm (OAE) and 6.5 ± 1.6 ppm (DOCR); temperature effects are 0.04-0.07 °C and not statistically separable from internal variability. The spread is driven by circulation — NorESM mixes alkalinity to 800 m before it equilibrates — not surface chemistry. A sobering detail: unconstrained liming drives Chinese-EEZ surface pCO2 below pre-industrial levels; capping aragonite saturation at 5 cuts that region's uptake 29%.

Ciscato et al. add the seasonal dimension with FOCI-MOPS alone: 27 Tmol yr⁻¹ of alkalinity along European coasts. Summer stratification retains added alkalinity, reversing the natural alkalinity seasonal cycle and turning the region into a year-round sink, yet the biggest *excess* uptake still comes in winter, in phase with mixed-layer deepening. Under SSP3-7.0 the sink strengthens more than under SSP1-2.6 because of weaker buffering.

**Field reaction and who it touches**

Beyond the Zenodo archives and the Research Square listing, I haven't seen substantive commentary yet — expected for papers this fresh. The practical implication is clear, though: Xie et al. explicitly flag Australia, Japan and Saudi Arabia as geographically disadvantaged over 10-year horizons, citing Ebb Carbon's SWA desalination partnership for the latter. Any developer siting coastal OAE — Planetary among them — now has a free screening tool that says site selection within 100 km can matter as much as region choice.

**What I'll watch**

Whether the absorber method gets adopted into MRV protocols as a screening layer before expensive conventional runs; whether Eisenring et al.'s forthcoming buffer-capacity analysis explains the OAE-DOCR gap; and whether the multi-model spread narrows once someone pairs Xie's high-resolution nearshore pathways with coupled ESM feedbacks.
