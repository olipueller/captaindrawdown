<p>Here&rsquo;s the reality check the CDR industry needs to sit with: a new paper in <em>Communications Earth & Environment</em> (Nature portfolio) finds that scaling negative emissions technologies to the gigatons could create serious resource and environmental bottlenecks — some of which nobody is planning for.</p><p>The <a href=https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-03348-8>paper by Cobo, Galán-Martín, and Guillén-Gosálbez</a> runs a comprehensive life-cycle assessment across the full CDR technology portfolio and identifies what the authors call &ldquo;previously overlooked bottlenecks.&rdquo;</p><h2 id=the-key-findings>The Key Findings<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-key-findings>#</a></h2><p><strong>Biochar and BECCS</strong> could sharply raise nutrient demand, further compromising food security. That&rsquo;s a non-trivial finding for the fastest-scaling CDR pathway. If we&rsquo;re converting agricultural waste into carbon removal at scale, we need to account for what those nutrients were doing in the first place.</p><p><strong>DAC and ocean liming</strong> could critically exacerbate the mining of key mineral resources. The irony: two of the technologies praised for their lower &ldquo;collateral damage&rdquo; on land use and biodiversity may create their own extraction pressures elsewhere.</p><p>The paper&rsquo;s framing is important — it doesn&rsquo;t argue against scaling CDR. It argues for <em>informed</em> scaling that balances multiple risks simultaneously.</p><h2 id=the-cdi-connection>The CDI Connection<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-cdi-connection>#</a></h2><p>This reinforces what CDI has been writing about: the need to <a href=https://www.carbon-drawdown.de/blog/2026-2-24-cdr-must-shift-from-speed-amp-scale-to-prove-amp-learn>shift from &ldquo;Speed & Scale&rdquo; to &ldquo;Prove & Learn&rdquo;</a>. Rushing to gigatons without understanding resource constraints isn&rsquo;t ambition — it&rsquo;s recklessness.</p><p>For <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a> specifically, the mineral supply question is real but manageable. The feedstock (basalt, limestone) is abundant, but processing, transport, and application at scale still require significant energy and infrastructure. CDI&rsquo;s own research on <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>ERW</a> MRV has consistently highlighted that <a href=https://carbondrawdown.substack.com/p/59-leachate-ph-macro-and-micro-scale>understanding what&rsquo;s actually happening in the soil</a> matters more than headline removal estimates.</p><h2 id=bottom-line>Bottom Line<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#bottom-line>#</a></h2><p>The CDR industry can&rsquo;t afford to repeat the mistakes of other energy transitions — scaling fast and discovering resource conflicts later. This paper is a roadmap for doing it right: diverse technology portfolios, regional resource assessments, and honest accounting of trade-offs.</p><p>Read the paper. Then read it again.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-03348-8>Cobo et al.
