<p>Google just signed one of the largest waste-to-biochar carbon removal deals on record: 200,000 metric tons of CO₂ removal by 2030, powered by a company that uses AI to sort through your garbage.</p><p>The partner is <a href=https://ampsortation.com/>AMP</a>, a Colorado-based robotics company whose AI-powered sortation systems can scan thousands of items per minute on conveyor belts of unsorted municipal waste. Compressed air jets then separate organics from recyclables and landfill-bound materials — all without requiring residents to sort their own trash.</p><h2 id=from-landfill-to-locked-carbon>From Landfill to Locked Carbon<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#from-landfill-to-locked-carbon>#</a></h2><p>Here&rsquo;s the problem AMP is solving: when organic material ends up in a landfill, it decomposes and releases methane — a greenhouse gas <a href=https://www.globalmethanepledge.org/imperative-methane-action>80 times more potent</a> than CO₂ in the near term. Instead of letting that happen, AMP diverts the organics and converts them into biochar through pyrolysis (heating in a low-oxygen environment). The result is a stable carbon material that locks away CO₂ for hundreds of years.</p><p>The first major deployment is happening at <a href=https://ampsortation.com/articles/largest-us-recycling-project-spsa>Virginia&rsquo;s largest recycling project</a>, serving 1.2 million residents through the Southeastern Public Service Authority. The facility will process around 540,000 tons of waste annually — a number that could grow to 700,000 tons over time, generating hundreds of thousands of carbon credits.</p><h2 id=double-climate-win>Double Climate Win<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#double-climate-win>#</a></h2><p>What makes this deal notable isn&rsquo;t just the carbon removal. It&rsquo;s the methane avoidance built into the same process. By diverting organics from landfills, AMP tackles two greenhouse gases at once — the stored carbon in the biochar <em>and</em> the methane that would have been emitted from decomposition.</p><p>Google framed the partnership as building on its <a href=https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/vaulted-deep-partnership/>earlier work with Vaulted Deep</a>, another company that transforms waste into a carbon removal lever. The pattern is clear: Google is betting on waste streams as feedstock for permanent carbon removal.</p><h2 id=the-economics-work>The Economics Work<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-economics-work>#</a></h2><p>Biochar credits currently trade around $120–150 per ton of CO₂ equivalent — relatively cheap compared to other durable removal methods like direct air capture ($400–600+). AMP expects costs to drop below $100 as the operation scales and replicates across more municipal waste facilities.</p><p>The additionality case is straightforward: no regulation requires municipalities to divert organics from landfill this way, and no other revenue stream funds it. The credits are the mechanism that makes the whole thing viable.</p><p>The biochar itself won&rsquo;t sit idle either. Initially it&rsquo;ll serve as daily landfill cover (reducing odors and methane leakage from existing waste), with plans to eventually use it in concrete and soil amendment.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>The world&rsquo;s biggest supplier of biochar credits is Exomad in Bolivia, processing sustainable forestry residues. AMP is pioneering something different: municipal solid waste as feedstock. Every city has garbage. Not every city has forestry residues. If AI-powered sorting can reliably extract organics from mixed waste at scale, the addressable supply of feedstock becomes enormous.</p><p>At $100/ton and hundreds of thousands of tons per facility, this starts to look like carbon removal infrastructure that could pencil out without corporate philanthropy — just good waste economics with a carbon bonus.</p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href=https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/divert-waste-from-landfills-remove-carbon/>Google Blog</a>, <a href=https://trellis.net/article/biochar-carbon-credits-ai-municipal-waste-amp/>Trellis</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/big-tech-carbon-credits-explode-ai-race/>Big Tech Carbon Credit Purchases Explode Amid AI Race</a></li><li><a href=/posts/verde-bioasphalt-biochar-roads-carbon-sinks/>Roads as Carbon Sinks: Verde Locks In Major Biochar Supply</a></li></ul>
