Microsoft has contracted 36.4 million tonnes of durable carbon removal, which is 78.5% of every tonne ever booked in this market. But strip Microsoft and Frontier out of the picture, and a very different market appears: the other buyers have actually taken delivery of 90% of tonnes physically removed to date, and retired 94% of them.
That's the headline finding from CDR.fyi's April 2026 demand structure snapshot, prepared by Tank Chen and the CDR.fyi team after reports that Microsoft paused new carbon removal purchases. The data tells a story that's more nuanced than "one buyer carries the market."
## Why it matters
If Microsoft pulls back, the contracted-volume chart looks catastrophic. The delivery and retirement charts barely move. Those are two different markets sitting inside one dataset, and conflating them has led to a lot of bad takes about CDR demand fragility.
The other useful frame: 195 of the 259 durable CDR suppliers tracked by CDR.fyi have never sold a tonne to Microsoft or Frontier. The long tail is real, and it's growing.
## The numbers
Total contracted durable CDR sits at roughly 46.4 Mt across all buyers. The split:
- Microsoft: 36,439,157 tonnes (78.5%)
- Frontier-linked buyers: 1,841,384 tonnes (4.0%)
- Everyone else: 8,167,880 tonnes (17.5%)
On suppliers, Microsoft has contracted with 39, Frontier with 38, and 195 suppliers have sold to neither. That's a lot of small deals happening below the radar of the two marquee buyers.
Deal-size concentration is stark. Microsoft is the only buyer that has signed disclosed agreements above 1 Mt. For deals under 100,000 tonnes, the "everyone else" segment dominates by count. So when people say "Microsoft is the market," they mean Microsoft is the megadeal market. Smaller transactions are a different beast.
## The growth story outside Microsoft
Volume contracted by buyers outside Microsoft and Frontier roughly doubled every year from 2022 to 2024. In 2025, that segment hit 3.2 Mt, nearly equal to 2023 and 2024 combined. This is the part of the chart that gets ignored when Microsoft's 5 Mt and 7 Mt offtakes suck up the headlines.
Three million tonnes of annual demand from a diversified buyer base is, frankly, the healthier signal for long-term market structure. It doesn't depend on one procurement team's quarterly mood.
## Method concentration
Microsoft's 36.4 Mt portfolio is 76% bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS), 13% biomass geological sequestration, 7% biochar, and 2% direct air carbon capture and storage (DACCS). About 27.5 Mt of BECCS sits on Microsoft's books alone.
Here's the catch: zero BECCS tonnes have been delivered to Microsoft under disclosed offtakes. Of the 91,000 tonnes Microsoft has actually received, 82.3% came from biochar, 13.1% from mineralized biomass carbon capture and storage (MBCCS), and 2.4% from biomass geological sequestration. The portfolio Microsoft contracted and the portfolio Microsoft has received are almost entirely different categories.
This is normal for early-stage offtakes. BECCS projects take years to build. But it means the BECCS pipeline is heavily exposed to one customer's continued willingness to pay on delivery, years from now.
## Implications
For suppliers: if you're chasing a megadeal, there is one buyer. If you're building a business on dozens of mid-sized contracts, there are hundreds of buyers, and that group is growing fast.
For BECCS developers specifically: 76% of Microsoft's portfolio is your method, and Microsoft's posture matters more to you than to anyone else. A pause hurts BECCS disproportionately.
For biochar, ERW (enhanced rock weathering), and other methods scaling through smaller deals: the non-Microsoft, non-Frontier segment is where you live, and it doubled three years running. That's the demand signal worth tracking.
For market analysts: stop using "tonnes contracted" as the only health metric. Delivered and retired tonnes tell you what's actually happening in the ground, and on those metrics Microsoft is a small minority.
## Caveats
The CDR.fyi data covers disclosed transactions only. Private deals, undisclosed volumes, and bilateral arrangements outside the tracked dataset don't appear. Microsoft's dominance could be overstated or understated depending on what's hidden.
"Frontier-linked buyers" is a category, not a single entity. Stripe, Alphabet, Meta, Shopify, and McKinsey all sit inside it, and each has its own procurement logic.
A pause is not a cancellation. Microsoft's contracted tonnes remain on the books, and deliveries are scheduled out for years. The question is what 2026 and 2027 contracting looks like, not whether existing offtakes evaporate.
And the deeper point worth keeping in view: durable CDR is for residual emissions in hard-to-abate sectors. The fact that one hyperscaler dominates the demand curve is a sign the buyer base needs to broaden, not that CDR is a substitute for cutting fossil emissions at the source. Those 3.2 Mt from "everyone else" in 2025 is the diversification the field needs more of.
