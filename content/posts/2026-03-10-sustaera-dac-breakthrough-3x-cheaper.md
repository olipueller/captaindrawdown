<p>Sustaera, a North Carolina-based DAC startup, just dropped a bold claim: their third-generation electro-thermal approach captures CO₂ from air at 90%+ energy efficiency, with capital costs 3-5x lower than existing thermal DAC technologies.</p><p>If verified at scale, that&rsquo;s not incremental. It&rsquo;s the kind of step change that could make DAC competitive with biochar.</p><h2 id=whats-different>What&rsquo;s Different?<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#whats-different>#</a></h2><p>Incumbent DAC systems — including Climeworks&rsquo; solid sorbent approach and Carbon Engineering&rsquo;s liquid solvent method — typically max out around 40% thermal efficiency. Sustaera says its proprietary nano-structured sorbent combined with integrated electric heating smashes through that ceiling.</p><p>The key distinction: this is &ldquo;3rd-generation&rdquo; DAC. Rather than relying on large baseload thermal energy (which requires either geothermal access or massive heat infrastructure), Sustaera&rsquo;s system runs on electricity. That opens the door to coupling with cheap renewables anywhere, not just locations with convenient geology.</p><h2 id=the-sub-100ton-question>The Sub-$100/ton Question<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-sub-100ton-question>#</a></h2><p>Sustaera claims these efficiencies put them on a credible path to sub-$100/ton carbon removal. That&rsquo;s the threshold where DAC stops being a premium climate product and starts competing in mainstream carbon markets.</p><p>To be clear: &ldquo;on a path to&rdquo; is not &ldquo;currently delivering at.&rdquo; And plenty of DAC companies have made cost projections that didn&rsquo;t survive contact with reality. But the 90%+ efficiency claim is specific and testable — either the physics work at scale or they don&rsquo;t.</p><h2 id=context-matters>Context Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#context-matters>#</a></h2><p>The DAC sector needs good news right now. The US just cancelled $1.2B in DAC hub funding. First-generation plants have delivered only a fraction of the credits they sold. The narrative around DAC has shifted from &ldquo;revolutionary&rdquo; to &ldquo;expensive and unreliable.&rdquo;</p><p>Sustaera has backing from Stripe and Shopify — carbon removal buyers who&rsquo;ve built reputations on picking technically credible projects. The company is now offering technology licensing to developers, a model that could accelerate deployment without Sustaera having to build and operate every plant itself.</p><p>If the efficiency numbers hold up at scale, this could rewrite DAC economics. Big if.
