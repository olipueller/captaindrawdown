CDR.fyi's June 2026 recap reports roughly **362,000 tonnes of durable CDR contracted in a single month** — a number that would have been an entire strong quarter not long ago. That figure, drawn from [CDR.fyi's report](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/cdr-monthly-recap-june-2026), is the anchor I want practitioners to sit with before parsing the deal list.

The buyer concentration story hasn't fundamentally changed, but it is broadening at the edges. Microsoft is still visible — CDR.fyi notes its involvement in the Deep Sky DAC delivery and a 36,920-tonne enhanced weathering offtake with Alt Carbon — but the more interesting signal is the financial sector filling in behind it. According to CDR.fyi, JP Morgan Chase committed to 61,500 tonnes from Charm Industrial *plus* $20 million in venture debt, and TD Bank signed two separate long-dated agreements (18,000 tonnes with Deep Sky over 10 years; a multi-pathway portfolio with Climeworks Solutions, also 10 years). When banks start writing 10-year offtakes across method portfolios, the market is maturing from spot-style procurement toward something closer to infrastructure finance.

Method mix is the other thing worth flagging. CDR.fyi's June tally leans heavily on biochar and enhanced weathering — the Altitude–Equilibrium deal alone is 180,000 tonnes over 8 years — with DAC volumes still smaller but delivering real credits (Deep Sky's Alpha facility supplying Microsoft and RBC, verified by Isometric, is called out as North America's first certified DAC delivery). This is roughly the shape prior monthly recaps have shown: near-term tonnage carried by lower-cost biomass and mineral pathways, with DAC contributing credibility and forward capacity rather than bulk volume. Climeworks Solutions' 14 partnerships totaling ~450,000 tonnes across H1 2026, per CDR.fyi, fits that same pattern — portfolio-first, pathway-agnostic.

The honest caveat: contracted is not delivered. A 180,000-tonne, 8-year biochar agreement is a financing instrument as much as a climate outcome, and the physics of scaling industrial biochar across "multiple agricultural regions" — as CDR.fyi describes the Equilibrium deal — is nontrivial. The same discipline applies to UnionDAC's 60,000 tonnes/year target for 2030 and Brineworks' sub-$115/tonne early data at a 100-tonne demonstration scale. These are promises against future engineering. The gap between announced offtake and verified retirement remains the number I'd watch hardest going into CDR.fyi's Q2 update.

If June's 362,000 tonnes holds as a floor rather than a spike, 2026 becomes the year durable CDR stops being measured in pilot volumes and starts being measured in project-finance flows — and that changes what every developer should be building toward next.
