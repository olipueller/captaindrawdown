CDR.fyi's August 2026 recap reports roughly 128,000 tonnes of durable CDR contracted in the month. A single deal accounts for 70,000 of those tonnes: Höganäs, a Swedish metal powder manufacturer, signed a 10-year agreement with Öresundskraft for supply from its planned BECCS facility at Filbornaverket in Helsingborg. By my arithmetic, that is about 55% of the month's volume from one contract.

That concentration is the real story in [CDR.fyi's report](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/cdr-monthly-recap-august-2026). Add Microsoft's 23,602-tonne offtake with CREW Carbon, which integrates removal into existing wastewater treatment, and two deals make up roughly 73% of the headline figure. The Höganäs deal is the more interesting one. It pairs an industrial buyer with a utility-scale supplier in the same region. Most deals come from tech companies buying for their own offsets. If regional industrial buyers start signing decade-long contracts with nearby BECCS operators, demand stops depending on a handful of corporate climate budgets. Spread evenly, which is my assumption and not something the report states, 70,000 tonnes over 10 years is about 7,000 tonnes a year. That is meaningful anchor demand for a facility that has not been built yet, but it is not huge.

The rest of the buyer list is broad but thin. CDR.fyi notes U.S. Bank's first-ever purchase, from Carba's Minnesota landfill biochar project, and a MetLife partnership with Charm Industrial that combines credit purchases with insurance and benefits. First-time financial-sector buyers matter more for market depth than for tonnage. The method mix is similarly wide: BECCS, wastewater-integrated removal, biomass direct storage, biochar, and enhanced weathering through Stack Carbon's Uganda offtake with Wild Assets. No single pathway owns the month.

For scale, CDR.fyi reports that Occidental's Stratos DAC facility is targeting 500,000 tonnes of removal a year, with operations expected by the end of 2026. A single plant's annual target is therefore nearly four times August's entire contracted volume. Contracting is still well behind the capacity developers are building.

One caveat on the numbers. The recap doesn't provide a month-over-month comparison, so I can't say whether 128,000 tonnes is a strong or weak August relative to earlier periods. The figures also don't reconcile cleanly. Höganäs (70,000), CREW (23,602), and the Senken–Carbonsate biomass storage deal (50,000) sum to about 143,600 tonnes, which is more than the headline total. The most likely explanation is that CDR.fyi applies its own rules on inclusion, classification, or timing. Practitioners citing the headline number should check the underlying data in CDR.fyi's portal before assuming every listed deal is inside it. The published text I worked from was also truncated, so there may be context I didn't see.

The signal to watch in the coming months is whether August's two new patterns repeat: regional industrial buyers signing long-term BECCS contracts, and banks and insurers moving from first purchases to recurring offtakes.
