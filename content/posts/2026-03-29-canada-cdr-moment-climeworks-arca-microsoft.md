<p>While one North American government is busy dismantling climate infrastructure, the other is quietly becoming a meaningful player in carbon dioxide removal.</p><p>Three data points from Canada tell a coherent story.</p><p><strong>Climeworks chose Calgary</strong> for its Canadian headquarters. The Swiss direct air capture company — operator of the Mammoth facility in Iceland and one of the most credible DAC companies in the world — looked at North America and picked Calgary. That&rsquo;s not a symbolic choice. It reflects real assessment of Canada&rsquo;s regulatory environment, talent pipeline, and long-term policy stability.</p><p><strong>Arca signed a major deal with Microsoft.</strong> Arca Climate&rsquo;s approach uses autonomous rovers to accelerate carbon mineralization of mining waste — a genuinely scalable, low-energy pathway that leverages existing industrial footprints. A Microsoft-scale buyer signing with a Canadian CDR company signals that the Canadian supply chain is being taken seriously at enterprise procurement level.</p><p><strong>Canada runs the world&rsquo;s only active government CDR buying program.</strong> Through Natural Resources Canada&rsquo;s standing offer mechanism, the government has committed to purchasing CDR credits across multiple methods — DACCS, BECCS, biochar, BiCRS, and enhanced mineralization. No other national government has operationalized this kind of direct demand signal.</p><h2 id=the-contrast-is-not-subtle>The Contrast Is Not Subtle<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-contrast-is-not-subtle>#</a></h2><p>South of the border, the IRA&rsquo;s CDR incentives face political pressure, DOE programs have been cut, and the federal government&rsquo;s stance on climate is openly hostile. The regulatory certainty that carbon removal companies need — multi-year purchase commitments, credible MRV frameworks, stable policy — has become unreliable.</p><p>Canada has none of those problems right now. That&rsquo;s why companies building for a 10–20 year horizon are looking north.</p><h2 id=what-it-means-for-the-market>What It Means for the Market<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-it-means-for-the-market>#</a></h2><p>Government demand signals matter disproportionately at this stage of the CDR market. When a government commits to purchasing, it validates the credit quality, creates bankable revenue, and gives companies the certainty to raise capital for scaling. Canada&rsquo;s standing offer does all three.</p><p>The Pembina Institute, which has closely tracked Canada&rsquo;s CDR policy development, framed it plainly: &ldquo;Canada&rsquo;s carbon removal moment is here.&rdquo; The combination of infrastructure investment, corporate deal flow, and government demand creation makes that assessment hard to dispute.</p><p>This isn&rsquo;t a story about Canada being perfect. CDR in Canada still faces the same challenges as everywhere — scale, cost, MRV rigor, public acceptance. But the direction is right, and the momentum is real.</p><hr><p><em>Via the <a href=https://bsky.app/profile/pembina.org/post/3mi2bsqubir2s>Pembina Institute</a> on Bluesky.</em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/canada-first-government-cdr-procurement/>Canada Becomes First Government to Buy CDR Credits</a></li><li><a href=/posts/cdr-critical-minerals-three-similarities/>CDR and Critical Minerals Have More in Common Than You&rsquo;d Think</a></li><li><a href=/posts/canada-advance-carbon-removal-coalition/>Canada Assembles Its CDR Dream Team With $100M Coalition</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-crcf-carbon-removal-certification/>The EU Just Built the World&rsquo;s First Government CDR Credit Label</a></li></ul>
