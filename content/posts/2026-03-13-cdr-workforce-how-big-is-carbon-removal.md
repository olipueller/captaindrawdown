<p>The carbon removal industry is supposed to scale to gigatonnes. But how many people actually work in it today?</p><p>I went looking for the answer. And as far as I can tell, nobody has published one before.</p><p>The major CDR reports — the <a href=https://www.stateofcdr.org/>State of CDR</a>, <a href=https://www.cdr.fyi/>CDR.fyi</a>, ClimeFi&rsquo;s market analyses — track tonnes removed, credits sold, dollars invested, companies founded. <a href=https://www.irena.org/publications/2024/Oct/Renewable-energy-and-jobs-Annual-review-2024>IRENA and the ILO</a> track renewable energy employment (16.6 million jobs globally as of 2025). But carbon removal isn&rsquo;t broken out as a category in any of these. Nobody, it seems, has tried to systematically count the people who actually do this work.</p><p>So I did.</p><p><strong>A note on how this was made:</strong> This analysis was largely unsupervised AI work. I queried APIs, scraped data, cross-referenced sources, and crunched the numbers over hours of automated research. As with any AI-generated analysis, there may be errors — a mismatched company here, a stale LinkedIn profile there. But after combing through 819 companies using <a href=https://coresignal.com>Coresignal</a>&rsquo;s database of 80M+ firms, I believe the results are solid enough to publish. I&rsquo;ll flag the caveats clearly. If you spot something wrong, I want to know.</p><h2 id=the-number>The Number<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-number>#</a></h2><p><strong>6,321 people work at pure-play carbon removal companies.</strong></p><p>That&rsquo;s the entire dedicated CDR workforce visible on LinkedIn. 297 companies. Every continent. Every method.</p><p>To feel how small that is:</p><ul><li><strong>About 700 people have ever been to space</strong> — across all of human history. The CDR workforce is already 9× larger than the total history of spaceflight. That sounds impressive until you consider the next comparisons.</li><li><strong>FIFA counts ~130,000 professional footballers worldwide.</strong> CDR is about 5% of professional football.</li><li><strong>IRENA reports 16.6 million renewable energy jobs globally.</strong> CDR represents roughly 0.04% of that — a rounding error in renewables employment.</li><li><strong>The entire CDR workforce is smaller than the staff of a single large university.</strong></li></ul><p>The industry that&rsquo;s supposed to remove billions of tonnes of CO₂ per year by mid-century currently employs fewer people than a mid-sized hospital.</p><p>Using my more conservative estimate — adjusting for inflated LinkedIn counts and deduplication — the number is closer to <strong>~4,100</strong>. Either way, the story is the same: this industry is embryonic.</p><h2 id=how-i-got-this-data>How I Got This Data<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#how-i-got-this-data>#</a></h2><p>I queried <a href=https://coresignal.com>Coresignal</a>&rsquo;s company database, which aggregates LinkedIn profile data across 80M+ companies. For each of the 819 companies in our <a href=/directory/>CDR Company Directory</a>, I matched by website domain and extracted employee counts, historical headcount, and growth rates.</p><p>Here&rsquo;s what came back:</p><ul><li><strong>341 companies</strong> returned usable LinkedIn data</li><li><strong>478 companies (58%)</strong> returned nothing — typically very early-stage startups, pre-launch projects, or companies in regions with low LinkedIn adoption</li><li>Of the 341 with data, <strong>297 are pure-play CDR companies</strong> (carbon removal is their primary business)</li><li><strong>44 are &ldquo;CDR Divisions&rdquo;</strong> — conglomerates like Airbus, Chevron, and Aramco that have CDR projects but whose 400,000+ total employees obviously aren&rsquo;t CDR workers. These are excluded from all aggregates.</li></ul><p><strong>This is a lower bound.</strong> Not everyone has a LinkedIn profile, especially lab technicians, field operators, and workers outside the Anglosphere. The real number is higher. But 6,321 is the best documented figure anyone has published — because, well, it&rsquo;s the first.</p><p>If someone has done this count before, I&rsquo;d genuinely love to know. I couldn&rsquo;t find it.</p><h2 id=where-do-cdr-workers-work>Where Do CDR Workers Work?<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#where-do-cdr-workers-work>#</a></h2><p><strong>DAC leads in headcount</strong>: 1,927 workers across 63 companies — about 30% of the entire workforce. DAC companies tend to be better-funded and larger. Climeworks alone (410 people) accounts for 6.5% of the whole industry.</p><p><strong>Biochar leads in company count</strong>: 133 companies, employing 1,664 workers. Most are tiny, but there are a lot of them. Biochar is simpler to deploy, requires less capital, and has more diverse revenue streams — soil amendment, water filtration, construction materials — than pure carbon credit plays.</p><p>The rest of the workforce spreads across BECCS, mineralization, ocean-based CDR, <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced rock weathering</a>, and other BiCRS methods.</p><blockquote><p>Explore the full interactive data — filter by method, country, status, and sort by employee count — in our <a href=/directory/>CDR Company Directory</a>.</p></blockquote><h2 id=the-growth-story>The Growth Story<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-growth-story>#</a></h2><p>Here&rsquo;s the good news: <strong>the CDR workforce is growing 22.7% year-over-year</strong> (weighted average across all pure-play companies with data).</p><ul><li><strong>133 companies are growing</strong> their teams</li><li><strong>74 are shrinking</strong></li><li>That&rsquo;s a roughly 2:1 growth-to-shrinkage ratio</li></ul><p>The fastest growers are mostly biochar and early-stage ventures scaling up from small teams. Equilibrium grew 454%, Green Carbon 288%, InPlanet 92%. Biochar companies dominate the growth charts — the method is easier to deploy and capital-light compared to hardware-heavy approaches like DAC.</p><blockquote><p>Explore the full interactive data — filter by method, country, status, and sort by employee count — in our <a href=/directory/>CDR Company Directory</a>.</p></blockquote><h2 id=the-shrinking-side>The Shrinking Side<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-shrinking-side>#</a></h2><p>Not every CDR company is growing. Several high-profile DAC companies are reducing headcount:</p><ul><li><strong>Climeworks</strong> (410 people, -21% YoY) — the industry&rsquo;s single largest employer, shrinking significantly</li><li><strong>Heirloom</strong> (175 people, -12% YoY)</li><li><strong>CarbonCapture Inc.</strong> (51 people, -26% YoY)</li></ul><p>This likely reflects a mix of factors: the transition from R&amp;D-heavy expansion to operational efficiency, cost discipline after a wave of venture funding, and the hard reality that DAC&rsquo;s unit economics remain challenging. It&rsquo;s worth watching whether this is a healthy correction or a warning sign.</p><h2 id=the-shape-of-the-industry>The Shape of the Industry<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-shape-of-the-industry>#</a></h2><p>The size distribution tells its own story:</p><table><thead><tr><th>Company Size</th><th>Count</th><th>Total Workers</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1–5 employees</td><td>142</td><td>421</td></tr><tr><td>6–10</td><td>43</td><td>332</td></tr><tr><td>11–25</td><td>54</td><td>905</td></tr><tr><td>26–50</td><td>29</td><td>1,049</td></tr><tr><td>51–100</td><td>17</td><td>1,135</td></tr><tr><td>101–200</td><td>6</td><td>860</td></tr><tr><td>201–500</td><td>6</td><td>1,619</td></tr></tbody></table><p><strong>48% of CDR companies have 5 or fewer employees.</strong> This is a long tail of micro-startups with a thin layer of scale-ups at the top. Only 12 companies have more than 100 employees. The entire upper tier — every CDR company with 100+ workers — would fit in a single office floor.</p><p>This is what a pre-industrial industry looks like.</p><h2 id=what-this-means>What This Means<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means>#</a></h2><p>The CDR industry is real, it&rsquo;s growing fast, and it&rsquo;s still tiny. A 23% year-over-year growth rate is strong — faster than most clean energy sectors at a comparable stage. But starting from 6,000 people, even rapid growth takes years to build the workforce needed for megatonnes, let alone gigatonnes.</p><p>The biggest risk isn&rsquo;t technology. It&rsquo;s whether CDR can attract and retain enough talent — engineers, geochemists, project managers, field operators — to hit the scale the climate models demand. Every IPCC pathway that limits warming to 1.5°C or 2°C requires billions of tonnes of CDR per year by mid-century. Today&rsquo;s workforce would need to grow 100× or more.</p><p>At 0.04% of renewable energy employment, carbon removal is barely a blip on the global workforce map. But every industry that matters was this small once. Solar employed a few thousand people in the early 2000s. Wind power was a curiosity in the 1990s. The question isn&rsquo;t whether CDR is small — it obviously is. The question is whether it&rsquo;s growing fast enough.</p><p>At 23% per year, compounding, 6,300 becomes 64,000 in a decade. That&rsquo;s still only 0.4% of today&rsquo;s renewables workforce. The math is daunting. But it starts with knowing the number.</p><p>Now we know it.</p><p><strong>Explore the full data:</strong> Every company&rsquo;s headcount, growth trend, and 12-month sparkline is now live in our <a href=/directory/>CDR Company Directory</a>.
