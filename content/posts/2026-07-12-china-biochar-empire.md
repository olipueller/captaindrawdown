Biochar research is a Chinese enterprise. Of the 21,978 researchers whose
primary pathway is biochar in the [CDR Researcher Census](/cdr-researcher-census/),
9,185 - 42% - are in China. The United States has 1,276. That is a ratio
of more than seven to one, the most lopsided national concentration of any
carbon-removal pathway.

![Biochar researchers by country](/images/posts/biochar-census-country.png)

No other pathway comes close to this. India is a distant second with 1,766
biochar researchers; the US, so dominant in direct air capture, is third
here and barely visible on the chart. Biochar is the one CDR field where a
single country holds a near-plurality of the entire global research
workforce.

## Why biochar is Chinese

The reason is agricultural, not climatic. Biochar is made by pyrolysing
crop residue - rice straw, corn stalks - into a stable, carbon-rich solid
that can be returned to soil. China produces more crop residue than any
country on earth, and for years burned much of it in the open, a major
source of seasonal air pollution. The policy response - bans on open straw
burning, subsidies to process residue instead - turned a waste problem into
a research boom. Biochar sits at the intersection of soil science,
agricultural engineering, and pollution control, three fields where
China's research output already leads. The carbon-removal framing arrived
on top of an existing agronomic machine.
[Jianying Shang](/cdr-researcher-census/researchers/jianying-shang-a5052558/)
at China Agricultural University and
[Ke Sun](/cdr-researcher-census/researchers/ke-sun-a5066774/) are among
the most-published names in that machine.

A scope note belongs here, because biochar is where the census and the
broader literature diverge most. [Lück and colleagues' 2025 map of global
CDR research in *Nature
Communications*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56166-9) count every
biochar publication, including pure agronomy with no carbon-removal
framing - their largest single cluster. The census requires a
carbon-removal context, so its biochar count is smaller and its
geography sharper. Either way the national pattern is the same: the centre
of gravity is in China.

## What it means

Research leadership tends to precede deployment leadership. The country
that trains the most biochar scientists, runs the most field trials, and
publishes the most methods is the country best positioned to scale biochar
as durable carbon removal - and to set the standards others adopt. Western
CDR buyers and policymakers who think of biochar as a boutique pathway are
looking at a field whose expertise base is overwhelmingly outside their
own institutions. If biochar becomes a serious removal pathway at gigatonne
scale, the science it runs on will have been written, in large part, in
Chinese universities. That is worth knowing now, while the field is still
being defined.
