Here is the number that stopped me cold: Microsoft accounted for **78.5% of all disclosed durable CDR tonnes purchased as of April 2026**, according to [CDR.fyi's report](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/durable-cdr-market-update-carbon-unbound-east-coast-2026) from Carbon Unbound East Coast. One buyer, nearly four-fifths of a market. That is not a market — that is a customer with suppliers attached.

The good news buried in the same report is that the rest of the market has finally started to matter. CDR.fyi's update shows that excluding Microsoft and Frontier, durable CDR purchases still grew at a **151% CAGR from 2021 to 2025**, with deliveries compounding at 131% over the same window. Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 were the strongest quarters on record for this broader buyer base. That is the answer to the question every practitioner has been asking since Microsoft's multi-million-tonne orders began landing in Q2 2024: is there a market underneath the whale? Yes — smaller, lumpier, but real and accelerating.

The delivery data is where the story gets genuinely interesting. CDR.fyi reports that buyers outside Microsoft and Frontier represent only **17% of contracted tonnes but 90% of delivered tonnes and 94% of retired tonnes**. Read that twice. The headline-grabbing offtakes are concentrated in one buyer, but the tonnes actually hitting the atmosphere-to-storage pipeline are being pulled by a much more diversified base. Contracts are a promise; retirements are physics. The diversification of the market is further along than the purchase-concentration figure suggests.

The method mix tells you where that execution is coming from. CDR.fyi's numbers put **BiCRS at 96% of 2025 purchase volume and 91% of delivered volume**, with BECCS leading contracts and biochar leading deliveries. This is the pragmatic near-term face of durable CDR: biomass pathways that can be permitted, built, and measured today, while DAC and mineralization work through their cost curves. It is also a concentration risk of a different flavor — one feedstock category carrying the delivery load.

The honest caveat: two strong quarters do not make a trend, and CDR.fyi says as much. The market has never been tested by a full withdrawal of its anchor buyer, and the report frames Microsoft's pause as a "stress test," not a verdict. Whether compliance demand, public procurement, and intermediaries scale fast enough to absorb that gap over the next 12-24 months is the open question.

The next five years will not look like the last five — they will be judged on delivered and retired tonnes, and that is the scoreboard I will be watching.
