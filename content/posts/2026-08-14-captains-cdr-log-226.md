## Why this matters now

The enhanced rock weathering (ERW) credit market is being built on model-based accounting while the longest-running independent field trial in Europe just returned a null result. Registries are certifying tonnes. Buyers are paying. And the underlying measurement question - can we actually detect the CO2 removal in the soil - is not settled. That gap between rulebook and field data is the whole story today.

## What is enhanced rock weathering?

ERW spreads crushed silicate rock, usually basalt, on farmland. As the rock reacts with rainwater and soil acids, it consumes CO2 and locks the carbon into dissolved bicarbonate that eventually reaches the ocean. In theory it is durable removal on the scale of tens of thousands of years. In practice, the removal has to be measured in soils that are noisy, variable, and slow to respond. If you are new to the pathway, I wrote a plain-language explainer here: What Is Enhanced Weathering?.

## Who's involved?

- **The Carbon Drawdown Initiative**, whose researcher Pol Knops ran the XXL Lysimeter Experiment cited below.
- **Isometric**, the dominant durable-CDR registry, which just moved to Standard v2.0.
- **Buyers and brokers** issuing ERW purchase agreements against protocols that lean heavily on modeled tonnes.
- **Academic groups** publishing the field data that will either confirm or contradict the credit math.

## What just happened?

Four years in, twenty basalt-amended pots showed no more inorganic carbon removal than untreated controls. That is the headline from the [Carbon Drawdown Initiative's XXL Lysimeter Experiment](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7493651331526742016/), a 1,400-day trial run by Pol Knops. Yield co-benefits held up. The carbon claim, which is the entire basis for ERW credits, did not.

As I put it on Bluesky, this is ([@captaindrawdown.bsky.social](https://bsky.app/profile/captaindrawdown.bsky.social/post/3mszeynp7fz2e)) "a stress test of ERW registry rulebooks," not just a science story. And it matters that Knops separated the two claims cleanly ([@captaindrawdown.bsky.social](https://bsky.app/profile/captaindrawdown.bsky.social/post/3mszeyqycjk2n)): "no detectable inorganic carbon signal above noise. Yield co-benefits held." ERW marketing routinely blurs those.

Steel-manning the ERW side: one site is one site. A [Southeastern US field trial on CDRXIV](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/413) reports strong basalt yield benefits and different soil chemistry may produce a detectable weathering signal where cool German soils did not. New peer-reviewed work from [Lei et al. via KITopen](https://doi.org/10.5445/ir/1000196136) argues part of the ERW carbon effect may run through mineral-associated organic carbon, not the inorganic pool most protocols measure. If true, current MRV (measurement, reporting, verification) is looking in the wrong place, which would mean protocols are undercounting, not overselling.

Both can be true. The removal may be real and the current rulebook may still be wrong.

## Open questions to track

- Does [Isometric Standard v2.0](https://biochartoday.com/news/isometric-signs-tongao-greenchar-vastwing-energy-and-decarbon-tech-to-certify-first-chinese-carbon-removals/) require multi-year field measurement, or does it stay model-weighted?
- Do other long-duration field trials publish confirmatory or contradictory signals in the next two quarters?
- Will registries add an organic-carbon pathway to ERW accounting, as the KITopen work implies they should?
- Does marine ERW, where [Huysmans et al. compared olivine, basalt and kimberlite feedstocks](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21808606), face the same measured-versus-modeled reckoning?
