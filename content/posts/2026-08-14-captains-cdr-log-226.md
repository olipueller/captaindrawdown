## Why this matters now

A 1,400-day German pot trial just reported no measurable carbon removal from basalt-amended soils, right as new preprints tout yield gains from the same practice. Enhanced rock weathering (ERW, spreading crushed silicate rock on fields to pull CO2 from air as it dissolves) is one of the fastest-growing durable-CDR credit categories. If the longest independent field data cannot see the removal signal, buyers signing 2027 tonnage need to ask harder questions this quarter, not next year.

## What is it?

ERW credits are sold on modelled carbon math. A supplier spreads basalt, measures cations (calcium, magnesium) leaving the soil in water samples, and back-calculates how much CO2 the weathering reaction consumed. That number becomes tonnes. What almost nobody sells on is a direct, multi-year measurement of net CO2 drawdown from the treated plot versus an untreated control. That is the gap the new German null result sits in.

## Who's involved?

The Carbon Drawdown Initiative, a German independent research outfit, ran the [1,400-day, twenty-pot lysimeter experiment](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7493289822275432448/). On the other side of the ledger, a US team published a [southeastern-US basalt field trial reporting yield benefits](https://cdrxiv.org/preprint/413) at CDRXIV, and Lei et al. published [process work on how basalt weathering reshapes mineral-associated organic carbon](https://doi.org/10.5445/ir/1000196136). Registries writing the rules, Isometric and Puro.earth, sit downstream of a literature base that, as I noted last week, is still [curated by a single Yale postdoc](https://bsky.app/profile/captaindrawdown.bsky.social/post/3msue5tmzip2h).

## What just happened?

Carbon Drawdown Initiative reports that basalt-treated pots removed no more CO2 than untreated controls over nearly four winters. Zero. That is not a small effect size, it is an absence of signal in one of the longest-running independent ERW experiments in Europe. It landed the same week that [Isometric Standard v2.0 went live](https://bsky.app/profile/captaindrawdown.bsky.social/post/3msryf7l2is2w) with a tightened rulebook that still leans on modelled cation export rather than long-duration flux measurement.

Steel-manning the critique: if the best independent field data cannot detect the removal claimed by the models, either the models are wrong, the pot geometry does not represent field conditions, or the timescale to see cation export in a temperate climate is longer than four years. Any of those three answers is a problem for a credit being sold today as a 1,000-year removal. Mineralization practitioner Pol Knops ([@polknops.bsky.social](https://bsky.app/profile/polknops.bsky.social/post/3msx6cihb6c2s)) puts the honest frame plainly: removal is necessary but slow, and a different kind of work than emissions cuts. ERW may still deliver. The point is that the current sales pitch runs ahead of what long-run field data can confirm.

## Open questions to track

- Will Isometric, Puro.earth or CDR.fyi reference the Carbon Drawdown Initiative lysimeter dataset in the next revision of their ERW methodologies, or route around it?
- Do the next Frontier Climate or Google-funded ERW deployments publish matched, long-duration control plots, or only supplier-run cation models?
- If yield gains are real but inorganic removal is not, does ERW get reclassified as an agronomic input with a carbon co-benefit rather than a durable-CDR credit?
- Does the Lei et al. finding on mineral-associated organic carbon mean current MRV (measurement, reporting, verification) protocols are measuring the wrong soil carbon pool entirely?
