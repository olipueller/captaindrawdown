<p>For four days last August, the waters 50 miles off the Massachusetts coast turned maroon. On purpose.</p><p>Scientists from the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI) pumped 65,000 litres of sodium hydroxide — tagged with a red dye for tracking — into the Gulf of Maine as part of the <a href=https://locness.whoi.edu/>LOC-NESS project</a>, the first large-scale open-water test of ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE). The experiment, licensed by the US EPA, took place in an area commonly fished for cod, haddock, and lobster.</p><h2 id=what-they-found>What They Found<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-they-found>#</a></h2><p>Early results, announced at the AGU Ocean Sciences Meeting in Glasgow, are striking:</p><ul><li><strong>Up to 10 tonnes of CO₂</strong> entered the ocean during the five-day observation period</li><li><strong>Local pH rose from 7.95 to 8.3</strong> — effectively restoring alkalinity to preindustrial levels at the deployment site</li><li><strong>No significant harm</strong> to plankton, fish larvae, or lobster larvae was observed</li></ul><p>The team used autonomous gliders, long-range underwater vehicles, and shipboard sensors to trace how the alkaline plume dispersed.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>The ocean already holds roughly 38,000 billion tonnes of carbon as dissolved bicarbonate. OAE works by boosting this natural alkalinity — essentially accelerating a geological process that normally takes millennia. If it scales, OAE could simultaneously remove atmospheric CO₂ and counteract ocean acidification, which is now <a href=https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/mar/10/sodium-hydroxide-ocean-global-heating-solution>higher than at any point in the past million years</a>.</p><p>Several OAE startups are already selling verified carbon credits through registries like Isometric. But LOC-NESS represents something different: independent, EPA-permitted science generating the baseline data the field needs.</p><h2 id=the-pushback>The Pushback<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-pushback>#</a></h2><p>Not everyone is cheering. Friends of the Earth US called the approach &ldquo;profoundly concerning,&rdquo; citing risks of &ldquo;catastrophic unforeseen consequences&rdquo; at scale. The debate over chemical intervention in ocean systems isn&rsquo;t going away.</p><p>But as lead oceanographer Adam Subhas pointed out: we&rsquo;re already conducting an uncontrolled experiment on the climate. The question isn&rsquo;t whether to intervene — it&rsquo;s whether to do so with data or without.</p><h2 id=whats-next>What&rsquo;s Next<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#whats-next>#</a></h2><p>The LOC-NESS results haven&rsquo;t yet been peer-reviewed, and the experiment measured impacts on larvae but not adult fish or marine mammals. Scaling from 65,000 litres to ocean-scale deployment is a leap that will require far more ecological monitoring. But for a field that&rsquo;s been heavy on theory and light on open-water data, this is a significant step forward.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/mar/10/sodium-hydroxide-ocean-global-heating-solution>The Guardian</a> · <a href=https://locness.whoi.edu/>LOC-NESS Project</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/whoi-loc-ness-oae-trial-results/>First EPA-Permitted Ocean Alkalinity Trial Removes CO₂ Without Harming Marine Life</a></li><li><a href=/posts/gulf-of-maine-oae-trial-results/>Gulf of Maine OAE Trial: CO₂ Captured, No Harm to Marine Life</a></li><li><a href=/posts/microbes-marine-snow-ocean-carbon-sink/>Microbes on Marine Snow May Limit How Deep Carbon Actually Sinks</a></li><li><a href=/posts/ocean-cdr-nutrient-cycling-limits/>Ocean CDR Has a Nutrient Problem</a></li></ul>
