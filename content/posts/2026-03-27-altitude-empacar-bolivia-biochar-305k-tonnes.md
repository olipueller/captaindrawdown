<p>🌍 Bolivia wasn&rsquo;t on most CDR maps. It is now.</p><p>Carbon removal financier <a href=https://www.altitudecarbon.com/>Altitude</a> has signed an agreement with <strong>Empacar S.A.</strong>, one of Bolivia&rsquo;s largest industrial companies, for the purchase of over <strong>305,000 tonnes of biochar carbon dioxide removal</strong>. The credits will be generated via Empacar&rsquo;s Bolivia-based biochar production facilities, issued under <a href=https://puro.earth/>Puro.earth</a> or equivalent methodologies, with the first credits expected in 2027.</p><h2 id=who-is-empacar>Who Is Empacar?<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#who-is-empacar>#</a></h2><p>Empacar isn&rsquo;t a climate startup. It&rsquo;s a major Bolivian industrial player with decades of experience in circular materials management — the kind of company that understands supply chains, feedstock logistics, and long-term operations. It&rsquo;s now launching <strong>Carbon X</strong>, a dedicated business unit focused on climate innovation and voluntary carbon market participation.</p><p>The biochar production will be based in the <strong>Ascención de Guarayos region</strong> of Bolivia, converting <strong>forestry and agricultural residues</strong> into carbon-storing biochar. The facility is designed for approximately <strong>70,000 tonnes of CO₂ removal annually</strong> at full capacity. The Altitude deal represents a multi-year offtake commitment that helps justify the capital investment.</p><p>This isn&rsquo;t Bolivia&rsquo;s first CDR move. Empacar recently partnered with Puro.earth, <a href=https://www.cula.tech/>Cula</a>, and <a href=https://www.bioflux.earth/>Bioflux</a> on a separate biochar initiative — the Altitude deal is a direct commercialization of that production capacity.</p><h2 id=altitudes-south-american-strategy>Altitude&rsquo;s South American Strategy<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#altitudes-south-american-strategy>#</a></h2><p>Altitude is building a geographically diversified portfolio of high-quality CDR credits, with particular focus on <strong>South America, South East Asia, and West Africa</strong> — regions with abundant biomass feedstocks, lower production costs, and significant co-benefits for local communities and economies.</p><p>This isn&rsquo;t their first South American bet. Last year, Altitude locked in <a href=https://carbonherald.com/altitude-bets-big-on-biochar-locking-in-165000-tons-of-carbon-removal-in-argentina/>165,000 tonnes of biochar carbon removal in Argentina</a>. Combined with the Bolivia deal, they&rsquo;re building serious scale in the region.</p><p>CEO Daniel Benjamin Schulz framed the deal explicitly in terms of regional development: &ldquo;by sourcing CORCs from high-quality biochar projects in Bolivia, the company contributes to critical waste management and growth in these highly important regions.&rdquo;</p><h2 id=why-this-matters-beyond-the-numbers>Why This Matters Beyond the Numbers<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters-beyond-the-numbers>#</a></h2><p>The CDR market has a geographic concentration problem. Too much of the discussion — and too much of the capital — flows through North America and Northern Europe. The actual biomass feedstock potential, land availability, and cost competitiveness often points elsewhere.</p><p>Bolivia, specifically, has conditions that favour biochar at scale:</p><ul><li>Large-scale forestry and agricultural operations generating organic residues</li><li>Lower land and labour costs than comparable operations in Europe or North America</li><li>A growing policy interest in voluntary carbon markets — Empacar announced this deal at the <strong>2026 Bolivia Carbon Forum</strong>, which signals government-level engagement</li><li>Existing industrial infrastructure that can be repurposed</li></ul><p>For buyers of CDR credits, geographic diversification is also valuable. Credits from Bolivia versus Sweden versus the US have different co-benefit profiles and different regulatory/political risk exposures. Portfolio-level thinking favours spreading across regions.</p><h2 id=the-2027-credit-timeline>The 2027 Credit Timeline<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-2027-credit-timeline>#</a></h2><p>First credit issuance in 2027 gives Empacar time to complete facility development and go through Puro.earth&rsquo;s verification process. For Altitude and its eventual buyers, the 2027+ delivery timeline aligns with how most serious CDR buyers are thinking about their post-2030 removal requirements.</p><p>The voluntary market is building supply chains in parts of the world the industry barely noticed two years ago. That&rsquo;s what genuine scale looks like.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://carbonherald.com/altitude-signs-deal-with-empacar-s-a-for-over-305-000-tonnes-of-biochar-cdr/>Carbon Herald</a> | <a href=https://www.altitudecarbon.com/>Altitude Carbon</a> | <a href=https://www.empacar.com.bo/>Empacar</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/bolivia-biochar-facility-70000-tons/>Bolivia Gets Its First Large-Scale Biochar Facility — 70,000 Tons CO₂/Year</a></li><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/green-carbon-india-biochar-partnership/>Japan-India Biochar Alliance: Industrial-Scale CDR Comes to Asia</a></li></ul>
