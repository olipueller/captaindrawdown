<p>The number that gets thrown around to dismiss tech CDR goes like this: forests and soils remove roughly <strong>10 gigatonnes</strong> of CO₂ per year. Direct air capture removes about <strong>1.3 million tonnes</strong>. That&rsquo;s less than 0.02% of what natural systems do. So why bother?</p><p>The people making this argument have the data right and the logic backwards.</p><p>Natural carbon sinks doing 10 gigatonnes of work annually isn&rsquo;t an argument against tech CDR — it&rsquo;s a testament to how much biological infrastructure we&rsquo;ve built up over millions of years, and a reminder of how catastrophically we&rsquo;re undermining it. Net deforestation continues. The Amazon has regions that have flipped from carbon sink to carbon source. The 2023 fire season in Canada alone released more than a billion tonnes of CO₂. These aren&rsquo;t anomalies; they&rsquo;re a trend.</p><p>Natural systems are doing heroic work <em>under pressure</em>. They&rsquo;re not a stable baseline we can plan around — they&rsquo;re a stressed asset.</p><p>Tech CDR, at 1.3 million tonnes today, isn&rsquo;t trying to replace forests. It&rsquo;s building the infrastructure that exists when natural sinks fail — or when we need removal capacity that scales faster than ecosystems can grow. A forest planted today takes decades to reach its carbon storage potential. A DAC facility under construction today can be operational in 2-3 years. These aren&rsquo;t substitutes; they operate on different timescales with different risk profiles.</p><p>The scale comparison also obscures where tech CDR is in its development curve. Solar power in 2005 was a rounding error on global electricity capacity. The argument &ldquo;why bother with solar when coal does 99% of the work&rdquo; would have been wrong then for the same reason the natural-vs-tech framing is wrong now: it mistakes current scale for future trajectory.</p><p>The actual argument for urgency runs the other direction from what critics intend. If natural sinks are already removing 10 gigatonnes per year <em>and</em> we&rsquo;re still accumulating CO₂ in the atmosphere, that tells you something important about the scale of the problem. We&rsquo;re emitting roughly 37 gigatonnes annually. Natural sinks remove about 10. The math requires everything working together — preserved natural systems, accelerated reforestation, <em>and</em> tech CDR scaled by orders of magnitude.</p><p>We need both. The gigatonne gap doesn&rsquo;t argue against DAC. It argues against complacency about any of it.</p>
