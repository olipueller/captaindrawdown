**Carbon Herald just published [Capture6 Officially Launches Project Tortuga In Western Australia](https://carbonherald.com/capture6-officially-launches-project-tortuga-in-western-australia/).**

Carbon Herald reports that Capture6 has formally launched Project Tortuga in Western Australia, moving the project from planning into operations. The facility is designed to take brine, the salty waste stream left over from desalination, and process it to recover freshwater while generating the chemical inputs Capture6 uses for its direct air capture approach. The company has previously framed this water-plus-carbon model as a way to co-locate removal with existing desalination infrastructure and cut the cost of both. The Western Australian site has been described as a demonstration-scale step toward larger deployments, with the outlet covering the launch announcement and the project's stated goals rather than operating results.

_Our take (Heads-up):_ A brine-to-freshwater pathway that also feeds carbon removal is a sensible integration story, and Western Australia has the desalination base to test it. What is missing here is any operating data: tonnes captured, water recovered, energy use per tonne, or costs. Until Capture6 publishes measured performance from the running plant, treat this as a milestone announcement, not evidence the model scales.
