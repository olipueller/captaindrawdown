This chart is a horizontal bar plot where each bar is a CDR pathway and the length shows the share of that pathway's total employee headcount sitting inside its three largest companies. A bar near 80% means three firms carry most of the people working in that pathway; a bar near 20% means the workforce is spread across dozens of smaller teams.

Concentration matters because headcount is a rough proxy for where operational capacity, hiring pipelines, and institutional knowledge actually live. A raw company list flattens this - you see many logos and assume a healthy field, when in practice one or two players may hold the bulk of the expertise. Watching this ratio shift over time tells you whether a pathway is consolidating around incumbents or genuinely broadening.

Read it carefully. High concentration is not inherently bad, and low concentration is not inherently healthy - some pathways are capital-intensive and naturally cluster. This also reflects only companies in the Directory, and self-reported headcount has noise at the edges.

## What the chart shows today

DAC sits at the top of the concentration index with just 125 companies in the directory, meaning the top-3 names swallow a hefty share of pathway headcount. Biochar anchors the other end: 377 companies, the most fragmented pathway on the board, where the top-3 barely register against a long tail of small operators. Enabling Tech (179) and the smaller pathways like Mineralization (42), Enhanced Weathering (33), and Biomass Burial (31) cluster mid-to-high concentration, mostly because thin company counts mathematically force concentration up. The so-what: if you are sourcing DAC talent or doing competitive intel, three logos cover most of the labor pool, but in biochar you need a wide net to see the real market.
