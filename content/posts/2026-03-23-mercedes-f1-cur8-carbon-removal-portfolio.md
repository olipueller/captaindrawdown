<p>An F1 team just assembled one of the most diversified carbon removal portfolios in the corporate world. Not a tech company. Not an energy major. A racing team.</p><p>Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 announced a <a href=https://www.mercedesamgf1.com/news/team-sets-new-benchmark-for-sustainable-leadership-in-f1-expanding-carbon>major expansion of their carbon removal commitments</a>, adding 7 new projects across 6 distinct CDR technology types, curated by <a href=https://www.cur8.earth/>CUR8</a>. The total commitment: approximately 18,900 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent. The technology spread: Direct Air Capture, Biomass Carbon Removal and Storage, BECCS, Biochar, Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement, and <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>Enhanced Rock Weathering</a>.</p><p>That&rsquo;s not a token offset purchase. That&rsquo;s a real portfolio strategy.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>Most corporate carbon removal commitments are single-technology bets — usually a DAC pre-purchase or some forestry credits. Mercedes is doing something different: spreading risk across six fundamentally different approaches to pulling CO₂ out of the atmosphere. If one technology hits scaling problems, the others provide coverage. This is what the <a href=https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-01/Oxford-Offsetting-Principles-2020.pdf>Oxford Offsetting Principles</a> actually recommend, and Mercedes explicitly cites them as their guiding framework.</p><p>The geographic alignment is clever too. Projects span Brazil, Canada, the US, the UK, Denmark, and India — countries where the F1 circus actually races. Impact where you operate, not just where credits are cheapest.</p><h2 id=the-greenhushing-contrast>The Greenhushing Contrast<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-greenhushing-contrast>#</a></h2><p>Here&rsquo;s what makes this announcement stand out: timing. Across industries, companies are quietly walking back climate pledges. The phenomenon even has a name — &ldquo;greenhushing&rdquo; — where firms keep commitments but stop talking about them, or quietly drop targets altogether.</p><p>Mercedes is going the other direction. Alice Ashpitel, the team&rsquo;s Head of Sustainability, presented the strategy at Economist Impact&rsquo;s Sustainability Week. The target remains Race Team Control Net Zero by 2030.</p><p>Partners include CUR8 (portfolio curation), Carboneers (biochar), and InPlanet (<a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced rock weathering</a> in Brazil) — all names that serious CDR watchers will recognize.</p><h2 id=the-bigger-picture>The Bigger Picture<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-bigger-picture>#</a></h2><p>At ~18,900 tCO₂e, this isn&rsquo;t going to move the needle on global emissions. But that&rsquo;s not the point. The point is a high-visibility brand demonstrating what a credible, diversified removal portfolio looks like. Every company making net-zero pledges needs to eventually buy durable removal. Mercedes just published a blueprint for how to do it without putting all eggs in one basket.</p><p>For the CDR industry, the signal matters as much as the tonnes: there&rsquo;s real corporate demand for a mix of technologies, not just the cheapest available credit.</p>
