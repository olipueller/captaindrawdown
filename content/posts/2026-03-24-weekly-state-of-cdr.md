But the delivery gap is growing faster than procurement.</strong></p><hr><h2 id=the-gigaton-tracker>The Gigaton Tracker<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-gigaton-tracker>#</a></h2><table><thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>Value</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Spent on CO₂ removal</strong></td><td>$11.5B</td></tr><tr><td><strong>CO₂ sold</strong></td><td>44.3 Mt</td></tr><tr><td><strong>CO₂ delivered</strong></td><td>~1.2 Mt (2.7%)</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Active purchasers</strong></td><td>1,023</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Active suppliers</strong></td><td>735</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Target</strong></td><td>10 Gt/year by 2050</td></tr></tbody></table><p><strong>The gap:</strong> 1.2 Mt delivered vs. 10 Gt target = <strong>0.012% of the way there</strong>. Reaching the floor requires 35x acceleration over 24 years. The industry has built an enormous procurement apparatus. It has not built removal capacity yet.</p><hr><h2 id=the-headline-take>The Headline Take<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-headline-take>#</a></h2><p>This was a <strong>DEALS week</strong>. Google signed 200,000 tonnes of biochar through AMP. Microsoft committed to deep ocean CDR with Vaulted Deep (~$1B). Tapestry locked in a 10-year DAC deal with Climeworks. Mercedes F1 diversified across 7 projects spanning 6 technologies. Canada became the first government to directly purchase CDR credits ($10M CAD), Germany mapped 95 Mt/year of removal potential, and India opened a carbon market portal to 1.4 billion people.</p><p>But the signal behind the noise: <strong>the 2.7% delivery rate is the elephant in the room.</strong> For every tonne contracted, only 0.027 tonnes have been removed, verified, and delivered. The gap between promised and done isn&rsquo;t just a bottleneck — it&rsquo;s structural. And it&rsquo;s growing.</p><hr><h2 id=this-week-in-cdr>This Week in CDR<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#this-week-in-cdr>#</a></h2><h3 id=a-the-procurement-surge>A. The Procurement Surge<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#a-the-procurement-surge>#</a></h3><p>Corporate CDR is no longer PR purchases — it&rsquo;s becoming a supply chain. Google&rsquo;s 200k-tonne biochar deal with AMP is a bet on commercial-scale removal, not a photo op (<a href=/posts/google-amp-waste-biochar-200k-tons-carbon-removal/>read more</a>). Microsoft&rsquo;s ~$1B commitment to Vaulted Deep signals ocean pathways graduating from curiosity to commercial reality (<a href=/posts/microsoft-vaulted-deep-billion-cdr/>read more</a>). Mercedes F1 assembled the most sophisticated portfolio we&rsquo;ve seen — seven projects, six technologies — hedging because nobody knows which pathways actually scale. Verde&rsquo;s 38,500 tonnes/year of road biochar is the kind of application that scales silently: you need roads everywhere (<a href=/posts/verde-bioasphalt-biochar-roads-carbon-sinks/>read more</a>). And Big Tech combined purchased 68.4M removal credits in 2025, up 181% YoY. At this trajectory, Big Tech alone hits 1 Gt/year around 2035 — if those credits represent real removal.</p><h3 id=b-governments-waking-up-at-very-different-speeds>B. Governments: Waking Up at Very Different Speeds<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#b-governments-waking-up-at-very-different-speeds>#</a></h3><p>Canada made history as the first government to directly buy CDR credits — pivoting from carbon pricing to financing removal (<a href=/posts/canada-first-government-cdr-procurement/>read more</a>). Germany released engineering assessments mapping 95 Mt/year removal potential by 2045, suggesting formal targets are coming (<a href=/posts/germany-cdr-potential-95mt-2045/>read more</a>). India launched the Prakriti portal, potentially bringing 1.4B people into a carbon removal market — enormous and almost completely under the radar. The EU is finalizing <a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a> technical rules, and Sweden committed $34M in removal-focused industrial policy. Meanwhile, the US $3.5B DAC hub program is stuck in DOE audit limbo — no deployment, no credits, no construction starts. The first nations to build removal infrastructure will have cost advantages for decades.</p><h3 id=c-science-complicating-the-easy-narratives>C. Science Complicating the Easy Narratives<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#c-science-complicating-the-easy-narratives>#</a></h3><p>Princeton found many BECCS pathways may emit MORE CO₂ than natural gas over their lifetime, depending on sourcing assumptions — a body blow to net-zero models that assume BECCS is carbon-negative (<a href=/posts/beccs-higher-emissions-than-natural-gas/>read more</a>). CATF reviewed 25 biomass CDR protocols and found most have notable methodological flaws — an accounting crisis waiting to happen. EGU 2026 showed that adding organic carbon to <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a> reactions changes reactivity in ways not currently modeled, though Wageningen data suggests biochar + basalt co-deployment may have synergistic effects (<a href=/posts/enhanced-weathering-biochar-co-deployment/>read more</a>). None of this kills the pathways — it matures them. But BECCS needs a credibility reset, biochar needs protocol standards, and enhanced weathering needs better interaction modeling.</p><h3 id=d-dac-breakthrough-materials-vs-political-reality>D. DAC: Breakthrough Materials vs. Political Reality<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#d-dac-breakthrough-materials-vs-political-reality>#</a></h3><p>Three significant material advances in one week: a wood-waste sorbent with solar-powered CO₂ release (no thermal heating), dual-function materials that capture AND convert CO₂ in one step, and humidity-swing polymers reducing energy penalties by 40-60%. If any scale, DAC costs drop 20-30% in 18-24 months. But US policy is stalled while Australia and Japan signed a DAC + hydrogen partnership ($200M deployment target) and Climeworks keeps signing corporate contracts. Also notable: CDR had its own stage at CERAWeek for the first time in 60 years. The energy industry now treats removal as infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.</p><hr><h2 id=signal-vs-noise>Signal vs. Noise<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#signal-vs-noise>#</a></h2><p><strong>Signal:</strong> India&rsquo;s Prakriti Portal — 1.4B people suddenly able to trade carbon credits is a systemic market-opening, not a gesture. Bolivia&rsquo;s first biochar facility (70,000 t/year) — CDR is reaching new continents.</p><p><strong>Noise:</strong> Carbonology&rsquo;s &ldquo;jet fuel from air and sunlight&rdquo; — extraordinary claims, zero peer-reviewed evidence. The &ldquo;95% nature-based CDR credits&rdquo; stat — technically true but misleading; the durable market is growing fast from a near-zero base.</p><hr><h2 id=pathway-pulse>Pathway Pulse<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#pathway-pulse>#</a></h2><p><strong>Enhanced Weathering:</strong> Wageningen co-deployment data promising. EGU complicating reactivity models. Maturing from speculation to engineering. <em>Accelerating.</em></p><p><strong>Direct Air Capture:</strong> Three breakthrough materials in one week. Science fast, US policy stalled, rest of world deploying. <em>Bifurcated.</em></p><p><strong>Biochar:</strong> Google deal, Bolivia facility, Verde roads, 86% of delivered durable CDR. Not sexy, but real. <em>Stable growth.</em></p><p><strong>BECCS:</strong> Princeton study + protocol flaws = credibility reset needed before massive deployment. <em>Credibility risk.</em></p><p><strong>Ocean CDR:</strong> Microsoft-Vaulted Deep is huge. OAE mussel data encouraging. <em>Breakthrough week.</em></p><p><strong>Soil Carbon:</strong> EGU data on organic carbon interactions. Biggest field, fewest credits. <em>Research-phase.</em></p><p><strong>Biomass Storage:</strong> 17-company coalition formed. Early but organized. <em>Emergent.</em></p><hr><h2 id=the-long-view>The Long View<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-long-view>#</a></h2><p>44.3 Mt sold. 1.2 Mt delivered. 2.7%. The entire industry is pre-revenue in the traditional sense. Solar panels followed a similar lag in the 2000s — manufacturing scaled faster than installation, the gap lasted 2-3 years before closing. CDR is harder: you can&rsquo;t stockpile removal credits like panels in a warehouse. Every credit requires real-time verification and permanence assurance over decades. MRV is the rate-limiter, not demand. Reaching 1 Gt/year by 2050 requires 35x acceleration — not impossible (solar scaled 300x in 15 years), but solar had a clear cost-curve driver. Some CDR pathways will drop 50%+ with volume; others will stay expensive but essential. The question: Canada, Germany, EU, India, Sweden, and Japan all signaled this week they&rsquo;re treating removal as infrastructure. If that translates into deployment targets — not just purchase commitments — the delivery gap closes faster than the numbers suggest.</p><hr><h2 id=what-were-watching-next-week>What We&rsquo;re Watching Next Week<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-were-watching-next-week>#</a></h2><ul><li><strong>EU Parliament Climate Committee Hearings:</strong> CRCF implementation discussion.
