<p>While Washington is busy dismantling climate policy, Brussels is quietly building the market architecture that could make carbon dioxide removal a viable industry. A new paper published in the journal <em>Joule</em> on March 31st makes the case — with numbers.</p><h2 id=the-study>The Study<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-study>#</a></h2><p>Led by Darius Sultani and Michael Pahle at the <a href=https://www.pik-potsdam.de/en/home>Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research</a> (PIK), the paper — <em>How the EU can utilize its carbon market to scale up Carbon Dioxide Removal</em> — models what happens when CDR technologies are integrated into the EU Emissions Trading System.</p><p>The headline finding: <strong>around 60 million tonnes of CO₂ removals per year by 2050</strong>, primarily through direct air capture (DAC) and bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS). Potentially higher, depending on how fast costs fall.</p><p>That number matters. 60 Mt/yr is roughly the point at which CDR stops being a niche demonstration activity and starts being infrastructure — comparable in scale to entire national emissions budgets.</p><h2 id=why-ets-not-subsidies>Why ETS, Not Subsidies?<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-ets-not-subsidies>#</a></h2><p>The authors&rsquo; core argument is that markets do what grants cannot: they provide <strong>long-run price certainty</strong>.</p><p>DAC and BECCS are capital-intensive. The economics only work if developers can count on a carbon price floor years into the future. Grant-by-grant government support — however generous — doesn&rsquo;t provide that signal. A carbon price built into ETS does.</p><p>&ldquo;Using a market-based instrument makes sense,&rdquo; said Sultani, &ldquo;given limited fiscal space and growing pressure to meet climate neutrality targets.&rdquo;</p><p>The EU faces a fiscal bind: lots of climate ambition, not infinite budget. ETS revenue is already substantial. Extending the price signal to cover removals is, in principle, free to governments — the market does the incentivising.</p><h2 id=the-permanence-guarantee-question>The &ldquo;Permanence Guarantee&rdquo; Question<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-permanence-guarantee-question>#</a></h2><p>The obvious concern: what happens if removals are used as an excuse to weaken emissions cuts? This is the classic offset loophole problem that has plagued carbon markets since Kyoto.</p><p>The PIK study addresses it directly. Within a <strong>capped system</strong>, the risk is structurally limited. The total allowances in the system are fixed. If removals underperform, carbon prices rise, which forces additional emissions reductions elsewhere. The cap does the disciplining.</p><p>The authors do flag one real risk: political pressure to raise the cap in response to rising prices. That&rsquo;s not a market failure — it&rsquo;s a governance failure. The paper calls for safeguards, including sustainability standards for BECCS (excessive biomass demand is a real concern for land use and biodiversity).</p><h2 id=phased-integration--three-stages>Phased Integration — Three Stages<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#phased-integration--three-stages>#</a></h2><p>The proposal isn&rsquo;t &ldquo;throw CDR into ETS immediately.&rdquo; It&rsquo;s a careful step-by-step:</p><ol><li><strong>Phase 1 (now → ~2035):</strong> Build the infrastructure — robust MRV systems, sustainability standards, baseline data. Get the plumbing right before turning on the tap.</li><li><strong>Phase 2 (~2035–2040):</strong> Introduce removal credits gradually, with volume limits and scope restrictions. Let the market learn.</li><li><strong>Phase 3 (~2040 onward):</strong> Full integration — removals and residual emissions governed by a single carbon price, the ETS cap adjusted for net-zero ambition.</li></ol><p>That&rsquo;s a realistic timeline. It matches the EU&rsquo;s own Carbon Removal Certification Framework (<a href=/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/>CRCF</a>) trajectory and the European Commission&rsquo;s pledge to decide on ETS inclusion of removals by 2026.</p><h2 id=the-contrast-with-washington>The Contrast with Washington<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-contrast-with-washington>#</a></h2><p>It would be easy to frame this as &ldquo;Europe vs the US.&rdquo; It&rsquo;s more structural than that. The EU has spent 20 years building a carbon pricing infrastructure. It has institutional memory, regulatory capacity, and political will to use price signals as the primary climate tool.</p><p>Whatever direction US federal policy takes, that infrastructure exists. And it can be extended to removals.</p><p>CDR investors looking for durable demand signals know where to look: Brussels, not Washington.</p><h2 id=what-this-means-for-the-cdr-ecosystem>What This Means for the CDR Ecosystem<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means-for-the-cdr-ecosystem>#</a></h2><p>If the European Commission follows through — and the PIK paper gives policymakers a concrete mechanism to do so — the implications are significant:</p><ul><li><strong>DAC developers</strong> gain a credible long-run revenue model in Europe, independent of US DOE funding cycles</li><li><strong>BECCS projects</strong> (like those at Drax and Stockholm Exergi) can price their removal credits against a transparent market benchmark</li><li><strong>Smaller CDR methods</strong> (<a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced weathering</a>, ocean alkalinity enhancement) that earn certificates under the CRCF could eventually plug into the same price signal</li></ul><p>The 60 million tonne number is a model output, not a guarantee. Cost trends, political decisions, and technological progress will all shape the actual outcome. But the architecture being proposed is coherent — and that&rsquo;s more than most CDR policy proposals can claim.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/eu-carbon-market-could-drive-next-phase-of-climate-action-by-scaling-up-co-removals-study-finds>Down to Earth</a> | Sultani et al., Joule, March 2026</em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/canada-first-government-cdr-procurement/>Canada Becomes First Government to Buy CDR Credits</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-ets-suspension-italy-summit/>EU Carbon Market Under Fire: Italy Wants the ETS Suspended</a></li><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Goes All-In on Carbon Removal — 7 New Projects Across 6 Pathways</a></li><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Builds One of Motorsport&rsquo;s Biggest CDR Portfolios — 18,900 Tonnes Across 6 Pathways</a></li></ul>
