<p>Svante Technologies just moved a planned BECCS facility at a paper mill in the southeastern United States into the feasibility study stage. The target: capturing and permanently storing more than 500,000 metric tonnes of biogenic CO₂ every year.</p><p>To put that number in context — Climeworks&rsquo; entire Mammoth plant in Iceland, the world&rsquo;s largest operational DAC facility, is designed for 36,000 tonnes per year. This single project would exceed that by more than 13×.</p><h2 id=why-paper-mills-are-beccs-gold>Why Paper Mills Are BECCS Gold<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-paper-mills-are-beccs-gold>#</a></h2><p>Pulp and paper mills are almost purpose-built for bioenergy with carbon capture. The biomass feedstock is already on-site — wood from sustainably managed forests gets processed into paper, and the resulting flue gas is rich in biogenic CO₂. Capture that, store it permanently, and you&rsquo;ve got carbon dioxide removal credits without needing to source new biomass.</p><p>The project will use Svante&rsquo;s solid sorbent technology: rotary contactor machines fitted with nanoengineered filters that selectively grab CO₂ from industrial flue gas. It&rsquo;s a different approach from the liquid solvent and amine-based systems dominating CCS — potentially faster cycling and lower energy penalties.</p><h2 id=storage-and-design>Storage and Design<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#storage-and-design>#</a></h2><p>Captured CO₂ would be transported to the US Gulf Coast for permanent geological storage, leveraging existing regulatory frameworks for Class VI injection wells. The Gulf Coast&rsquo;s well-characterized subsurface formations make it one of the most storage-ready regions in the world.</p><p>The facility design also includes waste heat and water recovery systems to reduce the additional energy and water demands of the capture process — a critical detail, since energy parasitism is one of the main criticisms of bolt-on carbon capture.</p><h2 id=svantes-beccs-strategy>Svante&rsquo;s BECCS Strategy<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#svantes-beccs-strategy>#</a></h2><p>This isn&rsquo;t a one-off. Svante is systematically targeting the pulp and paper sector for BECCS deployment. Just last week, the company <a href=/posts/svante-acquires-carbon-alpha-beccs/>acquired Carbon Alpha</a> to develop a BECCS hub in Western Canada with First Nations partnership.</p><p>The strategy makes sense. Pulp and paper is one of the few heavy industries where the CO₂ is biogenic by default. In most CCS applications, you&rsquo;re preventing fossil carbon from reaching the atmosphere — useful, but not removal. In BECCS at a paper mill, you&rsquo;re taking carbon that trees already pulled from the air and locking it underground permanently. That&rsquo;s genuine CDR.</p><h2 id=what-to-watch>What to Watch<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-to-watch>#</a></h2><p>The project is still in feasibility — detailed engineering, cost estimates, and risk assessments are ongoing. No construction timeline yet. But 500,000 tonnes per year from a single facility would be one of the largest CDR projects anywhere in the world.</p><p>The credits are intended to meet established MRV standards and target organizations offsetting Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. If this project reaches final investment decision, it&rsquo;ll be a major signal that BECCS in established industrial sectors is commercially viable — not just technically possible.</p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href=https://carbonherald.com/svante-advances-new-beccs-project-at-us-paper-mill/>Carbon Herald</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/svante-acquires-carbon-alpha-beccs/>Svante Acquires Carbon Alpha: BECCS Meets First Nations Partnership</a></li><li><a href=/posts/carbon-centric-beccs-certification/>Carbon Centric&rsquo;s BECCS Certification</a></li><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li></ul>
