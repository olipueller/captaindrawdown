<p>A milestone day for carbon markets: one buyer crossed 1 million tonnes financed, new research turned industrial waste into a CO₂ sink, and Europe&rsquo;s financial sector quietly showed up for certified removal.</p><hr><h2 id=what-we-covered-today>What We Covered Today<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-we-covered-today>#</a></h2><p><strong><a href=/posts/altitude-1-million-tonnes-cdr-financed-bolivia-empacar/>Altitude Carbon Reaches 1 Million Tonnes Financed — With Bolivia</a></strong></p><p>This is the number the CDR market has been building toward. Altitude Carbon, a CDI portfolio company, became the world&rsquo;s first commercial CDR buyer to cross 1 million tonnes financed — pushed over the line by a 305,000-tonne biochar deal with Empacar S.A. in Bolivia. What makes this notable isn&rsquo;t just the size: it&rsquo;s that Altitude is operating as a market-maker at scale, financing CDR across Southeast Asia, Bolivia, and beyond. The 1 million tonne mark is a proof point that commercial demand for durable carbon removal can actually accumulate.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/steel-cement-alkaline-wastewater-carbon-sequestration-30mt/>Steel and Cement Wastewater as a 30-Million-Tonne CO₂ Sink</a></strong></p><p>New research in <em>Environmental Science & Technology Letters</em> makes a counterintuitive case: the alkaline wastewater generated by steel and cement production is an asset, not just a disposal problem. By injecting CO₂ into these high-pH streams, the dissolved carbon mineralises permanently. The theoretical ceiling: <strong>30 million tonnes per year</strong> — using industrial infrastructure that already exists. The catch is scale and co-ordination between hard-to-abate industries and CDR operators, but the chemistry works.</p><p><strong><a href=/posts/biochar-italian-good-in-town-cdr-carbon-removal/>CDR Goes Italian: Biochar Explained for a New Audience</a></strong></p><p><em>The Good in Town</em>, an Italian-language sustainability magazine, published a solid biochar explainer this week — evidence that CDR literacy is spreading beyond English-speaking audiences.
