<p>&ldquo;DAC removes 1.3 million tonnes per year. A single good year of global forest growth removes 10 gigatonnes. Why are we spending billions on machines when we could just plant trees?&rdquo;</p><p>This framing shows up constantly, and it&rsquo;s wrong in a specific way that&rsquo;s worth unpacking — because the people making the argument often genuinely care about climate outcomes.</p><p>Afforestation and DAC don&rsquo;t compete for the same slot in the climate portfolio. They address different risk profiles, operate on different timescales, and fail in different ways. The question isn&rsquo;t which one to choose. The question is how to use each where it actually works.</p><p>Forests have constraints that tech CDR doesn&rsquo;t. Land is the obvious one — meaningful afforestation at scale requires enormous areas, and those areas compete with agriculture, existing ecosystems, and human settlement. Permanence is another: a tree planted today is a carbon sink for decades, but it&rsquo;s also a future wildfire, beetle outbreak, or drought waiting to happen. The 2023 Canadian fire season released over a billion tonnes of CO₂ from forest carbon that had been accumulating for years. Natural sinks aren&rsquo;t guaranteed storage — they&rsquo;re biological systems that respond to climate stress in ways we&rsquo;re only starting to model well.</p><p>DAC, by contrast, can operate on degraded land with no soil requirements. The carbon it captures can be stored geologically in basalt formations where it mineralizes within years. That&rsquo;s a different permanence guarantee than a living forest.</p><p>The timescale argument cuts both ways. Critics of DAC point out it&rsquo;s still expensive and small-scale. True. But a forest planted today won&rsquo;t hit significant carbon accumulation for 20-30 years. A DAC facility financed today can be operational within 2-3 years. Under scenarios where we need rapid carbon removal to hit specific temperature targets, that lead time matters.</p><p>The strongest argument for both working together: we&rsquo;re in a position where we need every tool to work. We&rsquo;re emitting ~37 gigatonnes per year. Natural sinks remove roughly 10. We&rsquo;re not choosing between two options that could each solve the problem alone — we&rsquo;re assembling a toolkit where every component needs to contribute.</p><p>Planting trees is good. Protecting existing forests is essential. Building DAC infrastructure is a hedge against the failure modes of both. These aren&rsquo;t competitors. They&rsquo;re the same team.</p>
