<h2 id=microsoft-bought-93-of-all-carbon-removal-credits-last-year>Microsoft Bought 93% of All Carbon Removal Credits Last Year<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#microsoft-bought-93-of-all-carbon-removal-credits-last-year>#</a></h2><p>New data from BloombergNEF and the Business Council for Sustainable Energy confirms what many suspected: Microsoft is essentially <em>the</em> carbon removal market. The tech giant purchased 93% of all global CDR credits in 2025, driven by its pledge to remove every tonne of CO₂ the company has emitted since 1975.</p><p>That kind of anchor buying — paying $500+ per tonne for high-quality DAC and engineered removal — is keeping startups like Climeworks and Heirloom alive and scaling. But one buyer propping up an entire sector isn&rsquo;t a strategy. It&rsquo;s a lifeline. Governments need to step up as procurement partners if this market is going to mature beyond Microsoft&rsquo;s goodwill. (<a href=https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/microsoft-is-the-carbon-removal-market/>Latitude Media</a>)</p><h2 id=-gulf-of-maine-ocean-alkalinity-trial-co-removed-no-immediate-harm>🌊 Gulf of Maine Ocean Alkalinity Trial: CO₂ Removed, No Immediate Harm<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-gulf-of-maine-ocean-alkalinity-trial-co-removed-no-immediate-harm>#</a></h2><p>Researchers dissolved sodium hydroxide into coastal waters off Maine and measured removal of up to 10 tonnes of CO₂ — with no detectable acute harm to marine life. It&rsquo;s a small-scale proof of concept, not a green light for deployment, but it&rsquo;s real-world data on a pathway that&rsquo;s been mostly theoretical until now. Longer trials, broader monitoring, and the sticky question of ocean governance still lie ahead. A mobile test unit at Jubail Industrial College has been collecting data for three months, confirming strong performance in coastal conditions — after earlier tests in Riyadh&rsquo;s arid desert also delivered. The Kingdom is positioning itself as a serious DAC deployment location. Without better monitoring, we&rsquo;re making climate decisions with incomplete data.
