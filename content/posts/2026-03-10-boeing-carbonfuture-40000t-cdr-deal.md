<p>Boeing just signed one of the aviation sector&rsquo;s largest carbon removal procurement deals ever: at least 40,000 tonnes of durable CDR credits through Carbonfuture, sourced from biochar projects across the Global South.</p><p>That number matters. Most corporate CDR deals still hover in the hundreds or low thousands of tonnes. Forty thousand signals that a Fortune 50 company views carbon removal not as a PR checkbox, but as a core decarbonization tool for hard-to-abate emissions.</p><h2 id=why-biochar-why-now>Why Biochar, Why Now?<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-biochar-why-now>#</a></h2><p>The credits come from four biochar projects — organic waste converted to stable charcoal through pyrolysis, then applied to soil. The carbon stays locked for centuries, not decades. And unlike DAC, biochar actually improves soil fertility where it&rsquo;s applied, making it one of the few CDR methods that generates co-benefits in the communities hosting the projects.</p><p>Boeing is specifically using these credits to offset residual Scope 3 emissions from business travel — the kind of emissions that can&rsquo;t yet be solved through aircraft efficiency gains or sustainable aviation fuel alone.</p><h2 id=carbonfutures-role>Carbonfuture&rsquo;s Role<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#carbonfutures-role>#</a></h2><p>Carbonfuture isn&rsquo;t producing the biochar itself. It operates the digital infrastructure that tracks carbon removal from production to final soil application. Every credit has a verifiable chain of custody — a critical feature as the CDR market matures and buyers demand real transparency, not just certificates.</p><p>CDI has invested in Carbonfuture as part of our portfolio. We&rsquo;ve written about <a href=https://www.carbon-drawdown.de/blog/2025-10-9-portfolio-spotlight-carbonfuture-a-digital-infrastructure-for-carbon-removal>their infrastructure approach before</a>.</p><h2 id=what-this-means-for-cdr-markets>What This Means for CDR Markets<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-this-means-for-cdr-markets>#</a></h2><p>A deal this size from Boeing validates a few things simultaneously:</p><ul><li><strong>Biochar is real.</strong> Not experimental, not pilot-stage. Deliverable at scale, right now.</li><li><strong>Diversified portfolios reduce risk.</strong> Boeing isn&rsquo;t betting on one project — it&rsquo;s spreading across four. Smart procurement.</li><li><strong>Aviation needs CDR.</strong> Under frameworks like CORSIA, airlines and manufacturers can&rsquo;t electrify their way to net zero. Carbon removal is the bridge.</li></ul><p>The CDR market is still tiny relative to what&rsquo;s needed. But when Boeing drops 40,000 tonnes on the table, other corporate buyers notice.</p><hr><p><em>Sources: <a href=https://esgnews.com/boeing-secures-40000-tonnes-of-durable-carbon-removal-credits-in-multi-year-deal-with-carbonfuture/>ESG News</a>, <a href=https://carbonherald.com/boeing-inks-a-new-deal-with-carbonfuture-for-one-of-aviations-boldest-cdr-procurements/>Carbon Herald</a>, <a href=https://www.businessgreen.com/news/4526608/boeing-lands-deal-remove-tonnes-co2>BusinessGreen</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/biochar-quiet-giant-of-cdr/>Biochar: The Quiet Giant of Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/boeing-carbonfuture-40k-tonnes/>Boeing Bets Big on Biochar — 40,000 Tonnes of Carbon Removal via Carbonfuture</a></li><li><a href=/posts/exomad-green-300k-biochar-credits/>Exomad Green Hits 300,000 Biochar Carbon Removal Credits</a></li><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li></ul>
