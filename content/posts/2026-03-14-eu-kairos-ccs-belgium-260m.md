<p>The European Commission has approved <strong>€260 million ($298M)</strong> in Belgian state aid for the <strong>Kairos@C</strong> carbon capture and storage project in Antwerp.</p><p>The project, run by <strong>Air Liquide</strong> and <strong>BASF</strong>, will capture CO₂ from hydrogen, ammonia, and ethylene oxide production, then transport it for permanent geological storage beneath the North Sea.</p><h2 id=scale-and-timeline>Scale and Timeline<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#scale-and-timeline>#</a></h2><p>Over a projected <strong>15-year operating period</strong>, the project is expected to prevent roughly <strong>20 million tonnes</strong> of greenhouse gas emissions from entering the atmosphere.</p><p>The funding structure is performance-linked: each company receives an initial €30M investment grant, followed by annual payments of €10M over a decade — but only if minimum emissions-reduction thresholds are met.</p><h2 id=this-isnt-new--its-bigger>This Isn&rsquo;t New — It&rsquo;s Bigger<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#this-isnt-new--its-bigger>#</a></h2><p>Kairos@C previously received more than <strong>€365 million</strong> from the EU Innovation Fund back in 2020 to demonstrate large-scale low-carbon technologies. But rising project costs linked to inflation meant additional public funding was required.</p><p>Total EU-backed investment now exceeds <strong>€625 million</strong> for a single cross-border CCS value chain.</p><h2 id=the-cdr-angle>The CDR Angle<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-cdr-angle>#</a></h2><p>This is CCS, not CDR — it captures emissions at source rather than removing atmospheric CO₂. But the infrastructure being built (transport, storage beneath the North Sea) is the same infrastructure that BECCS and DACCS projects will need.</p><p>Every pipeline laid, every storage site permitted, every monitoring protocol established reduces the barrier for future carbon <em>removal</em> projects. Belgium and the Netherlands are building the plumbing that CDR will eventually use.</p><h2 id=broader-context>Broader Context<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#broader-context>#</a></h2><p>This comes as eight EU countries — including Spain and the Netherlands — <a href=https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/eight-countries-warn-eu-not-weaken-carbon-market-document-shows-2026-03-12/>warned the EU</a> not to weaken the Emissions Trading System, even as Italy pushes to suspend it. The EU&rsquo;s carbon infrastructure buildout is accelerating despite political headwinds.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://www.eureporter.co/eu-2/belgium/2026/03/13/commission-approves-e260-million-belgian-state-aid-for-carbon-capture-and-storage-project/>EU Reporter</a> · <a href=https://carbonherald.com/eu-approves-298m-belgian-state-aid-for-kairosc-ccs-project/>Carbon Herald</a></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/eu-industrial-accelerator-act/>The EU&rsquo;s Industrial Accelerator Act: What It Means for Carbon Removal</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-ets-suspension-italy-summit/>EU Carbon Market Under Fire: Italy Wants the ETS Suspended</a></li><li><a href=/posts/eu-%5Bcrcf%5D(/posts/eu-first-cdr-certification-standard/)-carbon-removal-certification/>The EU Just Built the World&rsquo;s First Government CDR Credit Label</a></li><li><a href=/posts/cdr-roundup-feb-2026/>CDR Roundup: What&rsquo;s Moving in Carbon Removal (Feb 2026)</a></li></ul>
