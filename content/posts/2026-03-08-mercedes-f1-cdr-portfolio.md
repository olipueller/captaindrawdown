<p>Just ahead of the 2026 F1 season opener, Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS has announced what may be motorsport&rsquo;s most comprehensive carbon removal investment to date: <strong>7 new CDR projects across 6 different technology pathways</strong>, bringing the team&rsquo;s total portfolio to approximately <strong>18,900 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent</strong>.</p><p>The technologies span the full CDR spectrum:</p><ul><li><strong>Direct Air Capture (DAC)</strong></li><li><strong>Biochar</strong></li><li><strong><a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>Enhanced Rock Weathering</a> (<a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>ERW</a>)</strong></li><li><strong>Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement (OAE)</strong></li><li><strong>Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS)</strong></li><li><strong>Biomass Storage</strong></li></ul><p>Projects are located across <strong>Brazil, Canada, the US, the UK, Denmark, and India</strong> — deliberately overlapping with regions where the F1 circus races.</p><h2 id=why-this-is-significant>Why This Is Significant<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-is-significant>#</a></h2><p>At 18,900 tonnes, this isn&rsquo;t offsetting greenwash. It&rsquo;s a serious commitment to <em>durable</em> carbon removal — the kind measured in centuries, not growing seasons. The portfolio is curated by <a href=https://cur8.earth/>CUR8</a>, which evaluates and verifies each project for delivery reliability.</p><p>Alice Ashpitel, Head of Sustainability at Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS, put it clearly during <a href=https://carbonherald.com/the-mercedes-amg-petronas-f1-team-expands-its-global-cdr-portfolio/>Economist Impact&rsquo;s Sustainability Week</a>: &ldquo;Emissions reduction remains our priority, and high-quality carbon removals are essential for tackling the residual emissions that remain.&rdquo;</p><p>The team isn&rsquo;t trying to offset its way to net zero. It&rsquo;s reducing first, then using carbon removal for the residual — which is exactly how serious climate strategies should work.</p><h2 id=the-cdr-market-signal>The CDR Market Signal<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-cdr-market-signal>#</a></h2><p>When a global sports brand invests across six different CDR pathways simultaneously, that&rsquo;s a portfolio strategy, not a PR stunt. It diversifies technology risk while channeling capital to early-stage projects that need demand signals to scale.</p><p>Mercedes&rsquo;s partners — Signify, UBS, and Nasdaq — are watching. And the explicit name-drop of Microsoft and Meta AI as &ldquo;industry leaders&rdquo; in CDR procurement suggests the F1 team sees itself in that league.</p><p>For the CDR industry, corporate buyers like Mercedes help bridge the gap between laboratory promise and commercial reality. Every multi-year purchase agreement de-risks the next one.</p><p>And for F1 fans: the cars may run on <a href=https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/f1-petronas-fuel-for-mercedes-pus-has-been-approved-by-the-fia-/10801931/>newly approved sustainable fuel</a>, but the team&rsquo;s climate strategy extends well beyond the tailpipe.</p><hr><p><em>Source: <a href=https://carbonherald.com/the-mercedes-amg-petronas-f1-team-expands-its-global-cdr-portfolio/>Carbon Herald — The Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team Expands Its Global CDR Portfolio</a> · <a href=https://www.mercedesamgf1.com/news/team-sets-new-benchmark-for-sustainable-leadership-in-f1-expanding-carbon>Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS official announcement</a></em></p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Goes All-In on Carbon Removal — 7 New Projects Across 6 Pathways</a></li><li><a href=/posts/germany-98m-cdr-budget/>Germany Puts €98 Million Into CDR — Its First Dedicated Federal Budget Line Ever</a></li><li><a href=/posts/nature-cdr-resource-limits/>New Nature Paper: Scaling CDR to Gigatons Could Strain Global Mineral and Nutrient Supplies</a></li><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li></ul>
