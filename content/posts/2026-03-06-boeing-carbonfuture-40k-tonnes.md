<p>Boeing just signed one of the aviation sector&rsquo;s largest carbon removal procurements ever: at least 40,000 tonnes of durable CDR through <a href=https://www.carbonfuture.earth/>Carbonfuture</a>, sourced from four biochar projects across the Global South.</p><p><strong>Full disclosure:</strong> Carbonfuture is a <a href=https://www.carbon-drawdown.de/blog/2025-10-9-portfolio-spotlight-carbonfuture-a-digital-infrastructure-for-carbon-removal>Carbon Drawdown Initiative portfolio company</a>. We&rsquo;ve been tracking their progress closely, and this deal validates exactly the kind of infrastructure the CDR market needs.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters>Why This Matters<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters>#</a></h2><p>Aviation is one of the hardest sectors to decarbonize. Planes can&rsquo;t run on batteries (not yet, anyway), and sustainable aviation fuels are still scaling up. So for residual emissions — particularly Scope 3 business travel — durable carbon removal is the only honest answer.</p><p>Boeing chose biochar for good reasons. Compared to DAC ($400–1,000/tonne), biochar removals are more affordable and already delivering at scale. The portfolio spans multiple projects across the Global South, reducing concentration risk while supporting CDR deployment in regions where it creates co-benefits: soil improvement, waste management, and local employment.</p><h2 id=the-carbonfuture-advantage>The Carbonfuture Advantage<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-carbonfuture-advantage>#</a></h2><p>What makes this deal different from a generic offset purchase is the infrastructure behind it. Carbonfuture&rsquo;s Trust Infrastructure tracks every step — from biomass pyrolysis to soil application — creating a chain of custody that buyers like Boeing increasingly demand.</p><p>This is what the CDR market looks like when it works: a credible platform connecting large-scale demand to verified supply, with digital MRV that makes credit quality transparent rather than aspirational.</p><h2 id=the-bigger-picture>The Bigger Picture<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-bigger-picture>#</a></h2><p>Boeing joins a growing list of corporate buyers making multi-year CDR commitments: Microsoft, LEGO, Mercedes F1, Shopify. The pattern is clear — large companies are moving beyond one-off purchases toward portfolio approaches with diversified pathways.</p><p>The question now shifts to supply. Can biochar projects scale fast enough to meet commitments like this? Carbonfuture&rsquo;s Global South portfolio suggests the answer is yes — if the right infrastructure exists to ensure quality at scale.</p><p>40,000 tonnes won&rsquo;t solve aviation&rsquo;s climate problem. But it&rsquo;s a real number, backed by real verification, from a company with real skin in the game.
