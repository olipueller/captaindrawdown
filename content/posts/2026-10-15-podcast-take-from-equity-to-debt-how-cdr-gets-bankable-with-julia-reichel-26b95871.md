> Take on a podcast episode from **The CDR Policy Scoop**, originally published Mon, 12 Oc.
> Listen: <https://shows.acast.com/the-cdr-policy-scoop/episodes/from-equity-to-debt-how-cdr-gets-bankable-with-julia-reichel>

**TL;DR**

- Vaulted Deep's $35M Mediobanca debt facility was built on three legs: delivery track record, investment-grade offtakers (Frontier, Microsoft), and an insurer (Arturo) covering residual project risk.
- CFP Energy's floor for a loan conversation is ~$5M; the "sweet spot" is substantially larger. An offtake at or near signature is non-negotiable.
- Frontier brokered the introduction roughly a year before close. Its published offtake templates are the reusable asset for everyone else.
- Working-capital detail worth stealing: Vaulted pushed registry and buyers from quarterly/annual to monthly crediting because pay-on-delivery would otherwise have sunk it.
- Policy ask from the lender: anything that creates price predictability or a liquid forward market. Every CDR debt deal named is outside Europe.

Sebastian Manhart hosts Julia Reichelstein (co-founder/CEO, Vaulted Deep) and Tyler Manchester (head of voluntary carbon, CFP Energy) on [this episode](https://shows.acast.com/the-cdr-policy-scoop/episodes/from-equity-to-debt-how-cdr-gets-bankable-with-julia-reichel) to walk through Vaulted's $35M debt facility from Mediobanca. It's a deal post-mortem: why Vaulted waited to take debt, what the lender actually underwrote, and what other developers need in hand before knocking.

The underwriting logic is the useful part. CFP has done equity in carbon projects for ~20 years and concluded equity-level return requirements can't fund scale-up for developers already delivering. So this is their first CDR debt deal, and the structure is deliberately conventional for institutional lenders: a developer with operating history (Vaulted runs two sites, 30-50 trucks/day, sixth on CDR.fyi deliveries), offtakers with high credit ratings removing market risk, and a third-party insurer so the bank sees a product it recognises. Manchester is frank that there's no single threshold — "it depends" — but the hard gate is an offtake agreement signed or in advanced negotiation. Below ~$5M loan size the transaction costs don't work; the aim is debt "substantially cheaper than equity" that amortises over the offtake term and then exits.

Reichelstein's contribution is the founder-side translation of "bankable." She admits she initially skipped page 176 of the Frontier contract to get a deal done; that's now the page the lender, insurer and bank interrogate — liquidated damages, penalties, delivery cure rights. Her advice is to structure any multi-year offtake for how a financier will read it two to three years later. The monthly-crediting fight with the registry (Isometric) and buyers is the concrete example: pay-on-delivery with quarterly or annual issuance would have created a working-capital hole regardless of contracted volume. Frontier's role as matchmaker and template-publisher gets heavy credit; Manchester notes standardised terms materially shorten diligence on the second look.

Context: Manhart places this in a cluster of recent CDR debt deals across pathways — biochar lenders, Inplanet's enhanced rock weathering (ERW) financing in Brazil, plus Terradot and Mombak — and the obvious observation that none sit in Europe despite EU Emissions Trading System integration talk and national programmes. Manchester's policy answer is lender-shaped: fixed forward price agreements or a liquid forward market would let CFP underwrite without a single corporate offtaker as the credit backbone. Vaulted's position rests on the near-5 Mt Microsoft agreement and the [Frontier](https://frontierclimate.com) offtake; its [CDR.fyi](https://cdr.fyi) delivery rank is the other half of the pitch. Reichelstein also previews the next step — portfolio-style project financing across multiple sites — as "hundreds of millions of debt" eventually.

Not covered: the facility's pricing, tenor, or what Arturo's policy actually insures (delivery shortfall? reversal?). Those would have made the replicability claim testable.

Useful for founders mapping their first debt raise and for buyers wanting to know which contract clauses their offtake needs to make a supplier financeable.
