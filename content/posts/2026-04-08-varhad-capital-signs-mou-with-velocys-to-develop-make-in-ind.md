The recent Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between Varhad Capital and Velocys, aimed at developing "Make-In-India, cost-competitive biomass-to-SAF projects," is a fascinating development, not just for sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) but for the broader carbon dioxide removal (CDR) landscape. Varhad Capital, an Indian financial services firm, partnering with UK-based Velocys, a specialist in Fischer-Tropsch (FT) synthesis technology, points to a strategic move to tap into India's burgeoning aviation sector and its nascent SAF mandates. India's government has indicated a 1% SAF blending target for international flights by 2025, which, while modest, signals a direction of travel.

For us in the CDR space, the immediate takeaway is the inherent potential for net-negative emissions. Velocys's FT technology converts syngas derived from biomass into liquid fuels. The crucial part for CDR is that biomass, by its nature, sequesters atmospheric CO2 during its growth. When this biomass is processed, it releases biogenic CO2. If this process CO2 is captured and permanently stored – a pathway known as Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS) – the entire lifecycle can result in net removal of CO2 from the atmosphere. The article doesn't explicitly mention CCS, focusing on SAF production, but any serious analyst will immediately flag this as a significant opportunity for integrated CDR.

The "Make-In-India" aspect is also critical. It suggests a focus on localizing the value chain, from biomass sourcing and processing to potentially manufacturing components. This could help drive down costs and create a robust domestic ecosystem. However, achieving "cost-competitiveness" for SAF, let alone BECCS, in India will be a formidable challenge. While biomass feedstock might be abundant, establishing sustainable, verifiable supply chains without impacting food security or land use is complex. The cost of FT synthesis itself, coupled with the nascent state of CCS infrastructure in India, means significant capital expenditure and operational costs will need to be overcome.

This partnership highlights a broader trend: the convergence of decarbonization efforts in hard-to-abate sectors like aviation with CDR potential. As SAF mandates strengthen globally, the demand for biomass-derived fuels will increase. Companies like Velocys are at the forefront of providing the technological backbone. Our role is to ensure that as these projects scale, the opportunity for integrated, verifiable CDR is not overlooked but actively pursued. This means advocating for policy mechanisms that value and incentivize the permanent storage of biogenic CO2, alongside the production of sustainable fuels. Without strong policy signals for BECCS specifically, the captured CO2 might be vented or utilized in ways that don't lead to permanent removal. India, with its vast agricultural residue and growing energy demands, could become a significant player in BECCS if the right frameworks are established.
