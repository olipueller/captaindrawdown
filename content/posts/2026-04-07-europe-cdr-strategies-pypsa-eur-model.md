<p>A new study published on arXiv (Fernandes et al., March 2026) extends the sector-coupled European energy system model PyPSA-Eur to include four CDR pathways that most previous models have ignored or underweighted: <strong>afforestation, perennialisation, biochar, and <a href=/posts/what-is-enhanced-weathering/>enhanced rock weathering</a> (ERW)</strong>.</p><p>The results are striking — and structurally important for anyone thinking about CDR deployment priorities in Europe.</p><h2 id=the-core-finding-cdr-makes-the-system-9-cheaper>The Core Finding: CDR Makes the System 9% Cheaper<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-core-finding-cdr-makes-the-system-9-cheaper>#</a></h2><p>The model, run at 3-hourly resolution across 90 European nodes, finds that a climate-neutral energy system equipped with these CDR strategies is <strong>9% less expensive</strong> than an equivalent system that relies only on direct electrification and point-source capture.</p><p>That&rsquo;s a material number. It means CDR isn&rsquo;t just an environmental nice-to-have — it&rsquo;s an economic lever for decarbonization.</p><h2 id=what-gets-selected-afforestation-perennialisation-erw--not-biochar>What Gets Selected: Afforestation, Perennialisation, ERW — Not Biochar<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-gets-selected-afforestation-perennialisation-erw--not-biochar>#</a></h2><p>Here it gets interesting for portfolio thinking:</p><ul><li><strong>Afforestation, perennialisation, and ERW</strong> are <strong>fully utilized across regions</strong> — meaning the model deploys them to their maximum technical potential.</li><li><strong>Biochar is NOT selected.</strong> The reason: limited solid biomass feedstock gets allocated to higher-value processes elsewhere in the energy system. Biochar competes with bioenergy for biomass, and in a optimized European energy system, that competition goes to bioenergy.</li><li><strong>DAC is no longer required</strong> to achieve climate neutrality — but this finding depends on one critical infrastructure assumption (see below).</li></ul><h2 id=the-co2-transport-network-assumption>The CO2 Transport Network Assumption<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#the-co2-transport-network-assumption>#</a></h2><p>The key enabling condition: the model includes a <strong>continental CO2 transport network</strong> and <strong>underground geological sequestration</strong>. With that infrastructure in place, DAC becomes redundant because the other CDR pathways plus sequestration do the job.</p><p>Without a CO2 transport network, the picture changes — but the paper doesn&rsquo;t show the counterfactual explicitly. What it does show clearly is that CDR pathway selection is infrastructure-dependent. Build the pipes, and the CDR mix shifts.</p><h2 id=why-this-matters-for-cdis-erw-focus>Why This Matters for CDI&rsquo;s ERW Focus<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#why-this-matters-for-cdis-erw-focus>#</a></h2><p>CDI&rsquo;s ERW worldview — that enhanced rock weathering is one of the most scalable and underappreciated CDR pathways — gets some rare modeling support here. ERW is fully utilized in the model across European regions. The paper models ERW as accelerated silicate weathering, using crushed rock spread on agricultural land to enhance natural carbon sequestration through mineral dissolution.</p><p>That&rsquo;s consistent with what CDI&rsquo;s Head of Science has been saying about ERW&rsquo;s potential: passive, land-based, scalable without biomass constraints.</p><h2 id=what-the-paper-doesnt-tell-us>What the Paper Doesn&rsquo;t Tell Us<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#what-the-paper-doesnt-tell-us>#</a></h2><p>The model is European and energy-system-focused. It doesn&rsquo;t account for:</p><ul><li>Global supply chains for CDR feedstocks</li><li>Verification and MRV challenges for ERW and afforestation</li><li>Permanence questions for biochar versus geological storage</li><li>Land competition between afforestation and agriculture at scale</li></ul><p>It&rsquo;s one model. But it&rsquo;s one of the more rigorous European-sector-coupled analyses available, and it rewards the intuition that CDR diversity matters more than single-pathway bet.</p><p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href=https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25663>arXiv:2603.25663</a> — Fernandes et al., &ldquo;Exploring carbon dioxide removal strategies to help decarbonise Europe using high-resolution modelling&rdquo; (March 2026)</p><hr><h3 id=-related-reading>🔗 Related Reading<a hidden class=anchor aria-hidden=true href=#-related-reading>#</a></h3><ul><li><a href=/posts/why-carbon-removal-needs-more-than-trees/>Why Carbon Removal Needs More Than Trees</a></li><li><a href=/posts/cdr-census-top-researchers/>The Top Minds in Every CDR Method — And Why I&rsquo;m Showing You</a></li><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Goes All-In on Carbon Removal — 7 New Projects Across 6 Pathways</a></li><li><a href=/posts/mercedes-f1-cdr-portfolio/>Mercedes F1 Builds One of Motorsport&rsquo;s Biggest CDR Portfolios — 18,900 Tonnes Across 6 Pathways</a></li></ul>
