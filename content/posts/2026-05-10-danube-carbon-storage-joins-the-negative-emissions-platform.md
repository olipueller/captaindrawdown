**NEP just published [Danube Carbon Storage joins the Negative Emissions Platform](https://www.negative-emissions.org/updates/danube-carbon-storage-joins-the-negative-emissions-platform).**

The Negative Emissions Platform announced that Danube Carbon Storage, a Central European carbon removals developer, has joined its membership. The company is building a full-chain CCS project in Central Hungary that plans to capture more than 500,000 tonnes per year of biogenic CO2 from yeast fermentation and anaerobic digestion at Pannonia Bio, described as one of Europe's largest biorefineries. The captured CO2 would be transported by pipeline and stored permanently in an onshore saline aquifer within the Pannonian Basin. The project is positioned as a replicable bioCCS model targeting Europe's voluntary carbon markets, with commentary from James Cogan, Head of Carbon Markets at Danube Carbon Storage.

_Our take (Heads-up):_ The capture source is well-suited for bioCCS since fermentation and AD streams are already high-purity CO2, which lowers cost and energy penalties versus dilute flue gas. Open questions worth tracking: storage site characterisation and monitoring plans for the saline aquifer, how biogenic feedstock accounting is handled, and timelines plus permitting status, none of which are detailed in the announcement.
