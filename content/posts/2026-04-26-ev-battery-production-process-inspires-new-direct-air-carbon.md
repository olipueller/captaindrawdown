KAIST researchers borrowed an EV battery manufacturing trick and built a DAC sorbent film that releases captured CO2 in 60 seconds.
---
Prof. Dong-Yeun Koh's team adapted the dry-electrode process used for EV batteries to make a film that is 97% sorbent by weight. Resistive heating (running current through the film) triggers desorption in about 1 minute. It is a 2026 Carbon Removal Challenge top-4 finalist.
---
Two bottlenecks in DAC are slow cycle times and the energy needed to heat sorbents. Electrified, high-loading films attack both. If the lab numbers hold at scale, contactor designs get smaller and cheaper per ton. Big if, but the manufacturing path is already proven in batteries.
---
Caveat: a 97% sorbent film and a 1-minute desorption are bench results. Durability over thousands of cycles, real-world humidity, and full energy balance per ton CO2 removed are still open questions. DAC remains for residual emissions, not a reason to slow fossil phase-out.
