**Heatmap News just published [Transcript: Why Microsoft’s Carbon Removal Pullback Is Such a Big Deal](https://heatmap.news/podcast/shift-key-s3-e43-microsoft-carbon-removal-transcript).**

Heatmap's Robinson Meyer reports that Microsoft has told partners and suppliers it is pausing carbon removal purchases, a story Heatmap broke and that Bloomberg and Carbon Herald have since confirmed. Since 2020, Microsoft has bought over 70 million tons of carbon removal, roughly 40 times more than any other buyer, effectively underwriting the sector. Microsoft's chief sustainability officer Melanie Nakagawa says the program has not ended and that the company is adjusting pace and volume rather than ambition. Meyer talks with Jack Andreasen Cavanaugh of Columbia's Center on Global Energy Policy about the history of CDR, what Microsoft's pullback signals, and what comes next for buyers and suppliers.

_Our take (Heads-up):_ The dependence of an entire climate technology category on one buyer was a known fragility, and this pause exposes it. Microsoft's framing as a pace adjustment is worth tracking against actual procurement volumes over the next few quarters. The open question is whether other hyperscalers, governments, or compliance markets step in, or whether early-stage CDR firms simply run out of runway.
