> Take on a podcast episode from **Reversing Climate Change**, originally published Thu, 30 Ap.
> Listen: <https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/397-Should-Carbon-Dioxide-Removal-Rejoin-the-Mainstream-Carbon-Market-w-Martin-Freimller-of-Octavia-Carbon-e3ilb4t>

Ross Kenyon hosts Martin Freimüller, co-founder and CEO of [Octavia Carbon](https://octaviacarbon.com), a direct air capture company in Kenya, for a frank reassessment of how durable CDR has positioned itself versus the broader voluntary carbon market. The framing: durable carbon removal is the prodigal son, and the trip home is overdue. [Listen here](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/397-Should-Carbon-Dioxide-Removal-Rejoin-the-Mainstream-Carbon-Market-w-Martin-Freimller-of-Octavia-Carbon-e3ilb4t).

The load-bearing claim is a market-sizing one. Freimüller reckons roughly 200 corporates have ever bought durable carbon removal, against ~35,000 corporates buying carbon credits broadly and ~3 million individuals. CDR has spent five years differentiating itself from that larger pool — and from the registries that serve it — while quietly depending on a buyer base small enough to fit in a conference room. The implication isn't that durability stops mattering; it's that the path from $25/ton blended VCM prices to anything resembling DAC economics runs through portfolio buyers, not pure-play CDR funds. "Bad startups blame their customers," he says, and includes himself in the indictment.

The second claim worth flagging is on compliance demand. Freimüller says he was a Corsia and Article 6 skeptic until Q1 2026, when a wave of Corsia-eligible supply came online largely unnoticed by the CDR press. He puts Corsia on a trajectory toward ~60 million tons/year of demand, with ~30 million tons of supply already qualifying, and notes the first Article 6.4 issuances (clean cooking, Myanmar to South Korea) have cleared. Willingness-to-pay in those venues sits in the $20–30/ton range — not durable-CDR territory on its own, but enough that a portfolio approach leaves headroom for a removal slice. Octavia is already selling into buyers like Atmosfair and (per Freimüller) Klimate-style intermediaries with Global South footprints, which he argues is a more natural bridge than Bay Area pure-play demand.

Worth pairing this with the Oxford Principles for Net Zero Aligned Carbon Offsetting, which have argued the portfolio-with-rising-removal-share point for years, and with the ongoing rating work from BeZero and Sylvera showing AA-rated clean cooking credits now exist alongside BB-rated biochar. The durability gap between a 1,000-year DAC ton and a clean cooking ton is real; the quality gap is not as clean as CDR rhetoric implies.

Useful for DAC and biochar founders thinking about buyer diversification beyond Frontier and the usual hyperscalers, and for VCM intermediaries wondering whether durable-CDR suppliers are finally ready to talk.
