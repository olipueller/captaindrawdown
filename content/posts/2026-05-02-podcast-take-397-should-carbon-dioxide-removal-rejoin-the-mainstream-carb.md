> Take on a podcast episode from **Reversing Climate Change**, originally published Thu, 30 Ap.
> Listen: <https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/397-Should-Carbon-Dioxide-Removal-Rejoin-the-Mainstream-Carbon-Market-w-Martin-Freimller-of-Octavia-Carbon-e3ilb4t>

[Reversing Climate Change](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/397-Should-Carbon-Dioxide-Removal-Rejoin-the-Mainstream-Carbon-Market-w-Martin-Freimller-of-Octavia-Carbon-e3ilb4t) host Ross Kenyon sits down with Martin Freimüller, co-founder and CEO of [Octavia Carbon](https://octaviacarbon.com/), the Kenyan direct air capture developer. The episode is a public second-guessing: Freimüller argues that carbon removal's strategic decision to brand itself *against* the legacy voluntary carbon market was a mistake, and that suppliers should re-enter the broader market rather than wait for a dedicated removals buyer base to materialize.

The numbers Freimüller cites are the load-bearing part. He pegs the universe of corporates that have *ever* bought carbon removal at roughly 200, against ~35,000 buying carbon credits more broadly and ~3 million individuals. Meanwhile Article 6.2 and 6.4 flows are quietly moving tens of millions of tons per year of compliance-adjacent demand. His read: the dedicated removals pot is small and not visibly growing in recognized revenue, while the wider market — the one removals advocates spent years rhetorically distancing themselves from — is. Whether or not you accept the framing, those ratios should reset how founders think about commercial strategy. The second move is personal: Freimüller publicly retracts his own past pitch deck optimism about explosive direct air capture growth, and says he's now calling clean cooking founders to learn how they actually find buyers. That's a notable reversal from a CEO whose company depends on premium removal pricing.

The obvious tension here is durability. Re-entering the broader market means selling alongside avoidance and reduction credits whose quality issues — REDD+ baselines, clean cooking over-crediting — are exactly what removals buyers like Frontier and the [Oxford Offsetting Principles](https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/publications/oxford-principles-net-zero-aligned-carbon-offsetting-revised-2024) tried to firewall against. It also rubs against the [pre-compliance thesis](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/reversingclimatechange/episodes/391) the show itself examined in episode 391. Worth pairing this with Robert Höglund's [CDR.fyi](https://www.cdr.fyi/) data on delivered tons, and with the [CORSIA](https://www.icao.int/environmental-protection/CORSIA/Pages/default.aspx) eligibility debates, which are the most concrete near-term test of whether durable removals can plug into compliance demand without being diluted by it.

Useful for CDR founders sanity-checking commercial strategy, and for buyers wondering why their suppliers are suddenly pitching them on Article 6 pathways.
